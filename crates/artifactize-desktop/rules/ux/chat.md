# Desktop app: Chat journeys

Shared app, Window and Settings rules live in [index.md](index.md); shared graph and Detail journeys live in [verify.md](verify.md).

## Chat

Chat is Artifactize's own agent. A session belongs to one branch of one project and works in
that branch's worktree, with the reviewer's own environment, as a terminal there would; it uses
one set of tools whatever the model: reading, listing, finding, searching and editing the
worktree's files, running a command there, and Verify (starting a verify, waiting for it, and
reading its status, results, requests, Runs, the project's declarations and their graph). No
other agent program is installed or set up for it, and it asks no approval per tool.

- **J-CHAT**: Chat's Pane starts with a row of its own: **New chat**, its icon and its name, and at the row's right a Search icon. A click on the Search icon turns that row into a search field, focused; typing lists only the sessions whose title, project or branch matches, as the library finds them, and says so when none does; emptying it and Esc gives the row back with every session listed again. Under that row, with no line between them, **Projects** heads the projects, newest activity first, each a row with a folder icon, open while its sessions show under it, closed while it is folded; a click on a project folds or unfolds it. While the pointer or the keyboard is on a project's row, a New chat icon stands at its right, the row's height its target, named `New chat in {project}`: it opens New chat with its branch choice on that project (J-CHAT-NEW). Under a project its sessions, newest first, each one line: its title, its branch quieter after it, and at the right `running` while a turn runs; once a turn ended while the session was not shown, how it ended (`finished`, `failed`, `stopped`, or `interrupted` when the app or the agent's process ended in the middle of it) until the session is shown; else its age. The New chat icon and the folders stand in one column, and the sessions' titles in one column of their own. Human reviews waiting for a person are not marked here: the bar's Review count says so (J-BAR). A click on a session shows it in the main area: the stage header above it (J-STAGE), with the view tabs, **Conversation** or **Graph** (J-CHAT-GRAPH), the conversation, and the composer at the bottom; the session's row in the Pane names it, and nothing in the stage header repeats its title or branch. Sessions and their conversations are kept: they are listed and shown the same after the app restarts. With no session yet, the main area says how to start one. A session whose branch cannot be worked on now (no longer checked out, its worktree gone) stays listed and shown; its composer says why and sends nothing.
- **J-CHAT-GRAPH**: the stage header's view tabs, **Conversation** and **Graph**, switch a session between its two views. Graph shows the session's branch's Current (J-MAIN-CURRENT) in the conversation's place, the graph filling the main area under the stage header: the graph's tools at the stage header's right (J-STAGE), its scan state in the graph's status line, the graph with its Detail docked at the right and every edit of a Current (J-DEFINITION … J-COMMENT-MOVE). The composer floats over the graph's bottom, centred, the conversation's width, so the person writes to the agent while looking at the graph; with the Detail open it centres in the graph's part left of the Detail. A message sent from Graph shows its reply in a small card just above the composer as it streams, the message sent at its head; a click on the head folds the card to it and unfolds it again. The card holds only the newest message sent from Graph and its reply; the whole conversation is in Conversation, and the card is gone once Conversation is shown. Fitting the graph, and its first view, keep its Artifacts clear of the floating composer and card. What the agent does shows in the graph as the scan picks it up. The Current is the branch's: two sessions on one branch show the same live graph. Each session keeps the view it was left in, through mode switches and after the app restarts. A session whose branch cannot be worked on now shows, in Graph view, one quiet line in the graph's place saying why (its worktree gone, its branch no longer checked out), with nothing live, its conversation still readable in Conversation and its composer off (J-CHAT).
- **J-CHAT-FIND**: in Conversation, the stage header's Find (also Ctrl+F with focus outside the Pane) turns into a find field in its place, focused. Typing marks each place in the conversation where the text appears, ignoring case, shows the first of them and says which of how many is shown (`2 of 3`), or that none matches; Enter shows the next place and Shift+Enter the one before. Esc, or a click outside it with nothing typed, closes the field and takes the marks away, the conversation where it was read.
- **J-CHAT-NEW**: **New chat** opens a dialog with the session's branch, a choice of the live branches (the branch selected in Verify first; opened from a project's row, that project's branch of its newest session, else its first listed checkout); under it the model the session starts with and its effort (that branch's last session's model and effort, else the first model a connected provider lists, Antigravity's once it is signed in, with its default effort), which says it is reading the providers' models until that is known, and, when no provider lists a model, says the session starts without one and that a provider is connected in Settings; Create waits only while the models are being read, then creates the session with that model or, with none listed, with no model yet; Create (also Enter) shows a new, empty session on that branch, titled after its first message once sent, with the composer focused. A session without a model works like any other (its Graph view, editing its Current, the Detail and the Artifact Viewers); only sending waits for a model (J-CHAT-SEND, J-CHAT-MODEL). Cancel closes the dialog (also Esc).
- **J-CHAT-MOVE**: **Move**, the last of a session's tools in the stage header in either view (J-STAGE), moves the session to another checkout: it opens **Move chat**, a dialog with one choice, the live checkouts as New chat lists them, the session's own first, and a line saying the conversation stays. **Move** (also Enter) moves it, closes the dialog and shows the session on its new branch: its row moves under its new project with its new branch, its conversation, model and view kept; from its next turn the agent works in that checkout, and its Graph view shows that branch's Current. Move is disabled on the session's own checkout. While a turn runs, Move in the stage header is unavailable, saying to stop the turn first; a move the library refuses says why in the dialog and moves nothing. Cancel closes the dialog (also Esc). A session whose checkout cannot be worked on now can be moved to one that can, which makes it usable again (J-CHAT).
- **J-CHAT-SEND**: text typed in the composer is sent by **Send** at its bottom right (also Enter; Shift+Enter breaks the line). The message shows at once, exactly as typed, and the agent's turn streams in under it: its text as it is written, read as Markdown (paragraphs, lists, links, which open in the browser, inline code and code blocks, each code block with **Copy**; anything else in it, HTML included, shows as its text), and what it did between its words. Each tool it uses is one quiet line saying what it does and on what (`Read checkout/total.ts`, `Edited checkout/total.ts +3 −1`), in progress while it runs, and with the reason in red when it failed. Tools in a row that went well fold into one summary line of what they did (`Read 4 files · ran 2 commands`), which a click unfolds into their lines and folds again; a running or failed tool stays its own line, and a Run reference is never folded (J-CHAT-RUN). A click on a tool's line unfolds its details in place: what it was asked with, its exit code and the end of its output, as much as the library keeps; a second click folds them. A turn ends when the agent answers. The composer is usable while a turn runs; what is typed meanwhile stays in it and is sent with Send once the turn ends. While the session has no model, the composer holds the draft and Send is disabled, saying `Choose a model to send` beside it, the model selector at hand (J-CHAT-MODEL); the first Send after a model is chosen uses that model. A message the library does not accept is not shown as sent: a line above the composer says why, with **Copy** and **Restore**, which puts the message back in the composer while the composer is empty and is disabled while it holds newer text. A message the library accepted is never sent again unless the person sends it.
- **J-CHAT-READ**: a session's conversation follows its newest line only while it is read at its end: scrolled up, it stays where it is read while the turn streams and its Run references change in place. Each session keeps where it is read while another session or mode is shown and when its conversation is read again, and is shown there again; a session not read before opens at its end. Away from the end, **Scroll to end** stands at the conversation's bottom; a click on it shows the end, which is followed again. A session opened at a Run's reference (J-RUN-ORIGIN) stays there, not following, until it is read at its end again.
- **J-CHAT-RUN**: each verify the agent starts shows in the conversation as a Run reference: its Run ID, its status as it changes (running, then its verdict), what failed or waits for a person, and **Open in Verify**. A click on Open in Verify shows Verify with that Run chosen, its branch expanded and its entry shown; a session with several verifies has one reference for each, each opening its own Run.
- **J-CHAT-STOP**: while a turn runs, Send becomes **Stop** (also Esc in the composer, never Esc in the model menu, J-CHAT-MODEL); a click on it shows **Stopping…** in its place until the turn has stopped, and stops the turn where it is: what the agent did so far stays in the conversation, a running tool is stopped, the turn ends marked as stopped, and the composer sends the next message as usual. A turn the app could not finish because it quit or the agent's process ended ends marked as interrupted, with why, what it did so far kept, and is never resumed or resent by itself. A turn that fails (the provider refuses or cannot be reached) ends with why, in red, and the session stays usable.
- **J-CHAT-MODEL**: the model selector sits at the composer's bottom left, showing the session's model and, for a model that has reasoning efforts, its effort (`GPT-6.2 Sol · high`), or `Choose a model` for a session that has none yet, whose menu marks none. A click opens its menu upward, a filter field at its top focused: typing in it lists only the models whose name or ID, or whose provider's name, contains the text, and says so when none does. The menu holds every provider Settings lists that is set up, each with the models that provider offers, the session's one marked; a provider whose listing failed says why under its name. Providers not set up yet (no key, Antigravity not signed in) are named together under **Connect provider**. Antigravity's models, those of its Google account, are there once it is signed in (J-SET-ANTIGRAVITY). A model that has reasoning efforts, as the library lists them, shows them on its row, the session's one marked on the session's model. A click on an effort sets that model with that effort for the session's next turns; a click on a model's name sets it with the session's effort when the model has it, else with the model's default effort; a model without efforts is set alone; either way the menu closes. **Connect provider** at the menu's end opens Settings on its Providers tab (J-SET-PROVIDERS). The menu closes on a click outside it and on Esc, which closes only the menu and never stops a turn (J-CHAT-STOP). A new session starts with the model and effort its branch's last session used, else the first model listed with its default effort.

## Chat's Graph view: Current

- **J-MAIN-CURRENT**: a branch's Current, shown in a Chat session's Graph view (J-CHAT-GRAPH), scans its worktree as it is now, as `artifactize status` does. It draws the graph of its definitions as they are now at once, every Artifact still being checked; as soon as the scan knows what verify would do with an Artifact, its node is coloured in place, as a Run's Artifacts are, by whether it is reusable (every eval's result would be reused without evaluation), needs evaluation, waits or is blocked. A node shows the icon of what would happen with it, its name and, once it is checked, how many of its evals would be reused of how many (J-GRAPH-COLOURS; read aloud, it says which, with how many of its evals would run, or that it is still being checked). While the scan runs, the graph's status line, at its top left, shows the checking icon and how many Artifacts are still being checked, as `n left` (read aloud as "Checking, n Artifacts left"); once it ends, the time it was scanned at; if it fails, why in words, the graph staying. Refresh is the last of the Current's tools in the stage header (J-STAGE). It scans again when its Graph view is shown again, on Refresh and when a Run of the branch ends, every Artifact being checked again; while a scan of the branch is still running, these show that scan instead of starting over, and after a Refresh one more scan follows once it ends, so what changed meanwhile shows.

## Chat's Graph view: editing

A branch's Current, in a Chat session's Graph view, is where its Artifacts are defined. Each Artifact is one file,
`.artifactize/**/{name}.artf`, and every edit below writes those files as the CLI reads them:
what an Artifact is, and beside it, in sections of the same file that change nothing verify
does, where it is placed (`[annotations.style]`) and the comment people and agents leave on it
(`[annotations.comment]`). An edit is checked against the whole project before anything is written: one
that would leave the project invalid, or that is not allowed, writes nothing and says why.
After an edit the Current is scanned again (J-MAIN-CURRENT). The app has no undo: edits are
files in the worktree, and Git keeps their history. The definitions as they are now draw the
branch's Runs too, places and comments included, but a Run's graph is a record and offers
none of these edits.

- **J-DEFINITION**: on a Current, an Artifact's tab has its Definition where a Run's has Path: the file that defines it, its Path as a field (a target that is missing is said in red beside it), its Parts, each with `✕`, and `+ Add`, then Comment, Rename and Delete; under it Source, folded until unfolded, the file's text. On a Run's graph the tab keeps its Path and has no Definition or Source.
- **J-DEFINITION-EDIT**: a changed Path is written by Enter in its field (also Esc puts it back). Typing in the Source shows Save and Revert; Save (also Ctrl+S in the Source) writes the file, and the graph and the Definition follow; Revert puts back the file's text. An edit the project would not accept writes nothing: the error shows in red under the field or the Source, for the Source with its line and column, and the text stays to be corrected.
- **J-DEFINITION-DRAFT**: a Path being changed or a Parts field being typed in is never written by leaving it: Close or a click on the graph's background closes the Detail as always, and that Artifact's Detail, opened again on the same branch, shows the Path or the part still being typed.
- **J-DEFINITION-CONFLICT**: when a file an edit would write has changed on disk since the Current read it, by a terminal or an agent, the edit writes nothing, whether it is a save, a Path, a part, a rename, a deletion, a drop, a move, a Reset layout or a comment: the refusal names the file, under the field or the Source for an edit in the Detail and as the Main area's notice for one on the graph, and the Current is scanned again, so the graph and the Definition show what is on disk. A Source with typed text keeps it until Revert shows the file's text. Typed text in a Source is never overwritten by the graph either: a move, a drop, a Reset layout or a comment that would write a file whose Source holds typed text is refused the same way, the notice naming the file, until that Source is saved or reverted.
- **J-PARTS-EDIT**: `+ Add` under Parts opens a field that suggests the project's Artifacts (also Esc closes it); a click on a suggestion, or Enter in the field, adds the chosen one to the file's `parts` and the graph draws it inside this whole's box, in free space when the box is placed by hand. `✕` on a part removes it, and the place the box kept for it, and the graph draws it outside. A part that would make a cycle is refused, the reason under Parts, nothing written.
- **J-NODE-NEW**: New Artifact among a Current's tools (J-STAGE) opens a dialog with Name and Path. Create (also Enter) writes `.artifactize/{name}.artf` with that path, closes the dialog, draws the new node, in free space when the top level is placed by hand, and opens its Detail, the camera moved to it as to a found Artifact (J-GRAPH-FIND). A name already used, ignoring case, or a name or path that is not valid says why under its field and writes nothing; Cancel closes the dialog (also Esc).
- **J-NODE-RENAME**: the Definition's Rename opens a dialog with the name. Rename (also Enter) renames the file where it is and, in the same edit, every `parts` entry and `{name}` reference naming it and the place every whole keeps for it in its box; its own place and comment stay in the renamed file. The Detail and the graph show the new name. A name already used or not valid says why under the field; Cancel closes the dialog (also Esc).
- **J-NODE-DELETE**: the Definition's Delete opens a dialog that names the file it removes. Delete removes it, and with it its place, its parts' places and its comment, closing the dialog and the Detail, and the node goes. While another Artifact names it, in its `parts` or a reference, the deletion is refused: the dialog says which Artifacts name it, and nothing is written.
- **J-NODE-MOVE**: on a Current, a node, a minimized whole or a whole's title row can be dragged: it is drawn lifted and follows the pointer with its edges and its comment, a whole carrying its parts. Released in the box it was in (or outside every box when it was in none), it stays there, and the places of every Artifact in that box, as shown, are written once, on release: a move in a whole's box into that whole's file alone; a move at the top level into the moved node's own file, except the first move of a top level laid out automatically, which writes every top-level node's place into its own file in one edit. The places hold after a restart and draw the branch's Runs too. A box, and the top level, is laid out automatically until something in it is moved; a whole's box fits around its parts and their comments; an Artifact that appears later in a box placed by hand goes into free space. A press and release without moving is a click (J-DETAIL). A drag on the background, an edge, a whole's face outside its title row, or an Artifact drawn as a block (J-GRAPH-LARGE) pans. (Also Esc during a drag puts it back, writing nothing.) On a Run's graph nothing moves: a drag pans.
- **J-LAYOUT-RESET**: a box placed by hand has Reset layout in its title row, and the Current's tools have it while the top level is placed by hand. A click lays that box out automatically again, boxes inside it keeping their own: its places leave the whole's file, or, for the top level, every top-level node's place leaves its file.
- **J-PARTS-DRAG**: while a drop would make the dragged Artifact a part of another, that whole's box, or the node under the pointer, is highlighted. Released there, the dragged Artifact is added to that Artifact's `parts`, drawn where it was dropped (under the box's parts where it would cover one), and, in the same edit, leaves the whole whose box it was dragged out of, its place written into the new whole's file and removed from the old one's; released outside every box, it only leaves that whole, its place at the top level written into its own file. For a copy of a shared part only the whole it was dragged out of changes. A drop that would make a cycle highlights nothing: released there, the node returns and the Main area's notice says why, nothing written. Edges are not dragged: a reference is changed in the Source.
- **J-COMMENT**: an Artifact can carry one comment, a note people and agents leave on it, apart from the comment a Run was requested with. On both graphs it is drawn as text alone directly under its node, under a whole's title row inside its box, under a minimized whole, and under every copy of a shared part: in the quieter ink, wrapped at the width of what it is under, its first three lines and then an ellipsis when it is longer; no card, outline, colour, icon or count, and hovering it shows nothing. It moves with its node. Where Artifacts are drawn as blocks (J-GRAPH-LARGE), and for the parts a minimized whole hides, no comment is drawn. Read aloud, it is read after its node. On a Run's graph a click on a comment does what a click on its node does.
- **J-COMMENT-EDIT**: on a Current, the Definition's Comment, or a click on a comment, opens a field in place under the node holding the comment whole, the field focused. Enter writes the text into the node's file and changes nothing else in it, Shift+Enter breaks the line, and Enter on an empty field removes the comment from the file; a click outside the field closes it and puts the comment back, nothing written (also Esc). Closed by Enter, Esc or a click elsewhere that leaves the Detail open, the field returns focus to where it was opened from: the Comment button, or the graph for a click on the comment; a click on the Detail's Close or the graph's background puts the comment back and closes the Detail as well (J-DETAIL), the Comment button going with it. In the Source the comment is part of the file's text.
- **J-COMMENT-MOVE**: on a Current, a comment can be dragged by its text onto another Artifact, a node, a minimized whole or a whole's box: that Artifact is highlighted as the drop target unless it already has a comment. Released on the target, the comment leaves one file and enters the other in one edit and is drawn under its new node. Released on the background, on its own node or on an Artifact that has a comment, it returns and nothing is written (also Esc during the drag puts it back). On a Run's graph comments do not move and no field opens.
