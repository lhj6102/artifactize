# Desktop app: Review journeys

Shared app, Window and Settings rules live in [index.md](index.md).

## Review

Review is where Human reviews are found and evaluated: its Pane holds a filter and the list of
reviews it matches; its main area shows one review, its evaluation space.

- **J-REVIEW-LIST**: Review's Pane is one column: a filter field at the top, its query as typed, and below it the reviews of every repository on this machine that the filter matches, those of removed branches included while their folder exists, grouped by project, newest first, each one line: its eval as `{Artifact}/{eval}`, how long it waits and, once claimed, who claimed it. Its starting filter is `type:human waiting`, the Human reviews waiting for a person. A review whose repository folder is deleted leaves the list on its own, unless another worktree still waits on the same review, which then lists it as its own. With none matching, the list says so. When more reviews match than the library returns at once, **More reviews** ends the list and brings the next ones in below, the open review and the list's scroll kept. A click on a review shows it in the main area.
- **J-REVIEW-FILTER**: a click in the filter field, or the palette's "Filter the reviews", focuses it, showing Review and the Pane first when they are not shown. Terms must all match: `type:human` (also `type:agent`) keeps the requests of that kind, `waiting` those still waiting for a reviewer to claim them, free text matches part of an eval, a project or a branch, `repo:` and `branch:` part of that one field. The query is plain text, edited as typed, and the field suggests terms as the search does (J-PANE-SUGGEST); Esc puts back `type:human waiting`.
- **J-REVIEW-REF**: following a reference to a review (Open in Review in Verify, Open a request by ID, the launch's `review ID`, the pet overlay's ID) shows Review with that exact review open, whether or not the filter lists it, for example once it is claimed or submitted; the filter and the list stay as they were, and nothing more is said about it.
- **J-REVIEW-BACK**: a review's stage header (J-STAGE) holds, at its left, its way back when the review was opened by following Open in Review from Verify: `Verify · {Run} › {Artifact}`, a borderless tool, which shows Verify as it was left, that Detail open; a review opened from its Pane row, the pet overlay, Open a request by ID or the launch has none. At its right, Find in the conversation, which finds text in the review's conversation as Chat's Find does (J-CHAT-FIND), then **Open in Verify**, which shows Verify with the Run that asked for the review chosen and the reviewed Artifact's Detail open on the review's eval. The Pane's selected row names the review's eval.
- **J-HUMAN-DRAFT**: the result being written for a review is that review's draft for this reviewer: its points, the places Point added (J-HUMAN-POINTS), its issues (J-HUMAN-ISSUES) and the verdict chosen. Artifactize keeps it: it stays as written whichever window or mode has focus, through mode switches, the pet overlay and an Artifact Viewer, and after the app restarts; it is kept when the claim is released and gone once the review is submitted, which submits the chosen verdict with its issues alone. Review, the Detail's review, the pet overlay and Point all write the same draft, and a change made in one shows in the others; a point or an issue that arrives while a field is being typed in changes nothing typed. Text typed in an issue field and not yet added is that field's own until Enter adds it.

## Human review

- **J-HUMAN-CLAIM**: the Claim to review button, at the left of the review's decision bar until the review is claimed, claims a waiting Human review for this reviewer (also `c`); **Release claim**, in its place once the review is claimed, releases it once nothing of the review is running, also while issues are being written, the draft kept (also `u` outside a field). Beside the button the decision bar says where the claim stands: `Waiting` or `Yours`. While a claim or a release runs, its seconds and Cancel stand beside its button, and a failure says why there. A claim held by another reviewer shows as taken, the claimant named beside its stage; one this reviewer made elsewhere (another window, the CLI) is theirs and shows as `Yours`.
- **J-HUMAN-LAYOUT**: a Human review is a conversation, the same in Review's main area and on a Human eval's tab in the Detail, read as Chat's is (J-CHAT-SEND, J-CHAT-READ): the review's request asks at the top and the reviewer answers under it. At its top a head line: the eval's title (in the Detail, the Detail's title says it, and the head line holds Open in Review). Then the request, drawn as Chat's agent text is, without a box: its instruction, which reads first; the Tools, usable where they stand (J-HUMAN-TOOL); and **Debug**, one quiet folded line, which a click unfolds into the request's ID, its mode, its claimant and how long it has waited, and folds again (also `t`). Under it, newest last as they happen, each tool's run as one folded line (J-HUMAN-LOG) and each point Point adds (J-HUMAN-POINTS). Under the request, the reviewer's issues, each a bubble as Chat's messages are, oldest first (J-HUMAN-ISSUES). At the bottom, where Chat's composer stands, two parts apart from each other: the **issues box**, drawn as Chat's composer field, holding only the issue field and **Add issue** (J-HUMAN-ISSUES); and under it the **decision bar**, a bar of its own, with the claim at its left, where it stands and Claim to review or Release claim (J-HUMAN-CLAIM), and the decision at its right, the verdict choice, GREEN or RED, and Submit (J-HUMAN-GREEN, J-HUMAN-RED). Before a claim the issues box is not shown: one quiet line says that a claim opens the issues and the verdict, and the decision bar holds Claim to review, its GREEN, RED and Submit unavailable. A review taken by another reviewer has no issues box, its decision bar saying `Taken by {claimant}`; a request opened by ID that is not a waiting Human review (J-OPEN-ID) has none either, its decision bar saying why it is read-only, its request's sections shown read-only above. The conversation scrolls on its own and follows its newest line while it is read at its end. In Review it is a readable measure wide, centred under the stage header; in the Detail it takes the Detail's width. Nothing in it draws the eye away from the instruction; a field shows its focus only while it is typed in. What ended well is said by a run's end or by the completed result alone: once submitted, the issues box, the decision bar and the runs go, and the result as submitted stands as the reviewer's last bubble, its verdict and its issues, read-only.
- **J-HUMAN-TOOL**: the Tools stand in the review's request under its instruction, grouped by the Artifact whose declaration composes them, the reviewed one first, a group named by its Artifact only when more than one has tools, each a `▶` button named by its tool name (`files.open`), its description shown on hover; before a claim they are disabled, saying `Claim to run the tools`; after a claim a click on one runs it at once, its run added to the conversation (J-HUMAN-LOG). While any work of the review runs, a tool's run, a claim, a release or a submission, every tool button is disabled, so one tool runs at a time. A launch tool opens its application and returns at once; a viewer tool opens its Artifact Viewer (J-VIEWER) and returns at once; a failing tool says its error on its run's line.
- **J-HUMAN-LOG**: each run of a review's tool is one line of the conversation, added under the request when it starts, as a Chat tool's line is: its tool name and Artifact, then, while it runs, its newest line, its seconds and Cancel, which cancels it; once ended, its last stderr line if it printed one and its end with how many lines it printed: `exit N` (red unless 0), `done`, `launched` once a file is handed to its desktop application or an Artifact Viewer opens, or `error: …` in red when the tool could not run. A click on the line unfolds its lines under it, and another folds them: `▶ HH:MM:SS · tool · Artifact`, then each line the tool prints, stderr marked by a red rule, its `warning:` or `error:` prefix in red, then its end line (`■ exit N`, `■ done`, `■ launched` or `■ error: …`). A launched application's lines keep arriving under its own run until it exits. The lines are monospace, wrap, scroll on their own past half the conversation's height and follow the newest unless the reader scrolled up; their text can be selected and copied into an issue. A submission clears the runs from the conversation.
- **J-HUMAN-POINTS**: the places Point added to the review (J-POINT) are its **Points**, each one quiet line of the conversation, added under the request as it is pointed, newest last: its text, where it was pointed (the Artifact and its viewer) and when, **Insert**, which puts its text into the issue field at its caret, and a remove button that takes the point out of the draft. The Points show once there is one. They are the reviewer's own aid: a submission keeps only the verdict, and the points go with the draft.
- **J-HUMAN-ISSUES**: a reviewer writes the review's issues one at a time in the issues box, each one thing the reviewed Artifact must change: **Add issue** at the field's right (also Enter) adds what is typed as the newest issue and empties the field (Shift+Enter starts a new line in it); Add issue is unavailable while the field is empty. Each issue shows as a bubble at the conversation's right, as a Chat message does, exactly as typed, with Edit and Remove beside it: Edit turns that bubble into a field in its place holding its text, where Enter keeps the change and Esc leaves it as it was; Remove takes the issue out. The issues decide what can be submitted: RED with them, GREEN only with none (J-HUMAN-GREEN, J-HUMAN-RED).
- **J-HUMAN-GREEN**: after a claim the decision bar's GREEN chooses GREEN (also `g`) and Submit, then `Submit GREEN`, submits it (also Ctrl+S) with no issues: the Artifact approved as it stands. Nothing is chosen until the reviewer chooses it, so GREEN is never submitted by a stray Submit, and GREEN cannot be submitted while the review lists an issue. It can be submitted also while a tool of the review runs: the submission cancels that tool first. While it runs, its seconds and Cancel stand in Submit's place. The completed result stays shown.
- **J-HUMAN-RED**: the decision bar's RED chooses RED (also `r`) and Submit, then `Submit RED`, submits it (also Ctrl+S) with its issues, so RED needs at least one issue. GREEN and RED switch the verdict at any time, the issues kept. Submit is unavailable, saying why just left of it, while no verdict is chosen, while text typed in the issue field is not yet added, and while the chosen verdict cannot be submitted as it stands: RED without an issue, or GREEN with one; a result the library still refuses is not submitted: what failed is shown and the draft stays correctable.
- **J-HUMAN-TEXT**: text fields take any language, including Korean composed by an input method; no key typed during a composition, and no key without Ctrl (⌘) typed in a field, does anything but enter text.
- **J-HUMAN-ESC**: Cancel beside the running work cancels it. In the Detail, Close or a click on the graph's background first cancels the review's running work, then closes the Detail, the draft kept (J-DETAIL). (Also Esc, which first leaves an issue being edited or the issue field, then takes the same steps and, for a review shown in Review, returns to Review's Pane as its last step, showing it first when it is hidden.)

## Artifact Viewer, Point and the pet overlay

An Artifact's tools come from the toolsets its declaration composes with `&`
(`artf landing : files & html`): `files` (File Explorer), `markdown` (Markdown viewer) and
`artifactize` (Artifactize Viewer) are built in, and a project can add others as plugins, such
as the `html` example. A toolset's viewer tool (`html.view`) opens its viewer in an Artifact
Viewer window of the app.

- **J-VIEWER**: a viewer tool, run from a review's Tools or an Artifact's Tools in the Detail (`▶`), opens an Artifact Viewer: a window of the app of its own, titled `{Artifact} — {viewer}`, showing that Artifact in its plugin's viewer, never the system's default application. Each viewer tool of an Artifact has one window: running it again while its window is open brings that window to the front, and another viewer tool of the same Artifact (`files.view` beside `html.view`) opens a window of its own. Closing a viewer's window closes it alone. An Artifact's `launch` and `output` tools work as before (J-HUMAN-TOOL).
- **J-VIEWER-FILES**: the File Explorer shows the Artifact's files as a tree at its left, folders folding and unfolding by a click, and a click on a file shows its text in the document view at its right, with line numbers, read-only.
- **J-VIEWER-MARKDOWN**: the Markdown viewer shows the Artifact's Markdown rendered, headings, lists, links, tables and code as they read; an Artifact of several Markdown files lists them at its left, a click showing one.
- **J-VIEWER-ARTIFACTIZE**: the Artifactize Viewer shows what an Artifact composed with `artifactize` covers: the Artifacts bound in the `.artf` files under its path as nodes and the references between them as plain connections, with no verdicts (it shows what the project declares, not a Run). A click on an Artifact opens its panel at the right: its name and the toolsets it composes, its evals, each with its kind, its title, its criteria and the Artifacts it reads. The graph pans and zooms as Verify's does.
- **J-VIEWER-PLUGIN**: a project's own viewer plugin (the `html` example included) shows its own page inside the Artifact Viewer; for `html`, the Artifact's page in an embedded browser that can be used as in a browser. A plugin's page reaches the Artifact's files and the active review only through the viewer's bridge, nothing else of the app. A viewer page asks the bridge for its context when it starts: until the page first reaches the bridge the strip says *Loading {plugin}…*, and a page that finished loading without reaching it is said, in the strip, not to have connected, until it does. Once connected, the strip shows the latest status line the page reports (for `html`, the page shown and its title once loaded, or why it failed), each replacing the last; the strip's Point tells the page to point (J-POINT).
- **J-POINT**: while this reviewer holds a claimed review, its active review (the one last shown in Review), every viewer that supports it shows **Point**. A click on Point, then a click on a place in the Artifact, adds that place as readable text to the review's Points (J-HUMAN-POINTS), with the Artifact and viewer it was pointed in: an HTML element as a CSS selector (`#checkout .total`), a Markdown position as its line and column (`L42:C9`), an Artifact or an eval in the Artifactize Viewer as its ID (`front-design`, `front-design/matches-layout`). Point in the pet overlay (J-PET) points in every open viewer of the review's Artifact at once: the first click in any of them adds its place and stops pointing in all of them. Pointing never submits and never writes an issue. Esc, or Point again, stops pointing without adding anything. With no claimed review, Point is not shown.
- **J-PET**: while this reviewer holds a claimed review and works anywhere but its evaluation space (another mode, an Artifact Viewer, another application), a small pet overlay stays at the bottom right of the screen, above every window, tied to the active review: the pet, the review's eval and Run (a click on them shows the review in Review, J-REVIEW-REF), and the same draft, split as Review's is, compactly: its points, each with Insert, which puts its text into the overlay's issue field (J-HUMAN-POINTS), and its issues, each with Remove; then the issue field with **Add** (also Enter, J-HUMAN-ISSUES) and **Point** beside them (pointing in every Artifact Viewer of the review's Artifact that is open, J-POINT, and disabled, saying why, while none is); then, apart, a row with the GREEN and RED choice and **Submit**, unavailable and saying why as in Review (J-HUMAN-GREEN, J-HUMAN-RED). Typing in it writes the draft, kept as focus moves between the overlay, a viewer and other windows. Submit submits the review as Review's does (J-HUMAN-GREEN, J-HUMAN-RED). The overlay goes when the review is submitted or released, and while the review's evaluation space is in front.
