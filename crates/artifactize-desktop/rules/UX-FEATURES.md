# Desktop app: user journeys

What a person can do in the desktop app, from the app's real behaviour. Each journey has an id
(`J-...`) and ends in something visible. It is the artifactize app of this
machine, not of one repository: it shows every live branch of every project with Runs
on this machine and every Human review waiting for a person. It shows and changes the same state
as the `artifactize` CLI and MCP server; a change made by any of them shows in the others.

The window has three modes over that one state, each a workspace of its own above one bottom
bar: **Chat**, where a person works with Artifactize's own agent on a branch and, in a session's
Graph view, sees the branch's Current and defines its Artifacts; **Verify**, where branches and
their Runs are watched; and **Review**,
where Human reviews are found and evaluated. References connect them: a reference names one exact
thing (a Chat session, a Run, an Artifact of a Run, a review), and following it shows that thing
in its mode; every reference has a way back.

Hovering only highlights what is under the pointer: no popup, summary, tooltip or menu opens on
hover, and information opens only on a click; the one exception is the native tooltip of a
control: its name, above all for one drawn as an icon alone (a whole's Minimize or Expand button,
J-GRAPH-MINIMIZE, the bar's Settings, J-BAR, a stage tool, Search chats, a project's New chat or
Move, J-CHAT-MOVE), why it is unavailable while it is, or a Human tool's description
(J-HUMAN-TOOL). A pane is never wider than its place: its width
is set by the layout or by a drag, never by what it shows, and what it shows wraps or scrolls in
its own box. The Detail's tabs show as rounded chips, the chosen one's words brighter over a thin mark; a
choice within a tab, such as its Summary or its log, shows as plain text, the chosen one in the
strong text colour over the same thin mark and the other in the quieter one; a state is never
told by boldness. The marks the journeys write as characters (a tool's `▶`, a star's `☆` and `★`,
`✕`, `+ Add`, a verdict's glyph) are drawn as the app's own icons, one per meaning, each
read aloud as what it means; a Tool log's lines are text and keep their characters.

A screen shows what the task at hand needs and says it once. What runs or went well is shown
as an icon, a count or a time, not as a sentence; the app speaks in sentences only for what
went wrong, why a control is disabled, a place that would otherwise be empty, a decision
that cannot be undone and a setting whose name cannot say what it is (J-SET-SIGNOFF-GRACE).
What an agent or a person wrote (a Chat message, a tool's output, an instruction) is content and
is shown as written.

Every journey names the visible way to do what it describes: a button, a menu, the command
palette or a click. Keyboard shortcuts are optional: a key written in parentheses with "also"
is one the app may offer for the same thing, and the app may offer others or none. Where it
offers a key, the command palette and Help name it; a key without Ctrl (⌘) pressed in a field
(a true/false field and a choice included) does only the field's own work, and no key acts
during an input method's composition (J-HUMAN-TEXT).
Enter in a field that has no button of its own, a Path, a part or a comment, is how that field
is written, not a shortcut.

## Window

- **J-LAUNCH**: the app opens on one window titled "Artifactize": the shown mode's workspace above the bottom bar (J-BAR). Started without arguments it shows Verify. Started with `--repo PATH` it shows Verify with that branch selected; with `review ID` it shows Review with that review open (J-REVIEW-REF), and with `review` alone Review with the newest waiting Human review open; with `graph --repo PATH` (also the declared Human `graph` tool) it shows that branch's Current in Chat as Open in Chat does (J-OPEN-CHAT): the branch's newest session whose checkout can be worked on, in its Graph view (J-CHAT-GRAPH); if none can, the newest saved session, which shows why it can't be worked on and keeps its conversation readable; if the branch has no session, New chat on that branch (J-CHAT-NEW), whose Create works with no provider set up.
- **J-BAR**: one bar runs along the window's bottom in every mode and never moves: the brand alone at its left; **Chat**, **Verify** and **Review** in its middle, the shown mode marked as on, Review carrying the number of Human reviews waiting for a person whenever there is at least one (the same number whatever Review's filter shows; above 99 it reads `99+`, its accessible name keeping the exact number); **Settings**, its gear icon alone, named by its native tooltip, at its right. A click on a mode shows it (also Ctrl+1, Ctrl+2, Ctrl+3). A click on the brand opens the command palette (J-PALETTE).
- **J-MODES**: showing another mode changes only the workspace above the bar: each mode keeps its own place while another is shown, so going back to it shows it as it was left, its selection, scroll (in Chat, each session's view and its place in its conversation, J-CHAT-READ, J-CHAT-GRAPH), open panes and Detail, typed text, a Chat turn still streaming, a reason being typed and a Pane's search or filter included. A mode is shown by the bar, by the palette, or by following a reference into it (J-CHAT-RUN, J-RUN-ORIGIN, J-DETAIL-REVIEW, J-REVIEW-BACK).
- **J-PANE-ROWS**: in every mode's Pane a row that is chosen by a click (Chat's New chat, a project and a session, a Verify branch and its Run entries, a review) is chosen by a click anywhere on it: the whole row, across the Pane's width and of a comfortable height, the same in every Pane.
- **J-SETTINGS**: the bar's Settings button opens Settings as a modal over the window, on its Providers tab, its header naming this machine's reviewer, the name the app claims reviews as; its Close, the Settings button again or a click outside it closes it, the window beneath as it was (also Ctrl+, and Esc). Settings has four tabs, Providers, Chat Agents, Remote and Limits, and a click on one shows it (also ← and → on the focused tab).
- **J-PALETTE**: a click on the bar's brand opens the command palette (also Ctrl+K). It lists every command that can run now, each with the key that does the same where the app has one: the app's (Keyboard shortcuts, Show Settings, Show Chat, Show Verify, Show Review, Show or hide the Pane, New chat, Search the branches, Filter the reviews, Open a request by ID, Refresh, Quit), then those of what the shown mode shows, a graph's or a Human review's, and while Settings is open those of Settings. Typing filters them to the commands whose names contain the text, a group's heading staying only while one of its commands does. A click on a command runs it (also Enter on the selected one), and a click outside the palette closes it, nothing run (also Esc). It opens again after any use.
- **J-HELP**: the palette's Keyboard shortcuts (also `?`) opens Help: the keys the app has for what has focus (a Pane, a Chat, a graph with its Detail, a Human review, Settings) and, for a graph, what each of its marks means: each verdict's icon beside its word, on a Current what verify would do. Its Close or a click outside it closes it.
- **J-REFRESH**: the palette's Refresh (also F5 or Ctrl+R) reloads the state from disk, scans the branches again and, on a Current, scans its worktree again.
- **J-QUIT**: the palette's Quit, or closing the window, quits (also `q` outside a field), closing the app's Artifact Viewer windows and the pet overlay with it. With Human review claims held by this window it asks first, naming what is held: Stay keeps the window and its claims, Release and quit releases the claims and quits (also Esc and `u`); a claim's draft stays either way (J-HUMAN-DRAFT). With a Chat turn running it says so too, and quitting stops that turn as Stop would (J-CHAT-STOP).
- **J-OPEN-ID**: the palette's "Open a request by ID" (also Ctrl+O) opens a dialog with one field for the ID; its Open shows Review with that saved request open, as a reference opens it (J-REVIEW-REF): a waiting Human review actionable, any other request read-only. Cancel closes the dialog.
- **J-UPDATE**: when a newer stable release of artifactize is out, Settings shows `{version} available` in its header (J-SETTINGS). A click on it opens a dialog with the running and the new version and how to update the way this app was installed: the command to run, as text that can be selected and copied, or the file to download and install by hand; when the way is unknown, the release page. A link opens in the browser; Close closes the dialog (also Esc), the notice staying in Settings. A Microsoft Store install never shows it: the Store updates the app. The app looks up the latest release on GitHub when it starts and once a day while it runs, the same lookup `artifactize doctor` reuses, and never downloads or installs anything itself.
- **J-PANE-TOGGLE**: each mode has a Pane at its left: Chat's sessions, Verify's branches, Review's reviews. The palette's "Show or hide the Pane" (also Ctrl+B) hides the Pane and its divider in every mode, the workspace taking the window's whole width with its view as it was, and shows them again, each Pane at the width it had, its search or filter, selection and expanded rows as they were. Hidden while focus is in a Pane, focus moves to the mode's main area. Whatever needs a Pane shows it again first, such as the palette's "Search the branches" (J-PANE-SEARCH). The Panes are shown when the app starts. A graph, its Detail, a Chat and a Human review are read and used whole with the Pane hidden. While the Pane is hidden, the window's title names what Chat or Verify shows, which the Pane's selected row named: `{session title} · {branch} — Artifactize` for a Chat session, `{Run ID} · {project} / {branch} — Artifactize` for a Run; with the Pane shown, and in Review, it is `Artifactize`.
- **J-RESIZE**: the divider between a mode's Pane and its main area can be dragged to resize them; the split stays as dragged in every mode whatever the main area shows, and comes back as dragged when a hidden Pane is shown again. Dragging the Detail's left edge sets its width, between a readable column and leaving some graph visible, never over the Pane; the Detail keeps that width when opened again and in the next launch. Hovering a divider or the edge shows only the resize cursor.
- **J-LIVE**: a change made by another process (a new Run, a result, a claim, a submission, a draft written elsewhere, a worktree added or removed, a Chat session's turn) appears in the open app without a reload.

## Chat

Chat works in a branch's worktree through either an **ACP Chat agent** (a coding-agent CLI unified on ACP that runs in the worktree with its own native coding tools after explicit workspace trust, plus Artifactize's one-turn Verify bridge) or a **direct API provider** (Artifactize's own worktree-scoped tools: reading, listing, finding, searching and editing files, running a command, and Verify). Both share one conversation model, Run references, and Graph view; evals never use ACP agents and remain strictly Artifactize-driven.

- **J-CHAT**: Chat's Pane starts with a row of its own: **New chat**, its icon and its name, and at the row's right a Search icon. A click on the Search icon turns that row into a search field, focused; typing lists only the sessions whose title, project or branch matches, as the library finds them, and says so when none does; emptying it and Esc gives the row back with every session listed again. Under that row, with no line between them, **Projects** heads the projects, newest activity first, each a row with a folder icon, open while its sessions show under it, closed while it is folded; a click on a project folds or unfolds it. While the pointer or the keyboard is on a project's row, a New chat icon stands at its right, the row's height its target, named `New chat in {project}`: it opens New chat with its branch choice on that project (J-CHAT-NEW). Under a project its sessions, newest first, each one line: its title, its branch quieter after it, and at the right `waiting` while an ACP turn waits on a permission card, or `running` while a turn runs; once a turn ended while the session was not shown, how it ended (`finished`, `failed`, `stopped`, or `interrupted` when the app or the agent's process ended in the middle of it) until the session is shown; else its age. The New chat icon and the folders stand in one column, and the sessions' titles in one column of their own. Human reviews waiting for a person are not marked here: the bar's Review count says so (J-BAR). A click on a session shows it in the main area: the stage header above it (J-STAGE), with the view tabs, **Conversation** or **Graph** (J-CHAT-GRAPH), the conversation, and the composer at the bottom; the session's row in the Pane names it, and nothing in the stage header repeats its title or branch. Sessions and their conversations are kept: they are listed and shown the same after the app restarts. With no session yet, the main area says how to start one. A session whose branch cannot be worked on now (no longer checked out, its worktree gone) stays listed and shown; its composer says why and sends nothing.
- **J-CHAT-GRAPH**: the stage header's view tabs, **Conversation** and **Graph**, switch a session between its two views. Graph shows the session's branch's Current (J-MAIN-CURRENT) in the conversation's place, the graph filling the main area under the stage header: the graph's tools at the stage header's right (J-STAGE), its scan state in the graph's status line, the graph with its Detail docked at the right and every edit of a Current (J-DEFINITION … J-COMMENT-MOVE). The composer floats over the graph's bottom, centred, the conversation's width, so the person writes to the agent while looking at the graph; with the Detail open it centres in the graph's part left of the Detail. A message sent from Graph shows its reply in a small card just above the composer as it streams, the message sent at its head; a click on the head folds the card to it and unfolds it again. The card holds only the newest message sent from Graph and its reply; the whole conversation is in Conversation, and the card is gone once Conversation is shown. Fitting the graph, and its first view, keep its Artifacts clear of the floating composer and card. What the agent does shows in the graph as the scan picks it up. The Current is the branch's: two sessions on one branch show the same live graph. Each session keeps the view it was left in, through mode switches and after the app restarts. A session whose branch cannot be worked on now shows, in Graph view, one quiet line in the graph's place saying why (its worktree gone, its branch no longer checked out), with nothing live, its conversation still readable in Conversation and its composer off (J-CHAT).
- **J-CHAT-FIND**: in Conversation, the stage header's Find (also Ctrl+F with focus outside the Pane) turns into a find field in its place, focused. Typing marks each place in the conversation where the text appears, ignoring case, shows the first of them and says which of how many is shown (`2 of 3`), or that none matches; Enter shows the next place and Shift+Enter the one before. Esc, or a click outside it with nothing typed, closes the field and takes the marks away, the conversation where it was read.
- **J-CHAT-NEW**: **New chat** opens a dialog with the session's branch, a choice of the live branches (the branch selected in Verify first; opened from a project's row, that project's branch of its newest session, else its first listed checkout); under it the starting target (that branch's last session's target — an ACP agent or a direct provider model and effort — else the first available direct model or connected ACP agent), which says it is reading the providers' models until that is known, and, when none is available, says the session starts without one and that a provider or Chat agent is configured in Settings; Create waits only while the targets are being read, then creates the session with that target or, with none listed, with no target yet; Create (also Enter) shows a new, empty session on that branch, titled after its first message once sent, with the composer focused. A session without a target works like any other (its Graph view, editing its Current, the Detail and the Artifact Viewers); only sending waits for a target (J-CHAT-SEND, J-CHAT-MODEL). Cancel closes the dialog (also Esc).
- **J-CHAT-MOVE**: **Move**, the last of a session's tools in the stage header in either view (J-STAGE), moves the session to another checkout: it opens **Move chat**, a dialog with one choice, the live checkouts as New chat lists them, the session's own first, and a line saying the conversation stays. **Move** (also Enter) moves it, closes the dialog and shows the session on its new branch: its row moves under its new project with its new branch, its conversation, target and view kept; from its next turn the agent works in that checkout, and its Graph view shows that branch's Current. Move is disabled on the session's own checkout. While a turn runs, Move in the stage header is unavailable, saying to stop the turn first; a move the library refuses says why in the dialog and moves nothing. Cancel closes the dialog (also Esc). A session whose checkout cannot be worked on now can be moved to one that can, which makes it usable again (J-CHAT).
- **J-CHAT-SEND**: text typed in the composer is sent by **Send** at its bottom right (also Enter; Shift+Enter breaks the line). Before spawning an ACP agent in an untrusted repository or folder (or after its executable digest changed), Chat opens **Trust workspace**, naming the agent, its trust scope (a Git repository covering all its branches and worktrees, or a folder), and the resolved executable path and SHA-256 fingerprint, with **Cancel** and **Trust and continue**; trusting grants consent and sends the message. The message shows at once, exactly as typed, and the agent's turn streams in under it: its text as it is written, read as Markdown (paragraphs, lists, links, which open in the browser, inline code and code blocks, each code block with **Copy**; anything else in it, HTML included, shows as its text), and what it did between its words. Thought summaries show as a folded **Thought** line that unfolds on click. Each tool it uses is one quiet line saying what it does and on what (`Read checkout/total.ts`, `Edited checkout/total.ts +3 −1`), in progress while it runs, and with the reason in red when it failed or quietly marked when cancelled. Tools in a row that went well fold into one summary line of what they did (`Read 4 files · ran 2 commands`), which a click unfolds into their lines and folds again; a running, failed or cancelled tool stays its own line, and a Run reference is never folded (J-CHAT-RUN). A click on a tool's line unfolds its details in place: what it was asked with, locations, inline diff when reported, exit code and the end of its output, as much as the library keeps; a second click folds them. An agent plan shows as a compact step list with each step's status. When an ACP agent requests permission, an interactive **Permission** card appears inline with the tool title, kind, summary, optional detail and the agent's options (explicitly noting when an always option may be saved in the agent's own settings); choosing an option sends the response and settles the card read-only. Notices and cross-engine handoffs appear as quiet lines; switching engine or agent on an existing conversation shows a handoff note above the composer, and a retired legacy `claude-agent` session shows a migration banner above the composer while staying readable. A turn ends when the agent answers. The composer is usable while a turn runs; what is typed meanwhile stays in it and is sent with Send once the turn ends. While the session has no target, the composer holds the draft and Send is disabled, saying `Choose a model to send` beside it, the target selector at hand (J-CHAT-MODEL); the first Send after a target is chosen uses that target. A message the library does not accept is not shown as sent: a line above the composer says why, with **Copy** and **Restore**, which puts the message back in the composer while the composer is empty and is disabled while it holds newer text. A message the library accepted is never sent again unless the person sends it.
- **J-CHAT-READ**: a session's conversation follows its newest line only while it is read at its end: scrolled up, it stays where it is read while the turn streams and its Run references change in place. Each session keeps where it is read while another session or mode is shown and when its conversation is read again, and is shown there again; a session not read before opens at its end. Away from the end, **Scroll to end** stands at the conversation's bottom; a click on it shows the end, which is followed again. A session opened at a Run's reference (J-RUN-ORIGIN) stays there, not following, until it is read at its end again.
- **J-CHAT-RUN**: each verify the agent starts shows in the conversation as a Run reference: its Run ID, its status as it changes (running, then its verdict), what failed or waits for a person, and **Open in Verify**. A click on Open in Verify shows Verify with that Run chosen, its branch expanded and its entry shown; a session with several verifies has one reference for each, each opening its own Run.
- **J-CHAT-STOP**: while a turn runs (including while waiting on a permission card), Send becomes **Stop** (also Esc in the composer, never Esc in the model menu, J-CHAT-MODEL); a click on it shows **Stopping…** in its place until the turn has stopped, cancels any open permission requests, and stops the turn where it is: what the agent did so far stays in the conversation, a running tool is stopped, the turn ends marked as stopped, and the composer sends the next message as usual. A turn the app could not finish because it quit or the agent's process ended ends marked as interrupted, with why, what it did so far kept, and is never resumed or resent by itself. A turn that fails (the provider refuses or cannot be reached) ends with why, in red, and the session stays usable.
- **J-CHAT-MODEL**: the target selector sits at the composer's bottom left, showing the session's active target — for a direct provider, its model and effort (`GPT-6.2 Sol · high`), or for an ACP agent, its agent name and active config summary (`Antigravity · Gemini 4 Pro · high`) — or `Choose a model` for a session that has none yet, whose menu marks none. A click opens its menu upward, a filter field at its top focused: typing in it lists only the targets whose name, ID, agent or provider contains the text, and says so when none does. The menu lists ready **Chat Agents** (ACP presets, installed Registry agents and custom ACP agents) with their live `configOptions` (model, mode — clearly noting agent-native approval modes such as bypass/yolo — and thought level) and every direct **Provider** Settings lists that is set up, each with the models that provider offers, the session's selection marked; a provider or agent whose listing failed says why under its name. Providers not set up yet are named together under **Connect provider**. Antigravity's models, those of its Google account, are there under the Antigravity ACP agent once it is signed in (J-SET-ANTIGRAVITY). A direct model that has reasoning efforts, as the library lists them, shows them on its row, the session's one marked on the session's model. Choosing an ACP agent or its config options, or a direct model and effort, sets it for the session's next turns and closes the menu. **Manage chat agents** and **Connect provider** at the menu's end open Settings on Chat Agents or Providers (J-SET-CHAT-AGENTS, J-SET-PROVIDERS). The menu closes on a click outside it and on Esc, which closes only the menu and never stops a turn (J-CHAT-STOP). A new session starts with the target its branch's last session used, else the first available target listed.

## Verify

Verify is the machine's monitor: its Pane holds a search and the live branches with their
Runs; its main area shows a Run's graph, with the Detail of the chosen Artifact. A branch's
Current is not here: it is Chat's, in a session's Graph view (J-CHAT-GRAPH).

- **J-PANE**: Verify's Pane is one column: a search field at the top and the branches below it. The divider between it and the main area is shown whenever the Pane is (J-PANE-TOGGLE). There is no Scope heading, no Runs list and no list of waiting reviews: those are Review's (J-REVIEW-LIST), and the bar counts them (J-BAR).
- **J-BRANCHES**: each branch reads as its project and branch, `artifactize / feat/x`, the branch in bold and the project quieter: a Git worktree that exists now with the branch it has checked out (a detached one by its folder's name), or a folder outside Git that exists now and holds its definitions (`.artifactize/`). Its projects are those with Runs on this machine and the one the app was started for with `--repo` or `graph --repo`; a worktree or folder that was removed, or whose worktree Git no longer lists, is not shown, and worktrees added or removed appear or go within seconds, without a reload. Who ran a Run, an agent session or a person, makes no entry of its own: a branch's Runs are the Runs of that project on that branch, from whichever folder they ran in.
- **J-BRANCH-RUNS**: a branch is folded or expanded, any number expanded at once, with a chevron before its name. Folded, its newest Run sits under its name, a dot in its status colour, its status word and its age on the right (a running one its time so far); a branch without Runs shows nothing there. Expanded, its timeline sits there instead, newest first: one entry per Run, its status dot, status word and age and, below, one line: the comment the Run was requested with (`verify --comment`), cut short to one line, or else its Run ID, the dots joined by a line; while a Run is in progress on the branch the timeline starts with it. **Older runs** at its end brings older Runs in below the oldest. A click on a branch expands it and shows its newest Run; a branch without Runs shows, in the main area, one line saying so and **Open in Chat** (J-OPEN-CHAT); a click on its chevron only expands or folds it; a click on an entry shows that one in the main area, the shown entry a darker row across the Pane, its words brighter: it is what names the shown Run, which the stage header does not repeat (J-STAGE). The branch the app was started for starts expanded. (Also keys: ↑ ↓ or `j`/`k` through the branches and the timelines, → and ← to expand or fold, Enter into the main area, and Esc from there back to the Pane.)
- **J-BRANCH-STAR**: a star at the end of a branch's name line, faint until it is on, pins it: starred branches are listed before the others, each group in the search's order, and stay starred for every later monitor of this machine's user until the star is taken away.
- **J-PANE-SEARCH**: a click in the search field at Verify's Pane's top, or the palette's "Search the branches", focuses it, showing Verify and the Pane first when they are not shown (also `/`, and Ctrl+F, in a field too, unless a graph or a Chat conversation is shown and focus is outside the Pane and outside fields, J-GRAPH-FIND, J-CHAT-FIND); its starting query is `sort:recent`. Free text and `key:value` terms must all match: free text matches part of a project or a branch, `repo:` and `branch:` part of that one field; `sort:recent` puts the branch with the most recent Run first and branches without Runs last, `sort:name` orders them by project and branch.
- **J-PANE-SUGGEST**: while a Pane's search or filter field is focused, typing a term lists suggestions under it that complete that term: the field's keys and, after a key, its values (the projects, the branches, the sort orders, the review kinds). A click on one puts it in place of the term; a key taken lists its values next. The suggestions close when the field loses focus or is emptied (also ↓ opens them, ↑ ↓ choose one, Tab or Enter takes the chosen one or the first, and Esc closes them, then puts back the field's starting query, then leaves the field).

## Verify: main area

- **J-STAGE**: Chat, Verify and Review show one stage header above their main area, the same row in each, of controls only: at its left the way back when there is one (J-RUN-ORIGIN), then, left-anchored so tools never move them, the view tabs where a mode has two views (J-CHAT-GRAPH), and at its right the tools of the view shown: on a graph Find, Zoom out, the zoom in percent, Zoom in and Fit View (on a Current then New Artifact, Reset layout while it applies, and Refresh; on a Run then Run details and Open in Chat), in Conversation Find (J-CHAT-FIND); in Chat, in either view, Move last (J-CHAT-MOVE); in Review, Find and Open in Verify (J-REVIEW-BACK). The way back is a tool like the others: its icon and words, without a border. Its tools are plain words and icons without borders, brighter under the pointer and while what they open is open. It names nothing and rates nothing: what is shown, and how it stands, are the selected row in the Pane, and the graph's status line for a graph's state and for what went wrong. With nothing shown it stands empty.
- **J-MAIN-RUN**: a Run chosen in the Pane fills Verify's main area with the graph of its Artifacts and their verdicts, under the stage header with the graph's tools (J-STAGE); the Run's entry in the Pane, the selected row, is what names it: its status and age, and its comment or ID. Run details, among the tools, opens the Run's Detail (J-DETAIL): its outcome with what failed and what waits for a person, its tally, how long it took, its comment in full and what started it. With nothing chosen, while loading, on an error or with an empty graph the graph's place stays and says so. What went wrong, such as Runs that cannot be read, shows as a few words in the graph's status line at its top left, without moving the graph; a click on it shows it in full, and another click folds it back.
- **J-OPEN-CHAT**: where Verify leads to a branch's Current, its **Open in Chat** (the last of a Run's tools in the stage header, and for a branch without Runs in the main area) opens the branch's newest session whose checkout can be worked on, in Graph view (J-CHAT-GRAPH). If none can, it opens the newest saved session, which shows why it can't be worked on and keeps its conversation readable. If the branch has no session, New chat on that branch (J-CHAT-NEW).
- **J-RUN-ORIGIN**: a Run started by a Chat session says so at the stage header's left (J-STAGE): `Chat · {session title}`. A click on it shows Chat with that session, scrolled to the Run's reference (J-CHAT-RUN) and not following its newest line until it is read at its end (J-CHAT-READ). A Run started from the CLI or an MCP client has no such link; the Run's Detail names what started it.
- **J-MAIN-CURRENT**: a branch's Current, shown in a Chat session's Graph view (J-CHAT-GRAPH), scans its worktree as it is now, as `artifactize status` does. It draws the graph of its definitions as they are now at once, every Artifact still being checked; as soon as the scan knows what verify would do with an Artifact, its node is coloured in place, as a Run's Artifacts are, by whether it is reusable (every eval's result would be reused without evaluation), needs evaluation, waits or is blocked. A node shows the icon of what would happen with it, its name and, once it is checked, how many of its evals would be reused of how many (J-GRAPH-COLOURS; read aloud, it says which, with how many of its evals would run, or that it is still being checked). While the scan runs, the graph's status line, at its top left, shows the checking icon and how many Artifacts are still being checked, as `n left` (read aloud as "Checking, n Artifacts left"); once it ends, the time it was scanned at; if it fails, why in words, the graph staying. Refresh is the last of the Current's tools in the stage header (J-STAGE). It scans again when its Graph view is shown again, on Refresh and when a Run of the branch ends, every Artifact being checked again; while a scan of the branch is still running, these show that scan instead of starting over, and after a Refresh one more scan follows once it ends, so what changed meanwhile shows.

## Graphs: Detail

Both graphs, a Run's in Verify and the Current in a Chat session's Graph view, take the pointer (also the keyboard: Tab reaches their Artifacts and Enter on one does what a click does). What acts on each graph is among the stage header's tools (J-STAGE): Find, Zoom out, the zoom in percent and Zoom in, and Fit View; on a Current also New Artifact (J-NODE-NEW), while its top level is placed by hand Reset layout (J-LAYOUT-RESET), and Refresh (J-MAIN-CURRENT). What its marks mean, and the keys it has, are in Help (J-HELP). On a graph rest only its status line at the top left, Find's list while it is open, the Navigator (J-GRAPH-NAVIGATOR) and, in Chat's Graph view, the floating composer and its reply card (J-CHAT-GRAPH).

- **J-DETAIL**: a click on an Artifact opens its Detail, a panel docked at the graph's right edge over the graph, from the graph's top to the main area's bottom without a gap, below the stage header, whose tools stay readable and usable, never beside the Pane, the graph staying visible on its left. It opens on the Artifact's own tab, which has no chips: its list of evals leads to each eval's tab (J-DETAIL-NEIGHBOURS). On an eval's tab the Detail's tabs read as a path, `[Artifact icon] {name} / [eval] [eval] …`: the Artifact's own chip, which leads back to the Artifact's tab, the Artifact icon before its name, read aloud as "Artifact {name}"; only the name is cut short when long (the Detail's title shows it whole), then a `/`, then one chip per eval showing its verdict's glyph and colour, on a Current whether it would run, would be reused or is still being checked. Eval chips that do not fit on one line wrap onto the next, within the Detail; the `/` stays with the Artifact's chip. Close is the one control of the Detail's header besides an Artifact's star (J-DETAIL-STAR). A click on another Artifact shows that Artifact's Detail; a click on the graph's background or Close closes it (also Esc), after first cancelling a Human review's running work (J-HUMAN-ESC). Run details among a Run's tools (J-STAGE) opens the Run's Detail, and closes it again: its outcome (its status, the evals that failed, its tally, how long it took and its comment in full), what started it (the CLI, an MCP client or a Chat session) and what it waits for. Everything in the Detail, transcript and log lines, long words and code included, stays within its width.
- **J-DETAIL-STAR**: a ☆ beside the title of an Artifact's Detail stars the Artifact, a favorite: it turns ★, and a click on it again takes the star away. A starred Artifact stays starred on every branch of its project, in Runs and Currents alike, for every later monitor of this machine's user until the star is taken away.
- **J-DETAIL-NEIGHBOURS**: the Artifact's own tab is shown first. It lists the Artifact's evals with their verdicts (a click on one shows its tab). Under Neighbours, folded until it is unfolded (J-DETAIL-SECTIONS), it draws the Artifact between its neighbours with the graph's nodes and edges, the Artifacts its evals use on its left and those whose evals use it on its right; the drawing does not pan, zoom or drag, and a click on a neighbour opens that neighbour's Detail. Then come its Human tools (J-DETAIL-TOOLS) and, on a Run, its Path, the target and, for a Live Artifact, its address; on a Current, its Definition and Source (J-DEFINITION); every other fact, what it waits for included, folds under Debug. Each of these is a section under its own header row (J-DETAIL-SECTIONS).
- **J-DETAIL-REVIEW**: on a Run's graph, an Artifact's tab lists each Human eval waiting for a person with its stage beside its verdict: claimable, yours, or taken by its claimant; under it, **Claim to review** while it can be claimed, **Release claim** while it is this reviewer's, and **Open in Review**, which shows Review with that review open (J-REVIEW-REF). A claim or a release made here is the same claim as in Review (J-HUMAN-CLAIM). The Human eval's tab holds the review itself and its own Open in Review.
- **J-DETAIL-SECTIONS**: below its tabs the Detail shows sections, each under a header row that is its name alone, with no count or value beside it; a section with nothing to show is not shown: an Artifact's Evals, Neighbours, Tools, Tool log (once a tool has run) and Path, or on a Current Definition and Source, an eval's Result, Execution options, Execution metadata and Fingerprint, a Run's Outcome, Waits for, What and Provenance, and Debug, or a Run's Technical, last. A click on a header row folds its section to that row alone, and again unfolds it (also Enter or Space on it focused). A section stays folded or unfolded as a person left it, by its name, in every Detail opened later and in the next launch. Until then what a tab is opened for is unfolded (Evals, Tools, Path, Definition, Result, Outcome, Waits for) and what is read now and then starts folded (Neighbours, Source, Execution options, Execution metadata, Fingerprint, What, Provenance, Debug and Technical). A Human review on an eval's tab is its conversation, as in Review (J-HUMAN-LAYOUT), under the Detail's tabs without section header rows, each tool run folding by its own line (J-HUMAN-LOG).
- **J-DETAIL-TOOLS**: an Artifact's chip lists the Human tools the Artifact declares or its type provides, with the look and behaviour of a Human review's Tools and runs (J-HUMAN-TOOL, J-HUMAN-LOG): a click on a tool's `▶` runs it at once in the branch's worktree as it is now, whichever Run is shown, with no claim, and records nothing as a review's evidence. While one of its tools runs, the Artifact's tool buttons are disabled and a line under them shows it running with Cancel, which stops it; a tool that cannot run says why there in red. Its runs show in the Artifact's Tool log while the app runs, each run one line as a review's runs are (J-HUMAN-LOG); the Tool log shows from the first run on, and a run unfolds a Tool log that was folded. The graph's nodes carry no tool buttons.
- **J-DETAIL-SUMMARY**: an eval's tab shows its Summary: its status, beside it in grey what running it took and spent (time, tokens in `k` or `M`, model) or what reuse saved; its instruction; its result, each owner field by name and a list as numbered items; then Execution options, Execution metadata and Fingerprint when present, folded until unfolded (J-DETAIL-SECTIONS); every other fact, what it waits for included, folds under Debug. What the status says is not repeated below it. On a Current it shows whether the eval would run, would be reused or is still being checked and why, and the result it would reuse. An eval with a log switches below the tabs between its Summary, shown first, and its log, a plain text choice a level below the tabs: an Agent eval's Agent log (its transcript, with its own scrollbar) or a runtime eval's Runtime log (its saved output). A Human eval's tab in a Run holds its review and sign-off.
- **J-GRAPH-COLOURS**: a node says its verdict (GREEN, running or waiting for a Human, waiting, RED, ERROR, blocked), on a Current what would happen (reused, would run, waiting, blocked) or that it is still being checked, with that verdict's icon before its name and a strip along its bottom, both in the verdict's colour; its face and its outline stay neutral. After its name a node with evals says how many of them are GREEN of how many it has (`2/3`), on a Current how many would be reused; one still being checked says none. Edges are neutral, except an edge that leaves a RED, ERROR or blocked Artifact, drawn in that Artifact's colour, so what holds a blocked Artifact shows. Icons, strips, counts and edges change in place when a result arrives or a scan learns what would happen. A verdict has one colour wherever the app shows it: a node's colour is the colour the same status has in a Pane, in a chip and in a row.
- **J-GRAPH-PARTS**: on both graphs an Artifact made of parts, those its `parts` names, is drawn as a box that encloses its parts' nodes, its name, its verdict's icon and a strip in its verdict's colour at the box's top; a part with parts of its own is a box inside it, as deep as the parts go. The box is the whole and the nodes in it are its parts, and each is an Artifact of its own: a click on the box outside its parts opens the whole's Detail and a click on a part opens the part's. Edges to and from a whole meet its box; edges between Artifacts in different boxes are drawn and coloured like any other. The layout is made for parts that belong to one whole, each whole's parts laid out together inside it. A part of several wholes is drawn inside each of them, each copy with the part's edges; selecting one copy highlights every copy of it, and its Detail says that it is drawn in each of those wholes, naming them. The find field lists such a part once and goes to its copy in the first of its wholes.
- **J-GRAPH-MINIMIZE**: a whole's box has a Minimize button at its top right; a click on it draws the whole as a node of a node's size: its name, its verdict's icon and colour, how many parts it has and an Expand button, whose click draws its box again. The palette's "Minimize or expand the whole" does the same for the whole whose Detail is open (also `m`, for the whole focused on the graph or whose button is focused, else the whole whose Detail is open, while focus is outside the Pane, focus staying where it was). The edges to and from the Artifacts a minimized whole hides meet it instead, those between the same two drawn once in the light that comes first, as an Artifact's light comes from its evals'; edges among them are not drawn. A minimized whole is otherwise a node: a click on it outside its button opens its Detail, and it is highlighted while an Artifact it hides is open. The find field lists what it hides as before and goes to the minimized whole for it. A whole stays minimized on every graph of its branch, its Runs' and its Current, until it is expanded or the app quits; a part of several wholes stays drawn inside those not minimized.
- **J-GRAPH-LARGE**: a graph too large to draw at once stays navigable: panning or zooming the camera draws the Artifacts it brings into view. Zoomed out so far that names would be too small to read, each Artifact is drawn as a block of its verdict's colour and a whole as its box's outline in its colour, and a click on one still opens its Detail; zoomed in until names read, each is a node again (J-GRAPH-COLOURS).
- **J-GRAPH-NAVIGATOR**: whenever part of a graph lies outside the view, whatever the graph's size, a Navigator shows at the graph's bottom right, left of the Detail when one is open and, in Chat's Graph view, above the floating composer and its card: the whole graph in small, each Artifact a block of its verdict's colour, with the part in view framed. A click or a drag in it moves the camera so that place is in the middle of the view, the zoom kept. It goes when the whole graph is in view again. Hovering it opens nothing.
- **J-GRAPH-FIND**: the graph's tools have Find (also Ctrl+F with focus outside the Pane and outside fields, on the graph or in the Detail). With a Run's graph or a Current shown, a click on it turns it into a find field in its place in the stage header, focused, its list under it over the graph. With nothing typed it lists the graph's starred Artifacts (J-DETAIL-STAR) as if they had been found, in the graph's order, each with its verdict's glyph and colour; when none of the graph's Artifacts is starred, it says how to star one. Typing lists the Artifacts and evals whose names contain the text, ignoring case, in the graph's order: an Artifact by its whole name, an eval as `{Artifact} / {eval}`, each with its verdict's glyph and colour (on a Current, what would happen); the first 50, then how many more match, or that none does. No row shows as chosen when the list appears (also ↑ and ↓ choose a row, and Enter opens the chosen one, or the first while none is chosen). A click on a row opens it, an Artifact's Detail on its Artifact tab and an eval's on that eval's tab, closes the field and moves the camera so the Artifact sits in the middle of the graph left of the Detail, zooming in only as far as its name needs to be readable. A click outside the field closes it and does nothing else, the graph and the Detail as they were (also Esc), and Find is back in its place.
- **J-GRAPH-CAMERA**: a graph shows whole when it first appears, never enlarged beyond its nodes' own size: a small graph sits at that size in the middle of the graph's place. The graph's place, for this first view and for Fit View, leaves out the status line at the graph's top and, in Chat's Graph view, the floating composer and its card at its bottom, so no Artifact is shown under them. From then on only the person moves its camera, by panning, zooming (the wheel, or the stage header's Zoom out and Zoom in, the zoom in percent between them), Fit View (whole again, with the same limit), the Navigator (J-GRAPH-NAVIGATOR), opening what the find field found (J-GRAPH-FIND), creating an Artifact (J-NODE-NEW) minimizing or expanding a whole (J-GRAPH-MINIMIZE), which keeps that whole's top left corner where it was on the screen, or the keyboard moving focus to an Artifact out of view, which pans just enough to bring it in: opening, switching, resizing or closing the Detail, a result or scan arriving, a new layout and an edit (J-NODE-MOVE, J-PARTS-DRAG) never zoom or pan it.

## Chat's Graph view: editing

A branch's Current, in a Chat session's Graph view, is where its Artifacts are defined. Each Artifact is one file,
`.artifactize/**/{name}.artf`, and every edit below writes those files as the CLI reads them:
what an Artifact is, and beside it, in sections of the same file that change nothing verify
does, where it is placed (`[annotations.style]`) and the comment people and agents leave on it
(`[annotations.comment]`). An edit is checked against the whole project before anything is written: one
that would leave the project invalid, or that is not allowed, writes nothing and says why.
After an edit the Current is scanned again (J-MAIN-CURRENT). The app has no undo: edits are
files in the worktree, and Git keeps their history. The definitions as they are now draw the
branch's Runs too, places and comments included, but a Run's graph is a record and offers
none of these edits.

- **J-DEFINITION**: on a Current, an Artifact's tab has its Definition where a Run's has Path: the file that defines it, its Path as a field (a target that is missing is said in red beside it), its Parts, each with `✕`, and `+ Add`, then Comment, Rename and Delete; under it Source, folded until unfolded, the file's text. On a Run's graph the tab keeps its Path and has no Definition or Source.
- **J-DEFINITION-EDIT**: a changed Path is written by Enter in its field (also Esc puts it back). Typing in the Source shows Save and Revert; Save (also Ctrl+S in the Source) writes the file, and the graph and the Definition follow; Revert puts back the file's text. An edit the project would not accept writes nothing: the error shows in red under the field or the Source, for the Source with its line and column, and the text stays to be corrected.
- **J-DEFINITION-DRAFT**: a Path being changed or a Parts field being typed in is never written by leaving it: Close or a click on the graph's background closes the Detail as always, and that Artifact's Detail, opened again on the same branch, shows the Path or the part still being typed.
- **J-DEFINITION-CONFLICT**: when a file an edit would write has changed on disk since the Current read it, by a terminal or an agent, the edit writes nothing, whether it is a save, a Path, a part, a rename, a deletion, a drop, a move, a Reset layout or a comment: the refusal names the file, under the field or the Source for an edit in the Detail and as the Main area's notice for one on the graph, and the Current is scanned again, so the graph and the Definition show what is on disk. A Source with typed text keeps it until Revert shows the file's text. Typed text in a Source is never overwritten by the graph either: a move, a drop, a Reset layout or a comment that would write a file whose Source holds typed text is refused the same way, the notice naming the file, until that Source is saved or reverted.
- **J-PARTS-EDIT**: `+ Add` under Parts opens a field that suggests the project's Artifacts (also Esc closes it); a click on a suggestion, or Enter in the field, adds the chosen one to the file's `parts` and the graph draws it inside this whole's box, in free space when the box is placed by hand. `✕` on a part removes it, and the place the box kept for it, and the graph draws it outside. A part that would make a cycle is refused, the reason under Parts, nothing written.
- **J-NODE-NEW**: New Artifact among a Current's tools (J-STAGE) opens a dialog with Name and Path. Create (also Enter) writes `.artifactize/{name}.artf` with that path, closes the dialog, draws the new node, in free space when the top level is placed by hand, and opens its Detail, the camera moved to it as to a found Artifact (J-GRAPH-FIND). A name already used, ignoring case, or a name or path that is not valid says why under its field and writes nothing; Cancel closes the dialog (also Esc).
- **J-NODE-RENAME**: the Definition's Rename opens a dialog with the name. Rename (also Enter) renames the file where it is and, in the same edit, every `parts` entry and `{name}` reference naming it and the place every whole keeps for it in its box; its own place and comment stay in the renamed file. The Detail and the graph show the new name. A name already used or not valid says why under the field; Cancel closes the dialog (also Esc).
- **J-NODE-DELETE**: the Definition's Delete opens a dialog that names the file it removes. Delete removes it, and with it its place, its parts' places and its comment, closing the dialog and the Detail, and the node goes. While another Artifact names it, in its `parts` or a reference, the deletion is refused: the dialog says which Artifacts name it, and nothing is written.
- **J-NODE-MOVE**: on a Current, a node, a minimized whole or a whole's title row can be dragged: it is drawn lifted and follows the pointer with its edges and its comment, a whole carrying its parts. Released in the box it was in (or outside every box when it was in none), it stays there, and the places of every Artifact in that box, as shown, are written once, on release: a move in a whole's box into that whole's file alone; a move at the top level into the moved node's own file, except the first move of a top level laid out automatically, which writes every top-level node's place into its own file in one edit. The places hold after a restart and draw the branch's Runs too. A box, and the top level, is laid out automatically until something in it is moved; a whole's box fits around its parts and their comments; an Artifact that appears later in a box placed by hand goes into free space. A press and release without moving is a click (J-DETAIL). A drag on the background, an edge, a whole's face outside its title row, or an Artifact drawn as a block (J-GRAPH-LARGE) pans. (Also Esc during a drag puts it back, writing nothing.) On a Run's graph nothing moves: a drag pans.
- **J-LAYOUT-RESET**: a box placed by hand has Reset layout in its title row, and the Current's tools have it while the top level is placed by hand. A click lays that box out automatically again, boxes inside it keeping their own: its places leave the whole's file, or, for the top level, every top-level node's place leaves its file.
- **J-PARTS-DRAG**: while a drop would make the dragged Artifact a part of another, that whole's box, or the node under the pointer, is highlighted. Released there, the dragged Artifact is added to that Artifact's `parts`, drawn where it was dropped (under the box's parts where it would cover one), and, in the same edit, leaves the whole whose box it was dragged out of, its place written into the new whole's file and removed from the old one's; released outside every box, it only leaves that whole, its place at the top level written into its own file. For a copy of a shared part only the whole it was dragged out of changes. A drop that would make a cycle highlights nothing: released there, the node returns and the Main area's notice says why, nothing written. Edges are not dragged: a reference is changed in the Source.
- **J-COMMENT**: an Artifact can carry one comment, a note people and agents leave on it, apart from the comment a Run was requested with. On both graphs it is drawn as text alone directly under its node, under a whole's title row inside its box, under a minimized whole, and under every copy of a shared part: in the quieter ink, wrapped at the width of what it is under, its first three lines and then an ellipsis when it is longer; no card, outline, colour, icon or count, and hovering it shows nothing. It moves with its node. Where Artifacts are drawn as blocks (J-GRAPH-LARGE), and for the parts a minimized whole hides, no comment is drawn. Read aloud, it is read after its node. On a Run's graph a click on a comment does what a click on its node does.
- **J-COMMENT-EDIT**: on a Current, the Definition's Comment, or a click on a comment, opens a field in place under the node holding the comment whole, the field focused. Enter writes the text into the node's file and changes nothing else in it, Shift+Enter breaks the line, and Enter on an empty field removes the comment from the file; a click outside the field closes it and puts the comment back, nothing written (also Esc). Closed by Enter, Esc or a click elsewhere that leaves the Detail open, the field returns focus to where it was opened from: the Comment button, or the graph for a click on the comment; a click on the Detail's Close or the graph's background puts the comment back and closes the Detail as well (J-DETAIL), the Comment button going with it. In the Source the comment is part of the file's text.
- **J-COMMENT-MOVE**: on a Current, a comment can be dragged by its text onto another Artifact, a node, a minimized whole or a whole's box: that Artifact is highlighted as the drop target unless it already has a comment. Released on the target, the comment leaves one file and enters the other in one edit and is drawn under its new node. Released on the background, on its own node or on an Artifact that has a comment, it returns and nothing is written (also Esc during the drag puts it back). On a Run's graph comments do not move and no field opens.

## Review

Review is where Human reviews are found and evaluated: its Pane holds a filter and the list of
reviews it matches; its main area shows one review, its evaluation space.

- **J-REVIEW-LIST**: Review's Pane is one column: a filter field at the top, its query as typed, and below it the reviews of every repository on this machine that the filter matches, those of removed branches included while their folder exists, grouped by project, newest first, each one line: its eval as `{Artifact}/{eval}`, how long it waits and, once claimed, who claimed it. Its starting filter is `type:human waiting`, the Human reviews waiting for a person. A review whose repository folder is deleted leaves the list on its own, unless another worktree still waits on the same review, which then lists it as its own. With none matching, the list says so. When more reviews match than the library returns at once, **More reviews** ends the list and brings the next ones in below, the open review and the list's scroll kept. A click on a review shows it in the main area.
- **J-REVIEW-FILTER**: a click in the filter field, or the palette's "Filter the reviews", focuses it, showing Review and the Pane first when they are not shown. Terms must all match: `type:human` (also `type:agent`) keeps the requests of that kind, `waiting` those still waiting for a reviewer to claim them, free text matches part of an eval, a project or a branch, `repo:` and `branch:` part of that one field. The query is plain text, edited as typed, and the field suggests terms as the search does (J-PANE-SUGGEST); Esc puts back `type:human waiting`.
- **J-REVIEW-REF**: following a reference to a review (Open in Review in Verify, Open a request by ID, the launch's `review ID`, the pet overlay's ID) shows Review with that exact review open, whether or not the filter lists it, for example once it is claimed or submitted; the filter and the list stay as they were, and nothing more is said about it.
- **J-REVIEW-BACK**: a review's stage header (J-STAGE) holds, at its left, its way back when the review was opened by following Open in Review from Verify: `Verify · {Run} › {Artifact}`, a borderless tool, which shows Verify as it was left, that Detail open; a review opened from its Pane row, the pet overlay, Open a request by ID or the launch has none. At its right, Find in the conversation, which finds text in the review's conversation as Chat's Find does (J-CHAT-FIND), then **Open in Verify**, which shows Verify with the Run that asked for the review chosen and the reviewed Artifact's Detail open on the review's eval. The Pane's selected row names the review's eval.
- **J-HUMAN-DRAFT**: the result being written for a review is that review's draft for this reviewer: its points, the places Point added (J-HUMAN-POINTS), its reasons (J-HUMAN-REASONS), the verdict chosen, and what is typed in the chosen verdict's own fields (J-HUMAN-FORM). Artifactize keeps it: it stays as written whichever window or mode has focus, through mode switches, the pet overlay and an Artifact Viewer, and after the app restarts; it is kept when the claim is released and gone once the review is submitted, which submits the chosen verdict made of the reasons and those fields alone. Review, the Detail's review, the pet overlay and Point all write the same draft, and a change made in one shows in the others; a point or a reason that arrives while a field is being typed in changes nothing typed. Text typed in a reasons field and not yet added is that field's own until Enter adds it.

## Human review

- **J-HUMAN-CLAIM**: the Claim to review button, at the left of the review's decision bar until the review is claimed, claims a waiting Human review for this reviewer (also `c`); **Release claim**, in its place once the review is claimed, releases it once nothing of the review is running, also while reasons are being written, the draft kept (also `u` outside a field). Beside the button the decision bar says where the claim stands: `Waiting` or `Yours`. While a claim or a release runs, its seconds and Cancel stand beside its button, and a failure says why there. A claim held by another reviewer shows as taken, the claimant named beside its stage; one this reviewer made elsewhere (another window, the CLI) is theirs and shows as `Yours`.
- **J-HUMAN-LAYOUT**: a Human review is a conversation, the same in Review's main area and on a Human eval's tab in the Detail, read as Chat's is (J-CHAT-SEND, J-CHAT-READ): the review's request asks at the top and the reviewer answers under it. At its top a head line: the eval's title (in the Detail, the Detail's title says it, and the head line holds Open in Review). Then the request, drawn as Chat's agent text is, without a box: its instruction, which reads first; the Tools, usable where they stand (J-HUMAN-TOOL); while a verdict is chosen whose eval asks more of it than reasons, that verdict's own fields (J-HUMAN-FORM); and **Debug**, one quiet folded line, which a click unfolds into the request's ID, its mode, its claimant and how long it has waited, and folds again (also `t`). Under it, newest last as they happen, each tool's run as one folded line (J-HUMAN-LOG) and each point Point adds (J-HUMAN-POINTS). Under the request, the reviewer's reasons, each a bubble as Chat's messages are, oldest first (J-HUMAN-REASONS). At the bottom, where Chat's composer stands, two parts apart from each other: the **reasons box**, drawn as Chat's composer field, holding only the reasons field and **Add reason** (J-HUMAN-REASONS); and under it the **decision bar**, a bar of its own, with the claim at its left, where it stands and Claim to review or Release claim (J-HUMAN-CLAIM), and the decision at its right, the verdict choice, GREEN or RED, and Submit (J-HUMAN-GREEN, J-HUMAN-RED). Before a claim the reasons box is not shown: one quiet line says that a claim opens the reasons and the verdict, and the decision bar holds Claim to review, its GREEN, RED and Submit unavailable. A review taken by another reviewer has no reasons box, its decision bar saying `Taken by {claimant}`; a request opened by ID that is not a waiting Human review (J-OPEN-ID) has none either, its decision bar saying why it is read-only, its request's sections shown read-only above. The conversation scrolls on its own and follows its newest line while it is read at its end. In Review it is a readable measure wide, centred under the stage header; in the Detail it takes the Detail's width. Nothing in it draws the eye away from the instruction; a field shows its focus only while it is typed in. What ended well is said by a run's end or by the completed result alone: once submitted, the reasons box, the decision bar and the runs go, and the result as submitted stands as the reviewer's last bubble, each field by name, read-only.
- **J-HUMAN-TOOL**: the Tools stand in the review's request under its instruction, grouped by the Artifact that declares them or whose type provides them, the reviewed one first, a group named by its Artifact only when more than one has tools, each a `▶` button named by its operation, its description shown on hover; before a claim they are disabled, saying `Claim to run the tools`; after a claim a click on one runs it at once, its run added to the conversation (J-HUMAN-LOG). While any work of the review runs, a tool's run, a claim, a release or a submission, every tool button is disabled, so one tool runs at a time. A launch tool opens its application and returns at once; a viewer tool opens its Artifact Viewer (J-VIEWER) and returns at once; a failing tool says its error on its run's line.
- **J-HUMAN-LOG**: each run of a review's tool is one line of the conversation, added under the request when it starts, as a Chat tool's line is: its operation and Artifact, then, while it runs, its newest line, its seconds and Cancel, which cancels it; once ended, its last stderr line if it printed one and its end with how many lines it printed: `exit N` (red unless 0), `done`, `launched` once a file is handed to its desktop application or an Artifact Viewer opens, or `error: …` in red when the tool could not run. A click on the line unfolds its lines under it, and another folds them: `▶ HH:MM:SS · operation · Artifact`, then each line the tool prints, stderr marked by a red rule, its `warning:` or `error:` prefix in red, then its end line (`■ exit N`, `■ done`, `■ launched` or `■ error: …`). A launched application's lines keep arriving under its own run until it exits. The lines are monospace, wrap, scroll on their own past half the conversation's height and follow the newest unless the reader scrolled up; their text can be selected and copied into a reason. A submission clears the runs from the conversation.
- **J-HUMAN-POINTS**: the places Point added to the review (J-POINT) are its **Points**, each one quiet line of the conversation, added under the request as it is pointed, newest last: its text, where it was pointed (the Artifact and its viewer) and when, **Insert**, which puts its text into the reasons field at its caret, and a remove button that takes the point out of the draft. The Points show once there is one. They are the reviewer's own aid: a submission keeps only the verdict, and the points go with the draft.
- **J-HUMAN-REASONS**: a reviewer writes the review's reasons one at a time in the reasons box: **Add reason** at the field's right (also Enter) adds what is typed as the newest reason and empties the field (Shift+Enter starts a new line in it); Add reason is unavailable while the field is empty. Each reason shows as a bubble at the conversation's right, as a Chat message does, exactly as typed, with Edit and Remove beside it: Edit turns that bubble into a field in its place holding its text, where Enter keeps the change and Esc leaves it as it was; Remove takes the reason out. A reason carries no verdict: whichever verdict is chosen, its result is made of the reasons when it is submitted (J-HUMAN-GREEN, J-HUMAN-RED).
- **J-HUMAN-GREEN**: after a claim the decision bar's GREEN chooses GREEN (also `g`) and Submit, then `Submit GREEN`, submits it (also Ctrl+S) with the reasons as its comment, or with none, also while a tool of the review runs: the submission cancels that tool first. While it runs, its seconds and Cancel stand in Submit's place. The completed result stays shown.
- **J-HUMAN-RED**: the decision bar's RED chooses RED (also `r`) and Submit, then `Submit RED`, submits it (also Ctrl+S) with the reasons as its change list, so RED needs at least one reason. GREEN and RED switch the verdict at any time, the reasons kept. Submit is unavailable, saying why just left of it, while no verdict is chosen, while text typed in the reasons field is not yet added, and while the chosen verdict cannot be submitted as it stands, as RED without a reason or a required field of its own left empty (J-HUMAN-FORM); a result the library still refuses is not submitted: what failed is shown and the draft stays correctable.
- **J-HUMAN-FORM**: an eval may ask more of a verdict than its reasons: its pass or fail schema's other fields. While that verdict is chosen they stand in the review's request under its Tools: text, number, true/false and choice fields, a list of text with `+ Add` and `✕` per item, and repeated groups of fields with `+ Add` for a group and `✕` per group, one group always left to type in. Constants are filled in and not shown; `*` marks a required field and an empty optional one says Optional in grey; the field the reasons fill is not among them. There is no JSON editor.
- **J-HUMAN-TEXT**: text fields take any language, including Korean composed by an input method; no key typed during a composition, and no key without Ctrl (⌘) typed in a field, does anything but enter text.
- **J-HUMAN-ESC**: Cancel beside the running work cancels it. In the Detail, Close or a click on the graph's background first cancels the review's running work, then closes the Detail, the draft kept (J-DETAIL). (Also Esc, which first leaves a reason being edited or the reasons field, then takes the same steps and, for a review shown in Review, returns to Review's Pane as its last step, showing it first when it is hidden.)

## Artifact Viewer, Point and the pet overlay

An Artifact's type gives it the tools of the plugin that provides that type: `files` (File
Explorer), `markdown` (Markdown viewer) and `artifactize` (Artifactize Viewer) are built in,
and a project can add others, such as the `html` example. A plugin's Human tool opens its
viewer in an Artifact Viewer window of the app.

- **J-VIEWER**: a viewer tool, run from a review's Tools or an Artifact's Tools in the Detail (`▶`), opens an Artifact Viewer: a window of the app of its own, titled `{Artifact} — {viewer}`, showing that Artifact in its plugin's viewer, never the system's default application. Running it again while that Artifact's viewer is open brings that window to the front. Closing a viewer's window closes it alone. A Custom Artifact's `launch` and `output` tools work as before (J-HUMAN-TOOL).
- **J-VIEWER-FILES**: the File Explorer shows the Artifact's files as a tree at its left, folders folding and unfolding by a click, and a click on a file shows its text in the document view at its right, with line numbers, read-only.
- **J-VIEWER-MARKDOWN**: the Markdown viewer shows the Artifact's Markdown rendered, headings, lists, links, tables and code as they read; an Artifact of several Markdown files lists them at its left, a click showing one.
- **J-VIEWER-ARTIFACTIZE**: the Artifactize Viewer shows what an Artifact of type `artifactize` covers: the Artifacts declared under its path as nodes and the references between them as plain connections, with no verdicts (it shows what the project declares, not a Run). A click on an Artifact opens its panel at the right: its name and type, its evals, each with its kind, its title, its criteria and the Artifacts it reads. The graph pans and zooms as Verify's does.
- **J-VIEWER-PLUGIN**: a project's own viewer plugin (the `html` example included) shows its own page inside the Artifact Viewer; for `html`, the Artifact's page in an embedded browser that can be used as in a browser. A plugin's page reaches the Artifact's files and the active review only through the viewer's bridge, nothing else of the app. A viewer page asks the bridge for its context when it starts: until the page first reaches the bridge the strip says *Loading {plugin}…*, and a page that finished loading without reaching it is said, in the strip, not to have connected, until it does. Once connected, the strip shows the latest status line the page reports (for `html`, the page shown and its title once loaded, or why it failed), each replacing the last; the strip's Point tells the page to point (J-POINT).
- **J-POINT**: while this reviewer holds a claimed review, its active review (the one last shown in Review), every viewer that supports it shows **Point**. A click on Point, then a click on a place in the Artifact, adds that place as readable text to the review's Points (J-HUMAN-POINTS), with the Artifact and viewer it was pointed in: an HTML element as a CSS selector (`#checkout .total`), a Markdown position as its line and column (`L42:C9`), an Artifact or an eval in the Artifactize Viewer as its ID (`front-design`, `front-design/matches-layout`). Pointing never submits and never writes a reason. Esc, or Point again, stops pointing without adding anything. With no claimed review, Point is not shown.
- **J-PET**: while this reviewer holds a claimed review and works anywhere but its evaluation space (another mode, an Artifact Viewer, another application), a small pet overlay stays at the bottom right of the screen, above every window, tied to the active review: the pet, the review's eval and Run (a click on them shows the review in Review, J-REVIEW-REF), and the same draft, split as Review's is, compactly: its points, each with Insert, which puts its text into the overlay's reasons field (J-HUMAN-POINTS), and its reasons, each with Remove; then the reasons field with **Add** (also Enter, J-HUMAN-REASONS) and **Point** beside them (pointing in the Artifact Viewer of the review's Artifact that is open, and disabled, saying why, while none is); then, apart, a row with the GREEN and RED choice and **Submit**, unavailable and saying why as in Review (J-HUMAN-RED). Typing in it writes the draft, kept as focus moves between the overlay, a viewer and other windows. Submit submits the review as Review's does (J-HUMAN-GREEN, J-HUMAN-RED). The overlay goes when the review is submitted or released, and while the review's evaluation space is in front.

## Settings

- **J-SET-PROVIDERS**: the Providers tab lists the built-in eval and direct providers vertically on the left (Codex, Claude Agent, OpenAI, Anthropic, Gemini), then the custom providers by name, then **+ Custom provider** at the bottom. A click on one shows its setup on the right.
- **J-SET-CODEX**: Codex signs in to or out of the ChatGPT account through the browser; a sign-in in progress can be cancelled, and when the browser cannot return, **Finish by hand** unfolds the sign-in's address and a field where the redirect URL can be entered, which **Finish sign-in** sends.
- **J-SET-CLAUDE-AGENT**: Claude Agent shows Claude Code's sign-in as Claude Code itself reports it: `claude` not found, not signed in, signed in with its login method and plan, or the error it failed with; Check again asks again. It signs in and out by running the user's own `claude auth login` and `claude auth logout`: Claude Code opens the browser and keeps its own login, which artifactize never reads, copies or stores. While it signs in, the sign-in can be cancelled, and **Finish by hand** unfolds the URL Claude Code prints and a field where the code its page gives can be entered, which **Finish sign-in** sends; the status updates when it ends.
- **J-SET-CHAT-AGENTS**: the **Chat Agents** tab configures ACP coding agents for Chat (never used for evals): on the left, the built-in ACP presets (Antigravity, Claude ACP, Codex ACP, Gemini CLI, OpenCode, Copilot, Cursor, Goose), installed ACP Registry agents, custom ACP agents, **Browse Registry**, and **+ Custom agent**. Selecting an agent shows its status, resolved executable path, version, native isolation metadata, live `configOptions` / models (probed in an empty private tempdir) and saved workspace trust entries with **Revoke**. **Browse Registry** discovers official ACP Registry entries and installs a pinned exact version into `$STATE/chat/runtimes/` only after full-spec consent (exact version, platform, distribution kind, package or URL, args, env, artifact SHA-256, and explicit build-script consent when needed). **+ Custom agent** saves or edits a machine-local custom ACP agent (ID, display name, command, arguments, environment variables, and owner-only secret env values never echoed back) and can remove it.
- **J-SET-ANTIGRAVITY**: Antigravity is a built-in ACP preset under **Chat Agents** that runs Chat with the models of the person's Google account through their own Antigravity CLI (`agy`), and serves Chat only: an eval naming it in `backend` is a declaration error, and a custom provider cannot be an alias of it. It shows the CLI's sign-in as the CLI itself reports it: `agy` not found, too old (its version and the version it needs), not signed in, signed in with its Google account, or the error it failed with; Check again asks again. It signs in and out through the user's own `agy`: the CLI opens Google's sign-in in the browser and keeps its own login, which artifactize never reads, copies or stores. While it signs in, the sign-in can be cancelled, and **Finish by hand** unfolds the URL the CLI prints and a field where the code Google's page gives can be entered, which **Finish sign-in** sends; the status updates when it ends.
- **J-SET-KEY**: OpenAI, Anthropic and Gemini each show whether their key is set, from the environment (`OPENAI_API_KEY`, `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY`, `GEMINI_API_KEY`) or saved here, never its value. A key can be entered and saved, or removed; a set environment variable is used first.
- **J-SET-CUSTOM**: **+ Custom provider** adds a provider with a name: an alias of a built-in provider the library offers as an alias target (Codex, OpenAI, Anthropic or Gemini; not Claude Agent or Antigravity, which run through their own CLIs, J-SET-ANTIGRAVITY), used with its sign-in or key, or an endpoint with its API (OpenAI Responses, OpenAI Chat Completions, Anthropic Messages or Gemini), base URL and optional key. Any number can be added; a custom provider can be changed or removed. An eval uses it by its name in `backend`.
- **J-SET-MODELS**: a built-in or saved custom provider's setup has Models: its List models button asks the provider what it offers and lists each model by its name with its ID beside it, says `No models listed.` when it offers none, or says why it failed. Antigravity lists the models its signed-in Google account offers under Chat Agents; while it is not signed in, List models says so. The button is disabled while the list is read, and choosing another provider empties the list until List models is used again.
- **J-SET-REMOTE**: the Remote tab signs in to a shared review store with its URL, share level and token, shows its status in one line (signed in or out, the reviewer with the token's scopes and what was pushed today, or the error), pushes results (or a dry run), and signs out. Signed in, it shows the store's URL and share level as facts, without the fields and the button that sign in, which come back once signed out. While a sign-in, a sign-out or a push runs, the tab's buttons are disabled.
- **J-SET-REMOTE-STATUS**: on the Remote tab, Check status, beside Sign in to the store while signed out and first of the buttons while signed in, reads the store's status again into the status line. A push's or a dry run's result, what it pushed or would push, what the store already had and what it skipped, takes the status line until Check status reads the status again, and what was pushed today follows a push; an error of the store, a push or a check takes the line in red.
- **J-SET-SIGNOFF-GRACE**: the Limits tab's Sign-off idle grace says in one line what it is, how long a Run with only sign-offs left, none of them claimed, waits before it ends; its seconds field holds the grace in force (120 seconds unless changed). Save, usable once the field holds another value, changes it for the Runs started afterwards, the field then holding the new grace; only a failure to save is said.
