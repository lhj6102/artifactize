# Desktop app: user journeys

What a person can do in the desktop app, from the app's real behaviour. Each journey has an id
(`J-...`) and ends in something visible. It is the artifactize app of this
machine, not of one repository: it shows every live branch of every project with Runs
on this machine and every Human review waiting for a person. It shows and changes the same state
as the `artifactize` CLI and MCP server; a change made by any of them shows in the others.

The window has three modes over that one state, each a workspace of its own above one bottom
bar: **Chat**, where a person works with Artifactize's own agent on a branch; **Verify**, where
branches, their Runs and their Currents are watched and their Artifacts defined; and **Review**,
where Human reviews are found and evaluated. References connect them: a reference names one exact
thing (a Chat session, a Run, an Artifact of a Run, a review), and following it shows that thing
in its mode; every reference has a way back.

Hovering only highlights what is under the pointer: no popup, summary, tooltip or menu opens on
hover, and information opens only on a click; the one exception is the native tooltip of a
whole's Minimize or Expand button (J-GRAPH-MINIMIZE). A pane is never wider than its place: its width
is set by the layout or by a drag, never by what it shows, and what it shows wraps or scrolls in
its own box. The Detail's tabs show as rounded chips, the chosen one filled; a choice within a
tab, such as its Summary or its log, shows as plain text, the chosen one bold in the strong text
colour and the other in the quieter one. The marks the journeys write as characters (a tool's `▶`, a star's `☆` and `★`,
`✕`, `+ Add`, a verdict's glyph) are drawn as the app's own icons, one per meaning, each
read aloud as what it means; a Tool log's lines are text and keep their characters.

A screen shows what the task at hand needs and says it once. What runs or went well is shown
as an icon, a count or a time, not as a sentence; the app speaks in sentences only for what
went wrong, why a control is disabled, a place that would otherwise be empty, a decision
that cannot be undone and a setting whose name cannot say what it is (J-SET-SIGNOFF-GRACE).
What an agent or a person wrote (a Chat message, a tool's output, an instruction) is content and
is shown as written.

Every journey names the visible way to do what it describes: a button, a menu, the command
palette or a click. Keyboard shortcuts are optional: a key written in parentheses with "also"
is one the app may offer for the same thing, and the app may offer others or none. Where it
offers a key, the command palette and Help name it; a key without Ctrl (⌘) pressed in a field
(a true/false field and a choice included) does only the field's own work, and no key acts
during an input method's composition (J-HUMAN-TEXT).
Enter in a field that has no button of its own, a Path, a part or a comment, is how that field
is written, not a shortcut.

## Window

- **J-LAUNCH**: the app opens on one window titled "Artifactize": the shown mode's workspace above the bottom bar (J-BAR). Started without arguments it shows Verify. Started with `--repo PATH` it shows Verify with that branch selected; with `review ID` it shows Review with that review open (J-REVIEW-REF), and with `review` alone Review with the newest waiting Human review open; with `graph --repo PATH` (also the declared Human `graph` tool) it shows Verify with that branch's Current.
- **J-BAR**: one bar runs along the window's bottom in every mode and never moves: the brand alone at its left; **Chat**, **Verify** and **Review** in its middle, the shown mode marked as on, Review carrying the number of Human reviews waiting for a person whenever there is at least one (the same number whatever Review's filter shows); **Settings**, its gear icon alone, at its right. A click on a mode shows it (also Ctrl+1, Ctrl+2, Ctrl+3). A click on the brand opens the command palette (J-PALETTE).
- **J-MODES**: showing another mode changes only the workspace above the bar: each mode keeps its own place while another is shown, so going back to it shows it as it was left, its selection, scroll (in Chat, each session's place in its conversation, J-CHAT-READ), open panes and Detail, typed text, a Chat turn still streaming, a review's form and a Pane's search or filter included. A mode is shown by the bar, by the palette, or by following a reference into it (J-CHAT-RUN, J-RUN-ORIGIN, J-DETAIL-REVIEW, J-REVIEW-BACK).
- **J-PANE-ROWS**: in every mode's Pane a row that is chosen by a click (a Chat session, a Verify branch and its Run entries, a review) is chosen by a click anywhere on it: the whole row, across the Pane's width and of a comfortable height, the same in every Pane.
- **J-SETTINGS**: the bar's Settings button opens Settings as a modal over the window, on its Providers tab, its header naming this machine's reviewer, the name the app claims reviews as; its Close, the Settings button again or a click outside it closes it, the window beneath as it was (also Ctrl+, and Esc). Settings has three tabs, Providers, Remote and Limits, and a click on one shows it (also ← and → on the focused tab).
- **J-PALETTE**: a click on the bar's brand opens the command palette (also Ctrl+K). It lists every command that can run now, each with the key that does the same where the app has one: the app's (Keyboard shortcuts, Show Settings, Show Chat, Show Verify, Show Review, Show or hide the Pane, New chat, Search the branches, Filter the reviews, Open a request by ID, Refresh, Quit), then those of what the shown mode shows, a graph's or a Human review's, and while Settings is open those of Settings. Typing filters them to the commands whose names contain the text, a group's heading staying only while one of its commands does. A click on a command runs it (also Enter on the selected one), and a click outside the palette closes it, nothing run (also Esc). It opens again after any use.
- **J-HELP**: the palette's Keyboard shortcuts (also `?`) opens Help: the keys the app has for what has focus (a Pane, a Chat, a graph with its Detail, a Human review, Settings) and, for a graph, what each of its marks means: each verdict's icon beside its word, on a Current what verify would do. Its Close or a click outside it closes it.
- **J-REFRESH**: the palette's Refresh (also F5 or Ctrl+R) reloads the state from disk, scans the branches again and, on a Current, scans its worktree again.
- **J-QUIT**: the palette's Quit, or closing the window, quits (also `q` outside a field), closing the app's Artifact Viewer windows and the pet overlay with it. With Human review claims held by this window it asks first, naming what is held: Stay keeps the window and its claims, Release and quit releases the claims and quits (also Esc and `u`); a claim's draft stays either way (J-HUMAN-DRAFT). With a Chat turn running it says so too, and quitting stops that turn as Stop would (J-CHAT-STOP).
- **J-OPEN-ID**: the palette's "Open a request by ID" (also Ctrl+O) opens a dialog with one field for the ID; its Open shows Review with that saved request open, as a reference opens it (J-REVIEW-REF): a waiting Human review actionable, any other request read-only. Cancel closes the dialog.
- **J-UPDATE**: when a newer stable release of artifactize is out, Settings shows `{version} available` in its header (J-SETTINGS). A click on it opens a dialog with the running and the new version and how to update the way this app was installed: the command to run, as text that can be selected and copied, or the file to download and install by hand; when the way is unknown, the release page. A link opens in the browser; Close closes the dialog (also Esc), the notice staying in Settings. A Microsoft Store install never shows it: the Store updates the app. The app looks up the latest release on GitHub when it starts and once a day while it runs, the same lookup `artifactize doctor` reuses, and never downloads or installs anything itself.
- **J-PANE-TOGGLE**: each mode has a Pane at its left: Chat's sessions, Verify's branches, Review's reviews. The palette's "Show or hide the Pane" (also Ctrl+B) hides the Pane and its divider in every mode, the workspace taking the window's whole width with its view as it was, and shows them again, each Pane at the width it had, its search or filter, selection and expanded rows as they were. Hidden while focus is in a Pane, focus moves to the mode's main area. Whatever needs a Pane shows it again first, such as the palette's "Search the branches" (J-PANE-SEARCH). The Panes are shown when the app starts. A graph, its Detail, a Chat and a Human review are read and used whole with the Pane hidden.
- **J-RESIZE**: the divider between a mode's Pane and its main area can be dragged to resize them; the split stays as dragged in every mode whatever the main area shows, and comes back as dragged when a hidden Pane is shown again. Dragging the Detail's left edge sets its width, between a readable column and leaving some graph visible, never over the Pane; the Detail keeps that width when opened again and in the next launch. Hovering a divider or the edge shows only the resize cursor.
- **J-LIVE**: a change made by another process (a new Run, a result, a claim, a submission, a draft written elsewhere, a worktree added or removed, a Chat session's turn) appears in the open app without a reload.

## Chat

Chat is Artifactize's own agent. A session belongs to one branch of one project and works in
that branch's worktree, with the reviewer's own environment, as a terminal there would; it uses
one set of tools whatever the model: reading, listing, finding, searching and editing the
worktree's files, running a command there, and Verify (starting a verify, waiting for it, and
reading its status, results, requests, Runs, the project's declarations and their graph). No
other agent program is installed or set up for it, and it asks no approval per tool.

- **J-CHAT**: Chat's Pane has **New chat** at its top, a search field under it, and the sessions of every project under them, grouped by project and branch, newest first, each one line: its title and, at the right, `running` while a turn runs; once a turn ended while the session was not shown, how it ended (`finished`, `failed`, `stopped`, or `interrupted` when the app or the agent's process ended in the middle of it) until the session is shown; else its age. Typing in the search field lists only the sessions whose title, project or branch matches, as the library finds them, and says so when none does; emptying it (also Esc in it) lists them all again. Human reviews waiting for a person are not marked here: the bar's Review count says so (J-BAR). A click on a session shows it in the main area: its title and its branch in the header row, the conversation, and the composer at the bottom. Sessions and their conversations are kept: they are listed and shown the same after the app restarts. With no session yet, the main area says how to start one. A session whose branch cannot be worked on now (no longer checked out, its worktree gone) stays listed and shown; its composer says why and sends nothing.
- **J-CHAT-NEW**: **New chat** opens a dialog with the session's branch, a choice of the live branches (the branch selected in Verify first); under it the model the session starts with and its effort (that branch's last session's model and effort, else the first model a connected provider lists, Antigravity's once it is signed in, with its default effort), which says it is reading the providers' models until that is known, and says to connect a provider in Settings when none lists a model; Create waits until the model is known; Create (also Enter) shows a new, empty session on that branch, titled after its first message once sent, with the composer focused. Cancel closes the dialog (also Esc).
- **J-CHAT-SEND**: text typed in the composer is sent by **Send** at its bottom right (also Enter; Shift+Enter breaks the line). The message shows at once, exactly as typed, and the agent's turn streams in under it: its text as it is written, read as Markdown (paragraphs, lists, links, which open in the browser, inline code and code blocks, each code block with **Copy**; anything else in it, HTML included, shows as its text), and what it did between its words. Each tool it uses is one quiet line saying what it does and on what (`Read checkout/total.ts`, `Edited checkout/total.ts +3 −1`), in progress while it runs, and with the reason in red when it failed. Tools in a row that went well fold into one summary line of what they did (`Read 4 files · ran 2 commands`), which a click unfolds into their lines and folds again; a running or failed tool stays its own line, and a Run reference is never folded (J-CHAT-RUN). A click on a tool's line unfolds its details in place: what it was asked with, its exit code and the end of its output, as much as the library keeps; a second click folds them. A turn ends when the agent answers. The composer is usable while a turn runs; what is typed meanwhile stays in it and is sent with Send once the turn ends. A message the library does not accept is not shown as sent: a line above the composer says why, with **Copy** and **Restore**, which puts the message back in the composer while the composer is empty and is disabled while it holds newer text. A message the library accepted is never sent again unless the person sends it.
- **J-CHAT-READ**: a session's conversation follows its newest line only while it is read at its end: scrolled up, it stays where it is read while the turn streams and its Run references change in place. Each session keeps where it is read while another session or mode is shown and when its conversation is read again, and is shown there again; a session not read before opens at its end. Away from the end, **Scroll to end** stands at the conversation's bottom; a click on it shows the end, which is followed again. A session opened at a Run's reference (J-RUN-ORIGIN) stays there, not following, until it is read at its end again.
- **J-CHAT-RUN**: each verify the agent starts shows in the conversation as a Run reference: its Run ID, its status as it changes (running, then its verdict), what failed or waits for a person, and **Open in Verify**. A click on Open in Verify shows Verify with that Run chosen, its branch expanded and its entry shown; a session with several verifies has one reference for each, each opening its own Run.
- **J-CHAT-STOP**: while a turn runs, Send becomes **Stop** (also Esc in the composer, never Esc in the model menu, J-CHAT-MODEL); a click on it shows **Stopping…** in its place until the turn has stopped, and stops the turn where it is: what the agent did so far stays in the conversation, a running tool is stopped, the turn ends marked as stopped, and the composer sends the next message as usual. A turn the app could not finish because it quit or the agent's process ended ends marked as interrupted, with why, what it did so far kept, and is never resumed or resent by itself. A turn that fails (the provider refuses or cannot be reached) ends with why, in red, and the session stays usable.
- **J-CHAT-MODEL**: the model selector sits at the composer's bottom left, showing the session's model and, for a model that has reasoning efforts, its effort (`GPT-6.2 Sol · high`). A click opens its menu upward, a filter field at its top focused: typing in it lists only the models whose name or ID, or whose provider's name, contains the text, and says so when none does. The menu holds every provider Settings lists that is set up, each with the models that provider offers, the session's one marked; a provider whose listing failed says why under its name. Providers not set up yet (no key, Antigravity not signed in) are named together under **Connect provider**. Antigravity's models, those of its Google account, are there once it is signed in (J-SET-ANTIGRAVITY). A model that has reasoning efforts, as the library lists them, shows them on its row, the session's one marked on the session's model. A click on an effort sets that model with that effort for the session's next turns; a click on a model's name sets it with the session's effort when the model has it, else with the model's default effort; a model without efforts is set alone; either way the menu closes. **Connect provider** at the menu's end opens Settings on its Providers tab (J-SET-PROVIDERS). The menu closes on a click outside it and on Esc, which closes only the menu and never stops a turn (J-CHAT-STOP). A new session starts with the model and effort its branch's last session used, else the first model listed with its default effort.

## Verify

Verify is the machine's monitor: its Pane holds a search and the live branches with their
Runs; its main area shows a Run's graph or a branch's Current, with the Detail of the chosen
Artifact.

- **J-PANE**: Verify's Pane is one column: a search field at the top and the branches below it. The divider between it and the main area is shown whenever the Pane is (J-PANE-TOGGLE). There is no Scope heading, no Runs list and no list of waiting reviews: those are Review's (J-REVIEW-LIST), and the bar counts them (J-BAR).
- **J-BRANCHES**: each branch reads as its project and branch, `artifactize / feat/x`, the branch in bold and the project quieter: a Git worktree that exists now with the branch it has checked out (a detached one by its folder's name), or a folder outside Git that exists now and holds its definitions (`.artifactize/`). Its projects are those with Runs on this machine and the one the app was started for with `--repo` or `graph --repo`; a worktree or folder that was removed, or whose worktree Git no longer lists, is not shown, and worktrees added or removed appear or go within seconds, without a reload. Who ran a Run, an agent session or a person, makes no entry of its own: a branch's Runs are the Runs of that project on that branch, from whichever folder they ran in.
- **J-BRANCH-RUNS**: a branch is folded or expanded, any number expanded at once, with a chevron before its name. Folded, its newest Run sits under its name, a dot in its status colour, its status word and its age on the right (a running one its time so far); a branch without Runs shows nothing there. Expanded, its timeline sits there instead, newest first: one entry per Run, its status dot, status word and age and, below, one line: the comment the Run was requested with (`verify --comment`), cut short to one line, or else its Run ID, the dots joined by a line; while a Run is in progress on the branch the timeline starts with it, otherwise with the **Current**, a ring. **Older runs** at its end brings older Runs in below the oldest. A click on a branch expands it and shows its newest entry; a click on its chevron only expands or folds it; a click on an entry shows that one in the main area, the shown entry in a grey pill. The branch the app was started for starts expanded. (Also keys: ↑ ↓ or `j`/`k` through the branches and the timelines, → and ← to expand or fold, Enter into the main area, and Esc from there back to the Pane.)
- **J-BRANCH-STAR**: a star at the end of a branch's name line, faint until it is on, pins it: starred branches are listed before the others, each group in the search's order, and stay starred for every later monitor of this machine's user until the star is taken away.
- **J-PANE-SEARCH**: a click in the search field at Verify's Pane's top, or the palette's "Search the branches", focuses it, showing Verify and the Pane first when they are not shown (also `/`, and Ctrl+F, in a field too, unless a graph is shown and focus is outside the Pane and outside fields, J-GRAPH-FIND); its starting query is `sort:recent`. Free text and `key:value` terms must all match: free text matches part of a project or a branch, `repo:` and `branch:` part of that one field; `sort:recent` puts the branch with the most recent Run first and branches without Runs last, `sort:name` orders them by project and branch.
- **J-PANE-SUGGEST**: while a Pane's search or filter field is focused, typing a term lists suggestions under it that complete that term: the field's keys and, after a key, its values (the projects, the branches, the sort orders, the review kinds). A click on one puts it in place of the term; a key taken lists its values next. The suggestions close when the field loses focus or is emptied (also ↓ opens them, ↑ ↓ choose one, Tab or Enter takes the chosen one or the first, and Esc closes them, then puts back the field's starting query, then leaves the field).

## Verify: main area

- **J-MAIN-RUN**: a Run chosen in the Pane fills Verify's main area with the graph of its Artifacts and their verdicts. The main area's header is one row: the Run's ID and, beside it, the Run's headline, its verdict and what needs attention: each eval that is RED or ERROR and each sign-off that waits, by its status's icon and its name, cut at the row's end. Right under it come the graph's tool strip and the graph. The Run's tally, how long it took, its comment in full and what it waits for are in the Run's Detail (J-DETAIL). With nothing chosen, while loading, on an error or with an empty graph the graph's place stays and says so. What went wrong, such as Runs that cannot be read, shows as a few words in the main area's header without moving or hiding the graph; a click on it shows it in full, and another click folds it back.
- **J-RUN-ORIGIN**: a Run started by a Chat session says so first in the main area's header row, at its left before the Run's ID: `Chat · {session title}`. A click on it shows Chat with that session, scrolled to the Run's reference (J-CHAT-RUN) and not following its newest line until it is read at its end (J-CHAT-READ). A Run started from the CLI or an MCP client has no such link; the Run's Detail names what started it.
- **J-MAIN-CURRENT**: a branch's Current scans its worktree as it is now, as `artifactize status` does. It draws the graph of its definitions as they are now at once, every Artifact still being checked; as soon as the scan knows what verify would do with an Artifact, its node is coloured in place, as a Run's Artifacts are, by whether it is reusable (every eval's result would be reused without evaluation), needs evaluation, waits or is blocked. A node shows the icon of what would happen with it, its name and, once it is checked, how many of its evals would be reused of how many (J-GRAPH-COLOURS; read aloud, it says which, with how many of its evals would run, or that it is still being checked). While the scan runs, the main area's header shows, beside the Current's name, the checking icon and how many Artifacts are still being checked, as `n left` (read aloud as "Checking, n Artifacts left"); once it ends, the time it was scanned at; if it fails, why in words, the graph staying; Refresh sits at that row's end. It scans again when chosen again, on Refresh and when a Run of the branch ends, every Artifact being checked again; while a scan of the branch is still running, these show that scan instead of starting over, and after a Refresh one more scan follows once it ends, so what changed meanwhile shows.

## Verify: Detail

Both graphs, a Run's and the Current, take the pointer (also the keyboard: Tab reaches their Artifacts and Enter on one does what a click does). Above each graph, right under the main area's header, sits the graph's tool strip, one row of what acts on the graph and nothing else: Find, Zoom out, the zoom in percent and Zoom in, and Fit View; on a Current also New Artifact (J-NODE-NEW) and, while its top level is placed by hand, Reset layout (J-LAYOUT-RESET). What its marks mean, and the keys it has, are in Help (J-HELP). Nothing but the find field floats on a graph.

- **J-DETAIL**: a click on an Artifact opens its Detail, a panel docked at the graph's right edge over the graph, from the graph's top to the main area's bottom without a gap, below the main area's header and the graph's tool strip, which stay readable and usable, never beside the Pane, the graph staying visible on its left. It opens on the Artifact's own tab, which has no chips: its list of evals leads to each eval's tab (J-DETAIL-NEIGHBOURS). On an eval's tab the Detail's tabs read as a path, `[Artifact icon] {name} / [eval] [eval] …`: the Artifact's own chip, which leads back to the Artifact's tab, the Artifact icon before its name, read aloud as "Artifact {name}"; only the name is cut short when long (the Detail's title shows it whole), then a `/`, then one chip per eval showing its verdict's glyph and colour, on a Current whether it would run, would be reused or is still being checked. Eval chips that do not fit on one line wrap onto the next, within the Detail; the `/` stays with the Artifact's chip. Close is the one control of the Detail's header besides an Artifact's star (J-DETAIL-STAR). A click on another Artifact shows that Artifact's Detail; a click on the graph's background or Close closes it (also Esc), after first cancelling a Human review's running work and then leaving its form (J-HUMAN-ESC). A click on a Run's headline opens the Run's Detail: its outcome (its status, the evals that failed, its tally, how long it took and its comment in full), what started it (the CLI, an MCP client or a Chat session) and what it waits for. Everything in the Detail, transcript and log lines, long words and code included, stays within its width.
- **J-DETAIL-STAR**: a ☆ beside the title of an Artifact's Detail stars the Artifact, a favorite: it turns ★, and a click on it again takes the star away. A starred Artifact stays starred on every branch of its project, in Runs and Currents alike, for every later monitor of this machine's user until the star is taken away.
- **J-DETAIL-NEIGHBOURS**: the Artifact's own tab is shown first. It lists the Artifact's evals with their verdicts (a click on one shows its tab). Under Neighbours, folded until it is unfolded (J-DETAIL-SECTIONS), it draws the Artifact between its neighbours with the graph's nodes and edges, the Artifacts its evals use on its left and those whose evals use it on its right; the drawing does not pan, zoom or drag, and a click on a neighbour opens that neighbour's Detail. Then come its Human tools (J-DETAIL-TOOLS) and, on a Run, its Path, the target and, for a Live Artifact, its address; on a Current, its Definition and Source (J-DEFINITION); every other fact, what it waits for included, folds under Debug. Each of these is a section under its own header row (J-DETAIL-SECTIONS).
- **J-DETAIL-REVIEW**: on a Run's graph, an Artifact's tab lists each Human eval waiting for a person with its stage beside its verdict: claimable, yours, or taken by its claimant; under it, **Claim to review** while it can be claimed, **Release claim** while it is this reviewer's, and **Open in Review**, which shows Review with that review open (J-REVIEW-REF). A claim or a release made here is the same claim as in Review (J-HUMAN-CLAIM). The Human eval's tab holds the review itself and its own Open in Review.
- **J-DETAIL-SECTIONS**: below its tabs the Detail shows sections, each under a header row that is its name alone, with no count or value beside it; a section with nothing to show is not shown: an Artifact's Evals, Neighbours, Tools, Tool log (once a tool has run) and Path, or on a Current Definition and Source, an eval's Result, Execution options, Execution metadata and Fingerprint, a Run's Outcome, Waits for, What and Provenance, and Debug, or a Run's Technical, last. A click on a header row folds its section to that row alone, and again unfolds it (also Enter or Space on it focused). A section stays folded or unfolded as a person left it, by its name, in every Detail opened later and in the next launch. Until then what a tab is opened for is unfolded (Evals, Tools, Path, Definition, Result, Outcome, Waits for) and what is read now and then starts folded (Neighbours, Source, Execution options, Execution metadata, Fingerprint, What, Provenance, Debug and Technical). A Human review's Tools, Tool log and Result stand under the same rows and do not fold.
- **J-DETAIL-TOOLS**: an Artifact's chip lists the Human tools the Artifact declares or its type provides, with the look and behaviour of a Human review's Tools and Tool log (J-HUMAN-TOOL, J-HUMAN-LOG): a click on a tool's `▶` runs it at once in the branch's worktree as it is now, whichever Run is shown, with no claim, and records nothing as a review's evidence. While one of its tools runs, the Artifact's tool buttons are disabled and a line under them shows it running with Cancel, which stops it; a tool that cannot run says why there in red. Its runs show in the Artifact's Tool log while the app runs; the Tool log shows from the first run on, and a run unfolds a Tool log that was folded. The graph's nodes carry no tool buttons.
- **J-DETAIL-SUMMARY**: an eval's tab shows its Summary: its status, beside it in grey what running it took and spent (time, tokens in `k` or `M`, model) or what reuse saved; its instruction; its result, each owner field by name and a list as numbered items; then Execution options, Execution metadata and Fingerprint when present, folded until unfolded (J-DETAIL-SECTIONS); every other fact, what it waits for included, folds under Debug. What the status says is not repeated below it. On a Current it shows whether the eval would run, would be reused or is still being checked and why, and the result it would reuse. An eval with a log switches below the tabs between its Summary, shown first, and its log, a plain text choice a level below the tabs: an Agent eval's Agent log (its transcript, with its own scrollbar) or a runtime eval's Runtime log (its saved output). A Human eval's tab in a Run holds its review and sign-off.
- **J-GRAPH-COLOURS**: a node says its verdict (GREEN, running or waiting for a Human, waiting, RED, ERROR, blocked), on a Current what would happen (reused, would run, waiting, blocked) or that it is still being checked, with that verdict's icon before its name and a strip along its bottom, both in the verdict's colour; its face and its outline stay neutral. After its name a node with evals says how many of them are GREEN of how many it has (`2/3`), on a Current how many would be reused; one still being checked says none. Edges are neutral, except an edge that leaves a RED, ERROR or blocked Artifact, drawn in that Artifact's colour, so what holds a blocked Artifact shows. Icons, strips, counts and edges change in place when a result arrives or a scan learns what would happen. A verdict has one colour wherever the app shows it: a node's colour is the colour the same status has in a Pane, in a chip and in a row.
- **J-GRAPH-PARTS**: on both graphs an Artifact made of parts, those its `parts` names, is drawn as a box that encloses its parts' nodes, its name, its verdict's icon and a strip in its verdict's colour at the box's top; a part with parts of its own is a box inside it, as deep as the parts go. The box is the whole and the nodes in it are its parts, and each is an Artifact of its own: a click on the box outside its parts opens the whole's Detail and a click on a part opens the part's. Edges to and from a whole meet its box; edges between Artifacts in different boxes are drawn and coloured like any other. The layout is made for parts that belong to one whole, each whole's parts laid out together inside it. A part of several wholes is drawn inside each of them, each copy with the part's edges; selecting one copy highlights every copy of it, and its Detail says that it is drawn in each of those wholes, naming them. The find field lists such a part once and goes to its copy in the first of its wholes.
- **J-GRAPH-MINIMIZE**: a whole's box has a Minimize button at its top right; a click on it draws the whole as a node of a node's size: its name, its verdict's icon and colour, how many parts it has and an Expand button, whose click draws its box again. The palette's "Minimize or expand the whole" does the same for the whole whose Detail is open (also `m`, for the whole focused on the graph or whose button is focused, else the whole whose Detail is open, while focus is outside the Pane, focus staying where it was). The edges to and from the Artifacts a minimized whole hides meet it instead, those between the same two drawn once in the light that comes first, as an Artifact's light comes from its evals'; edges among them are not drawn. A minimized whole is otherwise a node: a click on it outside its button opens its Detail, and it is highlighted while an Artifact it hides is open. The find field lists what it hides as before and goes to the minimized whole for it. A whole stays minimized on every graph of its branch, its Runs' and its Current, until it is expanded or the app quits; a part of several wholes stays drawn inside those not minimized.
- **J-GRAPH-LARGE**: a graph too large to draw at once stays navigable: panning or zooming the camera draws the Artifacts it brings into view. Zoomed out so far that names would be too small to read, each Artifact is drawn as a block of its verdict's colour and a whole as its box's outline in its colour, and a click on one still opens its Detail; zoomed in until names read, each is a node again (J-GRAPH-COLOURS).
- **J-GRAPH-NAVIGATOR**: whenever part of a graph lies outside the view, whatever the graph's size, a Navigator shows at the graph's bottom right, left of the Detail when one is open: the whole graph in small, each Artifact a block of its verdict's colour, with the part in view framed. A click or a drag in it moves the camera so that place is in the middle of the view, the zoom kept. It goes when the whole graph is in view again. Hovering it opens nothing.
- **J-GRAPH-FIND**: the graph's tool strip has a Find button (also Ctrl+F with focus outside the Pane and outside fields, on the graph or in the Detail). With a Run's graph or a Current shown, a click on it opens a find field over the graph's top left, focused. With nothing typed it lists the graph's starred Artifacts (J-DETAIL-STAR) as if they had been found, in the graph's order, each with its verdict's glyph and colour; when none of the graph's Artifacts is starred, it says how to star one. Typing lists the Artifacts and evals whose names contain the text, ignoring case, in the graph's order: an Artifact by its whole name, an eval as `{Artifact} / {eval}`, each with its verdict's glyph and colour (on a Current, what would happen); the first 50, then how many more match, or that none does. No row shows as chosen when the list appears (also ↑ and ↓ choose a row, and Enter opens the chosen one, or the first while none is chosen). A click on a row opens it, an Artifact's Detail on its Artifact tab and an eval's on that eval's tab, closes the field and moves the camera so the Artifact sits in the middle of the graph left of the Detail, zooming in only as far as its name needs to be readable. A click outside the field closes it and does nothing else, the graph and the Detail as they were (also Esc).
- **J-GRAPH-CAMERA**: a graph shows whole when it first appears, never enlarged beyond its nodes' own size: a small graph sits at that size in the middle of the graph's place. From then on only the person moves its camera, by panning, zooming (the wheel, or the tool strip's Zoom out and Zoom in, the zoom in percent between them), Fit View (whole again, with the same limit), the Navigator (J-GRAPH-NAVIGATOR), opening what the find field found (J-GRAPH-FIND), creating an Artifact (J-NODE-NEW) or minimizing or expanding a whole (J-GRAPH-MINIMIZE), which keeps that whole's top left corner where it was on the screen: opening, switching, resizing or closing the Detail, a result or scan arriving, a new layout and an edit (J-NODE-MOVE, J-PARTS-DRAG) never zoom or pan it.

## Verify: editing

A branch's Current is where its Artifacts are defined. Each Artifact is one file,
`.artifactize/**/{name}.artf`, and every edit below writes those files as the CLI reads them:
what an Artifact is, and beside it, in sections of the same file that change nothing verify
does, where it is placed (`[annotations.style]`) and the comment people and agents leave on it
(`[annotations.comment]`). An edit is checked against the whole project before anything is written: one
that would leave the project invalid, or that is not allowed, writes nothing and says why.
After an edit the Current is scanned again (J-MAIN-CURRENT). The app has no undo: edits are
files in the worktree, and Git keeps their history. The definitions as they are now draw the
branch's Runs too, places and comments included, but a Run's graph is a record and offers
none of these edits.

- **J-DEFINITION**: on a Current, an Artifact's tab has its Definition where a Run's has Path: the file that defines it, its Path as a field (a target that is missing is said in red beside it), its Parts, each with `✕`, and `+ Add`, then Comment, Rename and Delete; under it Source, folded until unfolded, the file's text. On a Run's graph the tab keeps its Path and has no Definition or Source.
- **J-DEFINITION-EDIT**: a changed Path is written by Enter in its field (also Esc puts it back). Typing in the Source shows Save and Revert; Save (also Ctrl+S in the Source) writes the file, and the graph and the Definition follow; Revert puts back the file's text. An edit the project would not accept writes nothing: the error shows in red under the field or the Source, for the Source with its line and column, and the text stays to be corrected.
- **J-DEFINITION-DRAFT**: a Path being changed or a Parts field being typed in is never written by leaving it: Close or a click on the graph's background closes the Detail as always, and that Artifact's Detail, opened again on the same branch, shows the Path or the part still being typed.
- **J-DEFINITION-CONFLICT**: when a file an edit would write has changed on disk since the Current read it, by a terminal or an agent, the edit writes nothing, whether it is a save, a Path, a part, a rename, a deletion, a drop, a move, a Reset layout or a comment: the refusal names the file, under the field or the Source for an edit in the Detail and as the Main area's notice for one on the graph, and the Current is scanned again, so the graph and the Definition show what is on disk. A Source with typed text keeps it until Revert shows the file's text. Typed text in a Source is never overwritten by the graph either: a move, a drop, a Reset layout or a comment that would write a file whose Source holds typed text is refused the same way, the notice naming the file, until that Source is saved or reverted.
- **J-PARTS-EDIT**: `+ Add` under Parts opens a field that suggests the project's Artifacts (also Esc closes it); a click on a suggestion, or Enter in the field, adds the chosen one to the file's `parts` and the graph draws it inside this whole's box, in free space when the box is placed by hand. `✕` on a part removes it, and the place the box kept for it, and the graph draws it outside. A part that would make a cycle is refused, the reason under Parts, nothing written.
- **J-NODE-NEW**: the tool strip's New Artifact opens a dialog with Name and Path. Create (also Enter) writes `.artifactize/{name}.artf` with that path, closes the dialog, draws the new node, in free space when the top level is placed by hand, and opens its Detail, the camera moved to it as to a found Artifact (J-GRAPH-FIND). A name already used, ignoring case, or a name or path that is not valid says why under its field and writes nothing; Cancel closes the dialog (also Esc).
- **J-NODE-RENAME**: the Definition's Rename opens a dialog with the name. Rename (also Enter) renames the file where it is and, in the same edit, every `parts` entry and `{name}` reference naming it and the place every whole keeps for it in its box; its own place and comment stay in the renamed file. The Detail and the graph show the new name. A name already used or not valid says why under the field; Cancel closes the dialog (also Esc).
- **J-NODE-DELETE**: the Definition's Delete opens a dialog that names the file it removes. Delete removes it, and with it its place, its parts' places and its comment, closing the dialog and the Detail, and the node goes. While another Artifact names it, in its `parts` or a reference, the deletion is refused: the dialog says which Artifacts name it, and nothing is written.
- **J-NODE-MOVE**: on a Current, a node, a minimized whole or a whole's title row can be dragged: it is drawn lifted and follows the pointer with its edges and its comment, a whole carrying its parts. Released in the box it was in (or outside every box when it was in none), it stays there, and the places of every Artifact in that box, as shown, are written once, on release: a move in a whole's box into that whole's file alone; a move at the top level into the moved node's own file, except the first move of a top level laid out automatically, which writes every top-level node's place into its own file in one edit. The places hold after a restart and draw the branch's Runs too. A box, and the top level, is laid out automatically until something in it is moved; a whole's box fits around its parts and their comments; an Artifact that appears later in a box placed by hand goes into free space. A press and release without moving is a click (J-DETAIL). A drag on the background, an edge, a whole's face outside its title row, or an Artifact drawn as a block (J-GRAPH-LARGE) pans. (Also Esc during a drag puts it back, writing nothing.) On a Run's graph nothing moves: a drag pans.
- **J-LAYOUT-RESET**: a box placed by hand has Reset layout in its title row, and the tool strip has it while the top level is placed by hand. A click lays that box out automatically again, boxes inside it keeping their own: its places leave the whole's file, or, for the top level, every top-level node's place leaves its file.
- **J-PARTS-DRAG**: while a drop would make the dragged Artifact a part of another, that whole's box, or the node under the pointer, is highlighted. Released there, the dragged Artifact is added to that Artifact's `parts`, drawn where it was dropped (under the box's parts where it would cover one), and, in the same edit, leaves the whole whose box it was dragged out of, its place written into the new whole's file and removed from the old one's; released outside every box, it only leaves that whole, its place at the top level written into its own file. For a copy of a shared part only the whole it was dragged out of changes. A drop that would make a cycle highlights nothing: released there, the node returns and the Main area's notice says why, nothing written. Edges are not dragged: a reference is changed in the Source.
- **J-COMMENT**: an Artifact can carry one comment, a note people and agents leave on it, apart from the comment a Run was requested with. On both graphs it is drawn as text alone directly under its node, under a whole's title row inside its box, under a minimized whole, and under every copy of a shared part: in the quieter ink, wrapped at the width of what it is under, its first three lines and then an ellipsis when it is longer; no card, outline, colour, icon or count, and hovering it shows nothing. It moves with its node. Where Artifacts are drawn as blocks (J-GRAPH-LARGE), and for the parts a minimized whole hides, no comment is drawn. Read aloud, it is read after its node. On a Run's graph a click on a comment does what a click on its node does.
- **J-COMMENT-EDIT**: on a Current, the Definition's Comment, or a click on a comment, opens a field in place under the node holding the comment whole, the field focused. Enter writes the text into the node's file and changes nothing else in it, Shift+Enter breaks the line, and Enter on an empty field removes the comment from the file; a click outside the field closes it and puts the comment back, nothing written (also Esc). Closed by Enter, Esc or a click elsewhere that leaves the Detail open, the field returns focus to where it was opened from: the Comment button, or the graph for a click on the comment; a click on the Detail's Close or the graph's background puts the comment back and closes the Detail as well (J-DETAIL), the Comment button going with it. In the Source the comment is part of the file's text.
- **J-COMMENT-MOVE**: on a Current, a comment can be dragged by its text onto another Artifact, a node, a minimized whole or a whole's box: that Artifact is highlighted as the drop target unless it already has a comment. Released on the target, the comment leaves one file and enters the other in one edit and is drawn under its new node. Released on the background, on its own node or on an Artifact that has a comment, it returns and nothing is written (also Esc during the drag puts it back). On a Run's graph comments do not move and no field opens.

## Review

Review is where Human reviews are found and evaluated: its Pane holds a filter and the list of
reviews it matches; its main area shows one review, its evaluation space.

- **J-REVIEW-LIST**: Review's Pane is one column: a filter field at the top, its query as typed, and below it the reviews of every repository on this machine that the filter matches, those of removed branches included while their folder exists, grouped by project, newest first, each one line: its eval as `{Artifact}/{eval}`, how long it waits and, once claimed, who claimed it. Its starting filter is `type:human waiting`, the Human reviews waiting for a person. A review whose repository folder is deleted leaves the list on its own, unless another worktree still waits on the same review, which then lists it as its own. With none matching, the list says so. A click on a review shows it in the main area.
- **J-REVIEW-FILTER**: a click in the filter field, or the palette's "Filter the reviews", focuses it, showing Review and the Pane first when they are not shown. Terms must all match: `type:human` (also `type:agent`) keeps the requests of that kind, `waiting` those still waiting for a reviewer to claim them, free text matches part of an eval, a project or a branch, `repo:` and `branch:` part of that one field. The query is plain text, edited as typed, and the field suggests terms as the search does (J-PANE-SUGGEST); Esc puts back `type:human waiting`.
- **J-REVIEW-REF**: following a reference to a review (Open in Review in Verify, Open a request by ID, the launch's `review ID`, the pet overlay's ID) shows Review with that exact review open, whether or not the filter lists it, for example once it is claimed or submitted; the filter and the list stay as they were, and nothing more is said about it.
- **J-REVIEW-BACK**: a review's header starts, at its left, with its way back to Verify: `Verify · {Run} › {Artifact}`, the Run that asked for it and the Artifact it evaluates. A click on it shows Verify with that Run chosen and that Artifact's Detail open on the review's eval.
- **J-HUMAN-DRAFT**: the result being written for a review is that review's draft for this reviewer: its points, the places Point added (J-HUMAN-POINTS), the verdict chosen, and what is typed in each verdict's form. Artifactize keeps it: it stays as written whichever window or mode has focus, through mode switches, the pet overlay and an Artifact Viewer, and after the app restarts; it is kept when the claim is released and gone once the review is submitted, which submits the form's values alone. Review, the Detail's review, the pet overlay and Point all write the same draft, and a change made in one shows in the others; a point that arrives while a field is being typed in changes nothing typed.

## Human review

- **J-HUMAN-CLAIM**: the Claim to review button claims a waiting Human review for this reviewer (also `c`); the Release claim button releases it once nothing of the review is running, also while its form is being edited, the draft kept (also `u` outside a field). A claim held by another reviewer shows as taken, the claimant named beside its stage; one this reviewer made elsewhere (another window, the CLI) is theirs and shows as REVIEW (yours).
- **J-HUMAN-LAYOUT**: a Human review has one order: its instruction, the Tools, their Tool log, its Points once there is one (J-HUMAN-POINTS) and the Result. Nothing in it draws the eye away from the instruction, which reads first, then the evidence, then the verdict; a field shows its focus only while it is typed in. Its eval's title is said once, above it (Review's header row, or the Detail's title), and the review opens with its stage alone: CLAIM, or REVIEW (yours) once this reviewer holds the claim. The request's ID, its mode, its claimant and how long it has waited fold under Debug at its end, which a click on it shows and hides (also `t`). On a Human eval's tab in the Detail, and in a main area less than twice as wide as the Detail, it reads top to bottom in one column no wider than a readable measure. In Review's main area at least twice as wide as the Detail, its evaluation space, the review is at the left, its instruction, the Tools and, under them, the Tool log, folded to its last run until opened and then as tall as its lines up to the rest of the height, and the Points under it, and the **Evaluation** is at the right, of the Detail's width: the stage, then the Result, with Release claim and Submit along its bottom. Either way Release claim and Submit sit in one row of the Result's column, and between them what running work has to say: its seconds and Cancel, which cancels it, or its failure. While work runs, Release claim stays in its place, disabled; Submit stays usable while that work is a tool's run (J-HUMAN-GREEN) and is disabled while a claim, a release or a submission runs. What ended well is said by the Tool log's end line or by the completed result alone.
- **J-HUMAN-TOOL**: the Tools are grouped by the Artifact that declares them or whose type provides them, the reviewed one first, a group named by its Artifact only when more than one has tools, each a `▶` button; after a claim a click on it runs it at once. While any work of the review runs, a tool's run, a claim, a release or a submission, every tool button is disabled, so one tool runs at a time. A launch tool opens its application and returns at once; a viewer tool opens its Artifact Viewer (J-VIEWER) and returns at once; a failing tool shows its error.
- **J-HUMAN-LOG**: the Tool log opens folded to the last run: its start line, its last stderr line if any, and its end line with how many lines it printed, or its newest line while it runs; a click on the Tool log's header unfolds every run's lines, and another folds them. Unfolded, every run of the review shows in the Tool log as it happens: `▶ HH:MM:SS · operation · Artifact`, then each line the tool prints, stderr in red, then its end line: `■ exit N` (red unless 0), `■ done`, `■ launched` once a file is handed to its desktop application or an Artifact Viewer opens, or `■ error: …` in red when the tool could not run. A launched application's lines keep arriving under its own run until it exits. The log is monospace, wraps, scrolls on its own and follows its newest line unless the reader scrolled up; its text can be selected and copied into the result. A submission clears it.
- **J-HUMAN-POINTS**: the places Point added to the review (J-POINT) are its **Points**, listed in the review's left column under the Tool log, newest last, each its text, where it was pointed (the Artifact and its viewer) and when, **Insert**, which puts its text into the form's text field that was last focused, at its caret, or else into the form's Comment field, and a remove button that takes the point out of the draft; Insert is disabled while no form is open. The Points show once there is one. They are the reviewer's own aid: a submission keeps only the form's values, and the points go with the draft.
- **J-HUMAN-GREEN**: after a claim the GREEN button opens the GREEN form built from the eval's pass schema (also `g`); the Submit button submits it (also Ctrl+S), also while a tool of the review runs: the submission cancels that tool first. The completed result stays open.
- **J-HUMAN-RED**: the RED button opens the RED form (also `r`); a result that fails its schema is not submitted by the Submit button (also Ctrl+S): the failing path is shown and the form stays correctable. While a form is open, its GREEN and RED buttons switch the verdict, each verdict keeping what was typed into its form.
- **J-HUMAN-FORM**: the form shows text, number, true/false and choice fields, a list of text with `+ Add` and `✕` per item, and repeated groups of fields with `+ Add` for a group and `✕` per group, one group always left to type in. Constants are filled in and not shown; `*` marks a required field and an empty optional one says Optional in grey. There is no JSON editor.
- **J-HUMAN-TEXT**: text fields take any language, including Korean composed by an input method; no key typed during a composition, and no key without Ctrl (⌘) typed in a field, does anything but enter text.
- **J-HUMAN-ESC**: Cancel beside the running work cancels it. In the Detail, Close or a click on the graph's background first cancels the review's running work, then, with nothing running, leaves its form, its draft kept, and only then closes the Detail (J-DETAIL). (Also Esc, which takes the same steps and, for a review shown in Review, returns to Review's Pane as its last step, showing it first when it is hidden.)

## Artifact Viewer, Point and the pet overlay

An Artifact's type gives it the tools of the plugin that provides that type: `files` (File
Explorer), `markdown` (Markdown viewer) and `artifactize` (Artifactize Viewer) are built in,
and a project can add others, such as the `html` example. A plugin's Human tool opens its
viewer in an Artifact Viewer window of the app.

- **J-VIEWER**: a viewer tool, run from a review's Tools or an Artifact's Tools in the Detail (`▶`), opens an Artifact Viewer: a window of the app of its own, titled `{Artifact} — {viewer}`, showing that Artifact in its plugin's viewer, never the system's default application. Running it again while that Artifact's viewer is open brings that window to the front. Closing a viewer's window closes it alone. A Custom Artifact's `launch` and `output` tools work as before (J-HUMAN-TOOL).
- **J-VIEWER-FILES**: the File Explorer shows the Artifact's files as a tree at its left, folders folding and unfolding by a click, and a click on a file shows its text in the document view at its right, with line numbers, read-only.
- **J-VIEWER-MARKDOWN**: the Markdown viewer shows the Artifact's Markdown rendered, headings, lists, links, tables and code as they read; an Artifact of several Markdown files lists them at its left, a click showing one.
- **J-VIEWER-ARTIFACTIZE**: the Artifactize Viewer shows what an Artifact of type `artifactize` covers: the Artifacts declared under its path as nodes and the references between them as plain connections, with no verdicts (it shows what the project declares, not a Run). A click on an Artifact opens its panel at the right: its name and type, its evals, each with its kind, its title, its criteria and the Artifacts it reads. The graph pans and zooms as Verify's does.
- **J-VIEWER-PLUGIN**: a project's own viewer plugin (the `html` example included) shows its own page inside the Artifact Viewer; for `html`, the Artifact's page in an embedded browser that can be used as in a browser. A plugin's page reaches the Artifact's files and the active review only through the viewer's bridge, nothing else of the app. A viewer page asks the bridge for its context when it starts: until the page first reaches the bridge the strip says *Loading {plugin}…*, and a page that finished loading without reaching it is said, in the strip, not to have connected, until it does. Once connected, the strip shows the latest status line the page reports (for `html`, the page shown and its title once loaded, or why it failed), each replacing the last; the strip's Point tells the page to point (J-POINT).
- **J-POINT**: while this reviewer holds a claimed review, its active review (the one last shown in Review), every viewer that supports it shows **Point**. A click on Point, then a click on a place in the Artifact, adds that place as readable text to the review's Points (J-HUMAN-POINTS), with the Artifact and viewer it was pointed in: an HTML element as a CSS selector (`#checkout .total`), a Markdown position as its line and column (`L42:C9`), an Artifact or an eval in the Artifactize Viewer as its ID (`front-design`, `front-design/matches-layout`). Pointing never submits and never writes into the form. Esc, or Point again, stops pointing without adding anything. With no claimed review, Point is not shown.
- **J-PET**: while this reviewer holds a claimed review and works anywhere but its evaluation space (another mode, an Artifact Viewer, another application), a small pet overlay stays at the bottom right of the screen, above every window, tied to the active review: the pet, the review's eval and Run (a click on them shows the review in Review, J-REVIEW-REF), the same draft's points, compactly, each with Insert (J-HUMAN-POINTS), the GREEN and RED choice and the open form with its Comment field, **Point** (pointing in the Artifact Viewer of the review's Artifact that is open, and disabled, saying why, while none is), and **Submit**. Typing in it writes the draft, kept as focus moves between the overlay, a viewer and other windows. Submit submits the review as Review's does (J-HUMAN-GREEN, J-HUMAN-RED). The overlay goes when the review is submitted or released, and while the review's evaluation space is in front.

## Settings

- **J-SET-PROVIDERS**: the Providers tab lists the built-in providers vertically on the left (Codex, Claude Agent, Antigravity, OpenAI, Anthropic, Gemini), then the custom providers by name, then **+ Custom provider** at the bottom. A click on one shows its setup on the right.
- **J-SET-CODEX**: Codex signs in to or out of the ChatGPT account through the browser; a sign-in in progress can be cancelled, and when the browser cannot return, **Finish by hand** unfolds the sign-in's address and a field where the redirect URL can be entered, which **Finish sign-in** sends.
- **J-SET-CLAUDE-AGENT**: Claude Agent shows Claude Code's sign-in as Claude Code itself reports it: `claude` not found, not signed in, signed in with its login method and plan, or the error it failed with; Check again asks again. It signs in and out by running the user's own `claude auth login` and `claude auth logout`: Claude Code opens the browser and keeps its own login, which artifactize never reads, copies or stores. While it signs in, the sign-in can be cancelled, and **Finish by hand** unfolds the URL Claude Code prints and a field where the code its page gives can be entered, which **Finish sign-in** sends; the status updates when it ends.
- **J-SET-ANTIGRAVITY**: Antigravity runs Chat with the models of the person's Google account through their own Antigravity CLI (`agy`), and serves Chat only: an eval naming it in `backend` is a declaration error, and a custom provider cannot be an alias of it. It shows the CLI's sign-in as the CLI itself reports it: `agy` not found, too old (its version and the version it needs), not signed in, signed in with its Google account, or the error it failed with; Check again asks again. It signs in and out through the user's own `agy`: the CLI opens Google's sign-in in the browser and keeps its own login, which artifactize never reads, copies or stores. While it signs in, the sign-in can be cancelled, and **Finish by hand** unfolds the URL the CLI prints and a field where the code Google's page gives can be entered, which **Finish sign-in** sends; the status updates when it ends.
- **J-SET-KEY**: OpenAI, Anthropic and Gemini each show whether their key is set, from the environment (`OPENAI_API_KEY`, `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY`, `GEMINI_API_KEY`) or saved here, never its value. A key can be entered and saved, or removed; a set environment variable is used first.
- **J-SET-CUSTOM**: **+ Custom provider** adds a provider with a name: an alias of a built-in provider (any but Antigravity, J-SET-ANTIGRAVITY), used with its sign-in or key, or an endpoint with its API (OpenAI Responses, OpenAI Chat Completions, Anthropic Messages or Gemini), base URL and optional key. Any number can be added; a custom provider can be changed or removed. An eval uses it by its name in `backend`.
- **J-SET-MODELS**: a built-in or saved custom provider's setup has Models: its List models button asks the provider what it offers and lists each model by its name with its ID beside it, says `No models listed.` when it offers none, or says why it failed. Antigravity lists the models its signed-in Google account offers; while it is not signed in, List models says so. The button is disabled while the list is read, and choosing another provider empties the list until List models is used again.
- **J-SET-REMOTE**: the Remote tab signs in to a shared review store with its URL, share level and token, shows its status in one line (signed in or out, the reviewer with the token's scopes and what was pushed today, or the error), pushes results (or a dry run), and signs out. Signed in, it shows the store's URL and share level as facts, without the fields and the button that sign in, which come back once signed out. While a sign-in, a sign-out or a push runs, the tab's buttons are disabled.
- **J-SET-REMOTE-STATUS**: on the Remote tab, Check status, beside Sign in to the store while signed out and first of the buttons while signed in, reads the store's status again into the status line. A push's or a dry run's result, what it pushed or would push, what the store already had and what it skipped, takes the status line until Check status reads the status again, and what was pushed today follows a push; an error of the store, a push or a check takes the line in red.
- **J-SET-SIGNOFF-GRACE**: the Limits tab's Sign-off idle grace says in one line what it is, how long a Run with only sign-offs left, none of them claimed, waits before it ends; its seconds field holds the grace in force (120 seconds unless changed). Save, usable once the field holds another value, changes it for the Runs started afterwards, the field then holding the new grace; only a failure to save is said.
