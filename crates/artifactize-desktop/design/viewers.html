Viewers: Artifact Viewer windows and the pet overlay

A viewer tool opens an Artifact Viewer, a window of the app of its own, titled with the Artifact and its viewer. The app's strip runs along its top, the same for every viewer: the Artifact, the viewer, the active review and Point; under it the viewer itself, one of the built-in File Explorer, Markdown viewer and Artifactize Viewer, or a project's own plugin's page. While a claimed review is worked on outside Review, the pet overlay stays at the screen's bottom right above every window, holding the same draft. The figures draw each window over a dark backdrop that stands for the rest of the screen.

Rules

D-VIEW-1
An Artifact Viewer is a window of the app of its own, with the system's frame, titled {Artifact} — {viewer}, opening at a size of its own over the window it was opened from, on --surface-app. Along its top runs the app's strip, the same for every viewer: one row of --row-head on --surface-panel, closed below by --line-panel: the Artifact's name semibold, the viewer's name in --ink-2, and at the right, while this reviewer holds a claimed review, the active review's eval as {Artifact}/{eval} in --mono --ink-2 led by the human icon in --human, then Point (D-VIEW-2). Everything under the strip is the viewer's. a second title inside the window; the app's bottom bar or modes in a viewer; the viewer's own controls in the app's strip; a viewer drawn inside the main window.
D-VIEW-2
Point is a small button with the point icon at the strip's right end, shown only where the viewer supports pointing and while this reviewer holds the active review. A click starts pointing: the button reads as on (a 1px --accent outline inside its --accent border), the pointer is a crosshair over the viewer, and what is under it (an element, a line, a node or an eval) is outlined 2px in --accent; a click appends its text to the draft's Notes on a line of its own and ends pointing, and Esc or Point again ends it without appending. Nothing says the text was appended but the Notes themselves, where the pointed text stands in --mono on --accent-soft until the Notes are next edited (D-REV-4). Point shown without a claimed review; a toast or a sentence for an appended reference; an outline in a status colour; a pointer that submits.
D-VIEW-3
The File Explorer is two panels that meet at --line-panel: at the left the tree, about 240px wide, on --surface-panel, its rows --row-dense at --text-small, each indented 14px per depth, a folder led by its fold or unfold icon and the folder icon, a file by the file icon, all in --ink-2, its name in --ink, the shown file on --selection; at the right the document view on --surface-input: a head row of --row-head on --surface-panel with the file's path in --mono, closed below by --line-group, then the file's text in --mono at --text-small with line numbers in --ink-3, as the Source draws it (D-INS-11), wrapping within its width and read-only. an editable document view; syntax colours taken from a status; a tree that widens the window.
D-VIEW-4
The Markdown viewer shows the rendered document on --surface-panel in a column of --measure, padded 16px by 24px: its first heading at --text-heading bold over a --line-group line, its second at --text-title semibold, deeper ones at --text-body semibold, text at --text-body in --ink, links in --accent, inline code and code blocks in --mono on --surface-input, tables with --line-group rules. An Artifact of several Markdown files lists them at the left as the File Explorer's tree does (D-VIEW-3); one file takes the whole window. While pointing, the line under the pointer is outlined in --accent. the raw Markdown shown as the document; font sizes outside the tokens; a page wider than the measure.
D-VIEW-5
The Artifactize Viewer draws what the project declares, not a Run: a canvas as a graph's (D-GRAPH-1, D-GRAPH-6) with a node per declared Artifact, 168 × 36px, a --surface-panel face, a --line-node outline, its name in --ink and its type as a small tag (--mono at --text-label in --ink-2 inside a --line-group border of --radius-chip; an untyped Artifact's tag reads custom), with no status icon, strip or tally; references as edges in the neutral --edge, without ports or colour. A click on a node gives it the selected outline (D-NODE-2) and opens its panel, docked at the right as the Detail is (D-INS-1): its name and type in the header row, then each eval in a section opened by --line-group: its name in --mono with its kind as a tag (agent, human, runtime), then Title, Criteria (its instruction, whole) and Reads (the Artifacts it reads, as tags), each label in --ink-2 at --text-small. While pointing, the node or the eval under the pointer is outlined in --accent. verdict colours, icons or tallies in the Artifactize Viewer; an edge drawn as an eval; a connection that cannot tell which Artifact reads which.
D-VIEW-6
A project's own viewer plugin fills everything under the strip with its page, which the app does not restyle; the html example shows the Artifact's page in an embedded browser. Point stands in the strip as for every viewer (D-VIEW-2) and tells the page to point; what the page shows while pointing is its own, held to the same outline: what is under the pointer outlined 2px in --accent. While the plugin's page has not yet reached the viewer's bridge (a page asks it for its context when it starts), the strip says, after the viewer's name, Loading {plugin}… in --ink-2; a page that finished loading without reaching it says there, in --red, that it did not connect; the line goes once the page reaches the bridge, and from then on the same place holds the latest status line the page reports (for html, the page shown and its title once loaded, or why it failed), in --ink-2, each replacing the last. the app's controls drawn over a plugin's page; a plugin page that reaches the app's window or its state.
D-PET-1
The pet overlay is a small window of its own, without a frame, above every window, 16px from the bottom right of the screen's work area: a card 320px wide on --surface-raised, a --line-panel border of --radius-chip, the faint shadow (D-SYS-2), padded 12px. The pet sits on its top edge at the left, 56px, half over the card; beside it the head row in --mono at --text-small: the human icon in --human, the active review's eval, then · {Run} in --ink-2, the whole row a link to the review in Review. Under it the review's Result as the review's column draws it (D-REV-4): Notes, the GREEN / RED choice, then the chosen verdict's form, Notes and form scrolling inside the card past 280px; then one row: Point at the left (a small button, D-VIEW-2, disabled and followed by why in --ink-2 while no viewer of the review's Artifact is open), and Submit, the one primary button naming its verdict, at the right once a form is open. A failure to submit shows in --red above that row, the form kept. a second draft, or a free text box apart from the Notes and the form; Release claim in the overlay; the overlay over the review's evaluation space; a card that grows taller than the screen's lower half; a frame or title bar.
D-PET-2
The pet is the app's own drawing, an image asset: the brand icon's round faceted emerald in its ivory and silver bezel, with eyes, cheeks, two feet and a sprout. It is the one picture of the app whose colours are its own and not tokens, like the brand mark; it stands still and carries no status. a pet in a status colour; an animated pet that draws the eye while a person types; a third-party character.
