Viewers: Artifact Viewer windows and the pet overlay

A viewer tool opens an Artifact Viewer, a window of the app of its own, titled with the Artifact and its viewer. The app's strip runs along its top, the same for every viewer: the Artifact, the viewer, the active review and Point; under it the viewer itself, one of the built-in File Explorer, Markdown viewer and Artifactize Viewer, or a project's own plugin's page. While a claimed review is worked on outside Review, the pet overlay stays at the screen's bottom right above every window, holding the same draft. The figures draw each window over a dark backdrop that stands for the rest of the screen.

Rules

D-VIEW-1
An Artifact Viewer is a window of the app of its own, with the system's frame, titled {Artifact} — {viewer}, opening at a size of its own over the window it was opened from, on --surface-app. Along its top runs the app's strip, the same for every viewer: one row of --row-head on --surface-panel, closed below by --line-panel: the Artifact's name semibold, the viewer's name in --ink-2, and at the right, while this reviewer holds a claimed review, the active review's eval as {Artifact}/{eval} in --mono --ink-2 led by the human icon in --human, then Point (D-VIEW-2). Everything under the strip is the viewer's. a second title inside the window; the app's bottom bar or modes in a viewer; the viewer's own controls in the app's strip; a viewer drawn inside the main window.
D-VIEW-2
Point is a small button with the point icon at the strip's right end, shown only where the viewer supports pointing and while this reviewer holds the active review. A click starts pointing: the button reads as on as a panel toggle does: its icon and word --ink-bright where they were --ink-2, its face and border unchanged (a ring shows only for keyboard focus), the pointer is a crosshair over the viewer, and what is under it (an element, a line, a node or an eval) is outlined by a 2px dashed --handle line over a --handle-edge edge, as a viewer's handles are drawn on the object, which reads on any page and is never a solid ring, so it is told apart from keyboard focus; a click adds its text to the review's Points (D-REV-9) and ends pointing, and Esc or Point again ends it without adding anything. Nothing says the point was added but the Points themselves, where the new one stands on --accent-soft until the next (D-REV-9). Point shown without a claimed review; a toast or a sentence for an added point; an outline in a status colour; a pointer that submits or writes into the form.
D-VIEW-3
The File Explorer is two panels that meet at --line-panel: at the left the tree, about 240px wide, on --surface-panel, its rows --row-dense at --text-small, each indented 14px per depth, a folder led by its fold or unfold icon and the folder icon, a file by the file icon, all in --ink-2, its name in --ink-2, the shown file the darker --selection row with its name in --ink; at the right the document view on --surface-input: a head row of --row-head on --surface-panel with the file's path in --mono, closed below by --line-group, then the file's text in --mono at --text-small with line numbers in --ink-2, as the Source draws it (D-INS-11), wrapping within its width and read-only. an editable document view; syntax colours taken from a status; a tree that widens the window.
D-VIEW-4
The Markdown viewer shows the rendered document on --surface-panel in a column of --measure, padded 16px by 24px: its first heading at --text-heading bold over a --line-group line, its second at --text-title semibold, deeper ones at --text-body semibold, text at --text-body in --ink, links in --accent, inline code and code blocks in --mono on --surface-input, tables with --line-group rules. An Artifact of several Markdown files lists them at the left as the File Explorer's tree does (D-VIEW-3); one file takes the whole window. While pointing, the line under the pointer is outlined by a 2px dashed --handle line over its --handle-edge edge. the raw Markdown shown as the document; font sizes outside the tokens; a page wider than the measure.
D-VIEW-5
The Artifactize Viewer draws what the project declares, not a Run: a canvas as a graph's (D-GRAPH-1, D-GRAPH-6) with a node per declared Artifact, 168 × 36px, a --surface-panel face, a --line-node outline, its name in --ink and the toolsets it composes as a small tag, as its code writes them (files & html; --mono at --text-label in --ink-2 inside a --line-group border of --radius-chip; an Artifact that composes none has no tag), with no status icon, strip or tally; references as edges in the neutral --edge, without ports or colour. A click on a node gives it the selected outline (D-NODE-2) and opens its panel, docked at the right as the Detail is (D-INS-1): its name and its toolsets in the header row, then each eval in a section opened by --line-group: its name in --mono with its kind as a tag (agent, human, runtime), then Title, Criteria (its instruction, whole) and Reads (the Artifacts it reads, as tags), each label in --ink-2 at --text-small. While pointing, the node or the eval under the pointer is outlined by a 2px dashed --handle line over its --handle-edge edge. Above the canvas, one row of a graph's tools as the stage header draws them (D-MAIN-1, D-GRAPH-4), borderless: Zoom out, the zoom in percent, Zoom in and Fit, without Find; it pans and zooms as Verify's graph does. verdict colours, icons or tallies in the Artifactize Viewer; zoom controls floating on its canvas; an edge drawn as an eval; a connection that cannot tell which Artifact reads which.
D-VIEW-6
A project's own viewer plugin fills everything under the strip with its page, which the app does not restyle; the html example shows the Artifact's page in an embedded browser. Point stands in the strip as for every viewer (D-VIEW-2) and tells the page to point; what the page shows while pointing is its own, held to the same outline: what is under the pointer outlined by a 2px dashed --handle line over its --handle-edge edge. While the plugin's page has not yet reached the viewer's bridge (a page asks it for its context when it starts), the strip says, after the viewer's name, Loading {plugin}… in --ink-2; a page that finished loading without reaching it says there, in --red, that it did not connect; the line goes once the page reaches the bridge, and from then on the same place holds the latest status line the page reports (for html, the page shown and its title once loaded, or why it failed), in --ink-2, each replacing the last. the app's controls drawn over a plugin's page; a plugin page that reaches the app's window or its state.
D-PET-1
The pet overlay is a small window of its own, without a frame, above every window, 16px from the bottom right of the screen's work area: a card 320px wide on --surface-raised, a --line-panel border of --radius-chip, the faint shadow (D-SYS-2), padded 12px. The pet sits on its top edge at the left, 40px, partly over the card, smaller than the card's words are loud; beside it the head row in --mono at --text-small: the human icon in --human, the active review's eval, then · {Run} in --ink-2; the pet and the whole head row are a link to the review in Review (J-PET). Under it the review's draft, compact, each part once there is something in it: the Points, one --row row each, the point icon, the text in --mono cut with an ellipsis and Insert at the right, which puts the text into the overlay's issue field (D-REV-9); then the issues, each a one-line bubble as the review's are (D-REV-10) at --text-small, cut with an ellipsis, Remove at its left; Points and issues each at most three rows tall, scrolling past that. Then the issues row, as the review's issues box (D-REV-5): the issue field, a one-line --control field on --surface-input taking the row's width, its placeholder Enter adds the issue in --ink-2, then Add, a small secondary button disabled while the field is empty, and Point at the row's right (a small button, D-VIEW-2; while no viewer of the review's Artifact is open it is unavailable, drawn with no face, a --line-section edge and --ink-3, apart from Point off, which keeps the button face in --ink-2, with why on its own line under the field in --ink-2 at --text-small). Last, apart from it, the decision row, drawn as the review's decision bar (D-REV-12) without the claim: the GREEN / RED choice as tabs at the left and Submit, the one primary button naming its verdict, at the right, disabled with why on its own line above the row in --ink-2 at --text-small whenever Review's would be; while a submission runs, its seconds and Cancel in Submit's place. A failure to submit shows in --red in that line, the draft kept. a second draft, or a free text box apart from the issue field; the verdict or Submit in the issues row; a verdict mark on an issue; where and when a point was made, which the review's Points say; an issue edited in the overlay; Release claim in the overlay; the overlay over the review's evaluation space; a card that grows taller than the screen's lower half; a frame or title bar.
D-PET-2
The pet is the app's own drawing, an image asset (pet.svg, the same file as the app's): the brand icon's round faceted emerald in its ivory and silver bezel, with eyes, cheeks, two feet and a sprout. It is the one picture of the app whose colours are its own and not tokens, like the brand mark, drawn at 60% of its saturation so the review's name and what is being done read first; it stands still and carries no status. a pet in a status colour; a pet that outweighs the card's words; an animated pet that draws the eye while a person types; a third-party character.
