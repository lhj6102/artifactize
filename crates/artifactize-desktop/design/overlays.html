Overlays: the palette, Help, dialogs and the find field

What opens over the screen: the command palette (the bar's brand, or Ctrl+K), Help (the palette's Keyboard shortcuts), Open a request by ID, the Leave dialog (quit while claims are held or a Chat turn runs), the Update dialog (from the release notice in Settings' header), and the graph's find field (the stage header's Find), which opens in the find icon's place with its list over the canvas's top right.

Rules

D-OVL-1
A dialog stays inside the work area, between the window's top and the bottom bar: taller than the room, its content scrolls under its title and above its actions, which stay in view, a 4px --line-input thumb showing there is more. It is a --surface-raised panel with a 1px --line-panel border, --radius-chip corners, 16px padding and a shadow no stronger than 8px offset, 24px blur in --scrim, over a --scrim overlay; its title is --text-title semibold; its actions are right-aligned 24px buttons, at most one primary. A dialog's buttons carry no key cap (D-SYS-12), but for the Leave dialog's two, which may show their keys as hints at the end of their labels. a 12px radius; a borderless card floating on a blur; a key cap on any other dialog's button.
D-OVL-2
The command palette, which a click on the bar's brand opens (D-BAR-1), is 640px wide near the top: its input a field as Find's is, --surface-input inside a 1px --line-input edge with the 1px --current-mark editing outline while typed in, group headings at --text-label uppercase in --ink-2, its list bounded above the bottom bar and scrolling on its own, a 4px --line-input thumb at its right showing how much is out of view; items of --row with the label left in --ink-2 and its key cap, if it has one, right, the selected item the darker --selection row with its label in --ink; typing filters the rows: only the commands whose names contain the typed text stay, in their order, each group heading kept only while one of its commands does (typing rel here would leave Release the claim alone, under Human review), and the selected row among them on --selection. It lists every command that is enabled now, each with its key cap where the app offers a key (J-PALETTE): the app's first, under App, then those of what is shown, each under its group (Graph, Human review, and Settings while Settings is open); the Graph group holds a graph's commands that can run now, Minimize or expand the whole among them while a whole's Detail is open (J-GRAPH-MINIMIZE); while a graph's Detail shows a Human review on a Human eval's tab, that review's commands are listed too, under the review's group, as they are for a review in the Main area. icons per command; the selection shown by colour alone; a command that opens the palette itself.
D-OVL-3
A key cap is the one kbd style: --mono at --text-label, --ink-2, a 1px --line-input border, --radius-control, --surface-group. It shows where keys are looked up, in the palette and in Help, and on the Leave dialog's two buttons (D-OVL-1); nowhere else, other dialogs' buttons included (D-SYS-12). On the primary button, the secondary face with its --line-primary outline, the key cap is drawn as on any button. a second kbd style; a key cap on any control other than the Leave dialog's two buttons.
D-OVL-4
The find field is opened by the stage header's Find (D-GRAPH-4, J-GRAPH-FIND): the find icon turns into the field in its own place, up to 240px wide, the tools left of it moving left, a --mono input on --surface-input inside --line-input with the editing outline (D-SYS-13), empty and focused when it opens; its list drops under it, its left edge on the field's, 4px under the stage header, 240px to 380px wide and never past the work area's right edge less --pad, raised (D-SYS-2) on --surface-raised with a --line-panel border: result rows of --row-dense in --mono at --text-small led by the verdict's icon in its colour (on a Current, what verify would do), each showing the whole name, wrapping within the field's width rather than cut with an ellipsis, the chosen row the darker --selection row with its name in --ink, the others in --ink-2, a hovered row on --surface-group. It has four states. Nothing typed, with starred Artifacts on the graph: those Artifacts as rows, as if found, in the graph's order. Text typed with matches: the Artifacts and evals whose names contain the text, ignoring case, as rows in the graph's order, the list no taller than the canvas and scrolling inside itself, so all 50 listed rows and the line after them stay reachable, an eval as {Artifact} / {eval}, and past the first 50 one --ink-2 line under them saying how many more match. Nothing typed and nothing starred: no rows, one --ink-2 line saying how to star one. Text typed and nothing matching: no rows, one --ink-2 line saying that none does. Typing moves nothing on the graph. No row shows as chosen when the list appears. A click on a row gives the find icon back, opens and focuses the Detail on that Artifact's tab or that eval's tab, and moves the camera so the Artifact sits in the middle of the graph left of the Detail, zoomed in only as far as its name needs to be readable (J-GRAPH-FIND). A part of several wholes is listed once, and the camera goes to its copy in the first of its wholes. An Artifact hidden in a minimized whole, or an eval of one, is listed as any other; chosen, the Detail opens on that Artifact or eval, and the camera centres the minimized whole that hides it, which takes the selection's outline (J-GRAPH-MINIMIZE), the whole staying minimized. A click outside, or Esc, gives the find icon back and does nothing else: the click neither acts on the graph nor opens, switches or closes the Detail, and the graph, its camera and the Detail stay as they were. a find field floating over the canvas; a find bar under the stage header; a modal find; a camera that moves while typing or choosing a row; an empty list with no line under it; the starred list and the how-to-star line together; a no-match line that reads like the how-to-star line.
D-OVL-6
Open a request by ID (from the palette) is a dialog of D-OVL-1 at most 480px wide: its title, one --surface-input text field taking the request ID with req-… in --ink-2 as its placeholder, and the actions Cancel and the primary Open, without key caps; what opens is shown in Review as a reference opens it (D-REV-8): a waiting Human review, or one this reviewer holds, actionable (D-REV-1, D-REV-5), its state Yours for the one held; a Human review another reviewer holds as that review, read-only, Taken · {claimant} in its head line (D-REV-11), as any other reference opens it; a submitted Human review as that review with its completed result; any other request as the Request view, read-only (D-REV-6). a list of recent IDs; a second field.
D-OVL-5
Help lists the keys the app has for what has focus (a Pane, a Chat, a graph with its Detail, a review, Settings) as a key cap / action grid under an 11px group label, each key cap beside what it does, and Close as its one action (J-HELP); which keys there are is the app's to choose, and a Run's Help lists none that only a Current has. For a graph it adds the graph's marks under their own label, each status's icon in its colour beside its word (a Run's: GREEN, running or waiting for a Human (on a node, one mark for both), waiting, RED, ERROR, blocked by upstream, and the Human icon that the Run's Detail and a Human eval's chip and row use for a sign-off that waits; a Current's: reused, would run, waiting, blocked, checking), the one place that spells them out; the Leave dialog says which claims are held and offers Stay and Release and quit (D-OVL-1); the Update dialog, opened from the release notice in Settings' header (D-SET-1), shows the running and the new version as two numbers, then the one way to update that fits how the app was installed: a command to run, as selectable --mono text on --surface-input with a --line-input border and no link; a file to download, as a --accent link that opens in the browser, followed by the command that installs it by hand when there is one; or, when the way is unknown, the release page as a link; Close closes it. It never downloads or installs anything itself. a Current's marks in a Run's Help; a Help page outside the app; a legend on a working screen; an update that starts on its own; a link and a command for the same thing; an Install or Update button.
D-OVL-8
The Trust workspace dialog is a dialog of D-OVL-1 at most 480px wide opened before an ACP agent starts in an untrusted worktree or after its executable digest changes (J-CHAT-SEND): its title, a key/value fact grid naming the agent, the trust scope (explicitly stating when a Git repository scope covers all branches and worktrees of that repository), the resolved executable path and its SHA-256 fingerprint in --mono, and one quiet sentence explaining that the agent runs natively in the worktree and may read repository settings and hooks before any permission prompt; then Cancel and the primary Trust and continue, without key caps. Registry installation consent uses the same dialog anatomy, naming the complete pinned specification (registry ID, exact version, platform, distribution kind, package or URL, arguments, environment, artifact SHA-256, and optional build-script consent) before Cancel and the primary Install. hiding the executable SHA-256 or GitRepo scope disclosure; spawning the ACP agent before explicit confirmation.
