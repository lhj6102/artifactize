Overlays: the palette, Help, dialogs and the find field

What opens over the screen and closes with Esc: the command palette (Ctrl+K), Help (?), Open a request by ID (Ctrl+O), the Leave dialog (quit while claims are held), the Update dialog (from the bar's release notice), and the graph's find field (Ctrl+F), which floats over the graph's top left rather than over the screen, and, on a Current, the New Artifact, Rename and Delete dialogs.

Rules

D-OVL-1
A dialog is a --surface-raised panel with a 1px --line-panel border, --radius-chip corners, 16px padding and a shadow no stronger than 8px offset, 24px blur in --scrim, over a --scrim overlay; its title is --text-title semibold; its actions are right-aligned 24px buttons, at most one primary. The keys the journeys give a dialog work without key caps: Esc for Stay, Cancel and Close, Enter for Open by ID's Open, and u for the Leave dialog's Release and quit; a dialog's buttons carry no key cap (D-SYS-12). a 12px radius; a borderless card floating on a blur; a key cap on a dialog's button.
D-OVL-2
The command palette is 640px wide near the top: an input of 36px closed below by --line-group, group headings at --text-label uppercase in --ink-2, items of --row with the label left and the key cap right, the selected item on --selection; Typing filters the rows: only the commands whose names contain the typed text stay, in their order, each group heading kept only while one of its commands does (typing rel here would leave Release the claim alone, under Review), and the selected row among them on --selection; Enter runs the selected command, Esc closes. It lists every command that is enabled now, each with the key that does the same: the app's first (Keyboard shortcuts, Show Settings, Show or hide the Pane, Search the branches, Open a request by ID, Refresh, Quit), then those of what the Main area shows, a graph's (Find, Minimize or expand the whole) or a review's (Claim to review, Open the GREEN or the RED form, Choose GREEN or RED while editing, Submit, Release the claim, Show or hide Debug); while a graph's Detail shows a Human review on a Human eval's tab, the palette lists that review's commands that can run now too, under the review's group, as it does for a review in the Main area; Show or hide Debug is listed while the Detail shows an Artifact's tab, an eval's Summary or a Human review, and at no other time (not on an eval's log, a Run's Detail or the bare graph), and always once: under the review's group while the Detail shows a Human review, else under the graph's. Every key a working screen does not show is named here or in Help. icons per command; the selection shown by colour alone; a command that opens the palette itself.
D-OVL-3
A key cap is the one kbd style: --mono at --text-label, --ink-2, a 1px --line-input border, --radius-control, --surface-group. It shows where keys are looked up, in the palette and in Help, and on the bar's Commands button, which opens the palette; nowhere else, a dialog's buttons included (D-SYS-12). a second kbd style; a key cap on any control other than Commands, a dialog's button included.
D-OVL-4
The find field is opened by the tool strip's Find button (D-GRAPH-4), or by Ctrl+F only while a Run's graph or a Current is shown and focus is outside the Pane and outside fields (on the graph or in the Detail); Ctrl+F with focus elsewhere in the Pane or the Main area (in the Pane, in one of their fields, or with no graph shown) focuses the Pane's Search instead, showing a hidden Pane first (D-PANE-1). While a dialog, the palette, Help or Settings is open over the screen, Ctrl+F does neither. It floats over the canvas's top left (8px in, under the strip), 380px wide on --surface-raised with a --line-panel border: a --mono input, empty and focused when it opens, then result rows of --row-dense in --mono at --text-small led by the verdict's icon in its colour (on a Current, what verify would do), each showing the whole name, wrapping within the field's width rather than cut with an ellipsis, the chosen row on --selection with a 2px inset bar, a hovered row on --surface-group. It has four states. Nothing typed, with starred Artifacts on the graph: those Artifacts as rows, as if found, in the graph's order. Text typed with matches: the Artifacts and evals whose names contain the text, ignoring case, as rows in the graph's order, the list no taller than the canvas below the field and scrolling inside the field, so all 50 listed rows and the line after them stay reachable, an eval as {Artifact} / {eval}, and past the first 50 one --ink-2 line under them saying how many more match. Nothing typed and nothing starred: no rows, one --ink-2 line saying how to star one. Text typed and nothing matching: no rows, one --ink-2 line saying that none does. Typing and moving through the rows move nothing on the graph. No row is chosen until ↓ or ↑ chooses one. Enter on the chosen row, or on the first row when none is chosen (a found entry or a favorite alike), or a click on a row, closes the field, opens and focuses the Detail on that Artifact's tab or that eval's tab, and moves the camera so the Artifact sits in the middle of the graph left of the Detail, zoomed in only as far as its name needs to be readable (E-GRAPH-FIND-GO). A part of several wholes is listed once, and the camera goes to its copy in the first of its wholes. An Artifact hidden in a minimized whole, or an eval of one, is listed as any other; chosen, the Detail opens on that Artifact or eval, and the camera centres the minimized whole that hides it, which takes the selection's outline (E-GRAPH-MINIMIZED-FIND), the whole staying minimized. Esc or a click outside closes the field and does nothing else: the click outside neither acts on the graph nor opens, switches or closes the Detail, and the graph, its camera and the Detail stay as they were; after Esc, focus returns to where it was before the field opened (E-GRAPH-FIND-CLOSE). a modal find; a camera that moves while typing or choosing a row; an empty list with no line under it; the starred list and the how-to-star line together; a no-match line that reads like the how-to-star line.
D-OVL-6
Open a request by ID (Ctrl+O or the palette) is a dialog of D-OVL-1 at most 480px wide: its title, one --surface-input text field taking the request ID with req-… in --ink-3 as its placeholder, and the actions Cancel and the primary Open (Esc and Enter, without key caps); what opens is in the Main area: a waiting Human review as the Review screen, actionable (D-REV-1); any other request as the Request view, read-only (D-REV-6), which is not a Human review and offers none of its actions. a list of recent IDs; a second field.
D-OVL-5
Help lists the keys of what has focus (the Pane, a graph with its Detail, a review, Settings) as a key cap / action grid under an 11px group label; a graph's keys are those the journeys give it and its Detail: Tab to the next Artifact and, after a whole, to its Minimize or Expand button; Enter to open its Detail; in the Detail, Enter or Space on a focused section header to fold or unfold it; on a Current, in the Definition, Enter to write a changed Path and Enter in the Parts field to add the chosen part, both listed only in a Current's Help; Ctrl+F to find; m to minimize or expand the whole that is focused or whose button is focused, else the whole whose Detail is open, only with focus outside the Pane and outside a field typed in and never during a composition (D-SYS-12); t to show or hide Debug, only while the Detail shows an Artifact's tab, an eval's Summary or a Human review (not on an eval's log, a Run's Detail or the bare graph), with focus outside a field typed in and never during a composition (D-SYS-12); Esc, in this order: to close an open find field; on a Current, to put back what is being dragged, a Path being changed, or close the Parts field or an open comment field, nothing written; to cancel a Human review's running work in the Detail, then to leave its form with the draft kept; then to close the Detail; and, with no Detail open, to return focus to the Pane, which only Esc does. Close and a click on the graph's background are not keys and are not listed: they take the review's two steps as Esc does and then close the Detail. ← closes the Detail too, except while a review in it runs work or edits its form, or a Path or a new part is being typed, when it does nothing. A Run's Help lists only what a Run's graph has: no drag, Path, Parts, comment or Source keys; a Current's Help, and only it, adds Esc to put back what is being dragged, Ctrl+S in a Source to save it, and the comment field's keys: Enter writes the comment, or removes it when the field is empty; Shift+Enter breaks the line; Esc closes the field, nothing written, before it can close the Detail. A Run's graph has none of these. For a graph it adds the graph's marks under their own label, each status's icon in its colour beside its word (a Run's: GREEN, running or waiting for a Human (on a node, one mark for both), waiting, RED, ERROR, blocked by upstream, and the Human icon that the Run's headline and a Human eval's chip and row use for a sign-off that waits; a Current's: reused, would run, waiting, blocked, checking), the one place that spells them out; the Leave dialog says which claims are held and offers Stay (Esc) and Release and quit (u), its buttons without key caps; the Update dialog, opened from the bar's release notice (D-BAR-1), shows the running and the new version as two numbers, then the one way to update that fits how the app was installed: a command to run, as selectable --mono text on --surface-input with a --line-input border and no link; a file to download, as a --accent link that opens in the browser, followed by the command that installs it by hand when there is one; or, when the way is unknown, the release page as a link; Close, or Esc, closes it. It never downloads or installs anything itself. a Close button on Help, which Esc closes; a Current's editing keys in a Run's Help; a Help page outside the app; a legend on a working screen; an update that starts on its own; a link and a command for the same thing; an Install or Update button; t listed as a key of the bare graph.
D-OVL-7
The edit dialogs of a Current are dialogs of D-OVL-1 at most 400px wide, opened by the tool strip's New Artifact (D-CUR-3) and the Definition's Rename and Delete (D-INS-10). New Artifact: two fields under their names, Name (focused) and Path in --mono, then Cancel and the primary Create. A Create that succeeds writes .artifactize/{name}.artf with that path and closes the dialog; the new node is drawn on the Current, in free space when the top level is placed by hand, its Detail opens on its Artifact tab, and the camera moves to it as to a found Artifact, centred on the graph left of the Detail (D-OVL-4, E-GRAPH-FIND-GO). Rename: titled Rename {name}, one field holding the name, then Cancel and the primary Rename. Delete: titled Delete {name}?, one sentence naming the file it removes, then Cancel and the primary Delete. Enter creates or renames and Esc cancels, without key caps; Delete is done only by its button. A refusal shows in --red at --text-small under the field it concerns, or under Delete's sentence, and the dialog stays with what was typed: a name already used or not valid, or a path not valid, says why; Delete refused while other Artifacts name it says that the file was not removed and which Artifacts name it; and a Rename or Delete whose file (the Artifact's own, or a whole's or a referrer's that the edit would change) changed on disk since the Current read it names that file, {file} changed on disk; nothing written, without a line or column, and the Current is scanned again. a folder or kind chooser in New Artifact; a sentence that explains what Rename changes; Delete in red; a refusal that closes the dialog.
