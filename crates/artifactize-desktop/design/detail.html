Detail: the inspector of the selected Artifact

A click on a node opens its Detail: a panel docked at the graph's right edge, meeting the graph at a line, with the graph still visible on its left. The selection drives it: the outlined node and the Detail's title name the same Artifact. The Artifact's tab lists its evals, and a click on one opens that eval's tab, where chips read as a path back; the first figure shows the Artifact's tab, the second an eval's Summary. On a Current the Artifact's tab also edits its definition: the last figure shows its Definition and its Source.

  1. 1
    SelectionThe opened node keeps its face and its strip; its own outline turns neutral-bright and 2px. It is the Artifact the Detail is titled with.
  2. 2
    Dock headerThe title (the whole name, wrapping), the star beside it, Close. The 2px accent line at the top says the Detail has focus. The Detail starts under the graph's tool strip, which stays in reach. The outlined node and this title are the two places that name the Artifact.
  3. 4
    NeighboursFolded until asked for: the graph beside the Detail already shows them. Unfolded, it draws the Artifact between its neighbours with the graph's nodes and edges: the Artifacts its evals use on its left, those whose evals use it on its right; a click on a neighbour opens it.
  4. 5
    Eval rowsThe tab's navigation: a click on a row opens that eval's tab. Dense rows of four columns: the verdict's icon, the name, the status words in the status colour, and time and tokens pinned to the panel's right edge in secondary ink with tabular figures.
  5. 6
    SectionsEach a header row: the fold icon and the section's name in ink, nothing else; a click folds it to that row, and what it holds shows once it is unfolded. A section with nothing to show is not shown: the Tool log joins them with the first run of a tool.
  6. 20
    Navigator beside the DetailPart of the graph (here docs and site, and part of cli) lies behind the Detail, outside the view, so the Navigator shows at the bottom right of the graph's visible part, left of the Detail's edge, never over it; it goes once the whole graph is in view.
  1. 21
    Drawn inOnly for a part of several wholes: a fact under Debug naming every one of those wholes, in graph order, also while a minimized whole hides one of its copies and only one copy is drawn; every drawn copy of it carries the selection's outline. A part of one whole, or of none, has no such fact.
  2. 22
    PathOn a Run, read-only facts in secondary ink, selectable: Target, what the Artifact's PATH names, as its PATH writes it, in monospace (for a folder, a file, an aspect or a graph, its path in the worktree; here a Live Artifact, the address outside the worktree that its PATH names), and, for a Live Artifact only, Address, below it, the address it is observed at, in monospace. Nothing here edits it.
  1. 25
    A tool runningA click on a tool's run icon runs it at once in the branch's worktree, whichever Run is shown, with no claim. While it runs, every tool button of the Artifact is disabled, and a line under the tools shows the running icon and Cancel, which stops it; a tool that fails to run says so in red on that line. A launch tool returns at once.
  2. 26
    The Artifact's Tool logA section of its own under Tools, shown from the first run on and unfolded by every run, even when it was folded: the run's start line, then each line as it prints; an output tool's end line (■ exit 0, or red for a failure) closes its run once it ends. A launch tool returns at once: its end line is ■ launched, once the file is handed to its application, and the application's lines keep arriving under that same run until it exits. Nothing in it is a review's evidence.
  1. 3
    Chip pathThe title keeps the Artifact's star on every tab: here cli is starred, so the star is filled (the first figure shows it outlined, not starred). Only on an eval's tab: the Artifact's chip, which leads back to its tab, and a /, the two one group that never breaks, only the Artifact's name cut when long; then one chip per eval: rounded chips of 22px, the chosen one filled; eval chips lead with the verdict's icon in its colour; many chips wrap.
  2. 7
    View switchOnly for an eval with a log: plain text a level below the chips, Summary, chosen first, then the log, Agent log (its transcript) for an Agent eval or Runtime log (its saved output) for a runtime eval, as here. The chosen view is bold in the strong text colour; the other is at regular weight in the quieter one; both read plainly on the panel. No chip, no fill. An eval without a log has no switch, its Summary standing alone.
  3. 8
    Status lineThe verdict's icon and word bold in the status colour; beside it in neutral secondary ink what running it took and spent (time, tokens in k or M, and the model for an Agent eval) or what reuse saved, and how it ended, said here once.
  4. 9
    Header → label → valueThree levels: a section's header row, then its facts as a key/value grid, keys right-aligned in secondary ink, values in tabular figures. The Result is unfolded; the sections read now and then are folded to their names, and their values show once unfolded.
  1. 10
    Run DetailThe same dock, titled with the Run's ID; it has no chip path, since a Run has no evals of its own to tab through. It holds what the header row leaves out: the tally with its words, the duration and the comment in full.
  2. 11
    SectionsOutcome and Waits for (what the Run waits for, then its pending evals) are unfolded, their facts as key/value grids; What, Provenance and, last, Technical are folded to their names.
  1. 12
    A Current's chipsEach eval chip leads with what verify would do with that eval, as its icon in the plan's colour: would be reused, would run, or still being checked. Waiting and blocked mark Artifacts' nodes, not evals.
  2. 13
    A Current's SummaryThe plan line replaces the verdict line: what would happen and why, in the plan's colour and secondary ink; then the result it would reuse, when there is one; then Execution options, Execution metadata and Fingerprint, folded, each only when its facts are present, and Debug. An eval whose reused result has a log gets the Summary / log switch as on a Run; tests here would run, so it has none.
  1. 23
    Changed on disk, a fieldUnder the field in red, naming the file: nothing was written, and the Current is scanned again, so the graph and the Definition show what is on disk. The field then holds the file's Path as it is on disk (here the one an agent wrote meanwhile), not the Path that was typed, and Parts lists the file's parts; unlike a Source, a field keeps no draft after a conflict.
  2. 24
    Changed on disk, the SourceUnder the Source in red, naming the file and no line or column, no line marked: it is a conflict, not an error in the text. The typed text stays, with Save and Revert, until Revert shows the file's text as it is on disk.
  1. 14
    DefinitionUnfolded, in place of a Run's Path: the file that defines the Artifact, its Path and its Parts as a key/value grid, then Comment, which opens the comment's field under the node on the graph, Rename and Delete.
  2. 15
    PathA field holding the target, written by Enter in it (J-DEFINITION-EDIT). A target that is missing is said in red beside it. A Path left being changed when the Detail closed shows, when that Artifact's Detail opens again on the same branch, still as it was typed, in this field (J-DEFINITION-DRAFT).
  3. 16
    PartsOne row per part with its remove icon: a click on it removes that part and the place this whole's box kept for it at once, with no second question. Add is separate: it opens a field under Parts, focused, that suggests the project's Artifacts that are not yet parts of this one; a click on a suggestion or Enter adds it (J-PARTS-EDIT). A part left being typed when the Detail closed shows, when that Artifact's Detail opens again on the same branch, in this open field as it was typed, its suggestions under it (J-DEFINITION-DRAFT).
  4. 17
    SourceThe file's text in monospace with its line numbers, folded until unfolded; what is typed is written by Save, never on its own.
  5. 18
    RefusedUnder the Source in red, with the line and column; the line it names is marked and the text stays to be corrected.
  6. 19
    Save and RevertOnly while the Source holds typed text: Save the primary button, Revert beside it.

Rules

D-INS-1
The Detail is the inspector: a panel docked at the Main area's right edge over the graph's canvas, from the canvas's top to the area's bottom, below the area's header row and below the graph's tool strip (both stay readable and usable), with no gap at its top, right or bottom, never beside the Pane. It is a --surface-panel panel meeting the graph at lines, --line-panel at its left and the tool strip's --line-group at its top, with square corners and no shadow; its left edge takes a drag, hovering it showing the resize cursor alone (D-SYS-7), and the drag stops at the limits: never narrower than a readable column of 320px, never so wide that less than 240px of graph shows on its left, and never over the Pane; the width a drag leaves is kept, and the Detail opens at it again, after closing and after the next launch alike. a floating card (a gap, a radius or a shadow around the Detail); the Detail pushing the graph aside or moving its camera.
D-INS-2
The Detail's header is the panel header row (D-MAIN-1): the title at --text-small semibold, wrapping to show a long name whole, naming what the tab shows with the Artifact's name whole in it (Artifact {name} on the Artifact's tab, {Artifact}/{eval} · {eval title} on every eval's tab, a Human eval's included, the Run's ID on a Run's), the star beside it on every tab of an Artifact, its own and each eval's (the star icon in --ink-3 until the Artifact is starred, then the filled star in --ink; a click puts the star on or takes it away, for that Artifact on every branch of its project, in Runs and Currents alike, and for every later monitor of this machine's user), Close at the end. What closes the Detail, and what goes first, is J-DETAIL, J-DEFINITION-DRAFT and J-HUMAN-ESC: whatever else it holds, an Artifact's tool still running or a Source holding typed text, it closes; an open find field (D-OVL-4) takes an outside click first, the Detail staying open; an open comment field (D-CUR-8) is put back by the same click on Close or the graph's background that closes the Detail, the Comment button going with the Detail (J-COMMENT-EDIT). With focus in the Detail, its 2px --accent top line shows. On the Artifact's own tab, the outlined node and this title are the two places that name the opened Artifact (D-INS-6); an eval's tab also names it in its Artifact chip (D-INS-3). a cut title; a second close control; a key cap on Close.
D-INS-3
The chip row shows only on an eval's tab, under the header, closed below by --line-group; the Artifact's own tab has none, since its Evals rows lead to every eval (D-INS-4). It reads as a path: the Artifact's chip (the Artifact mark and its name, only the name cut), which leads back to the Artifact's tab, a /, then one chip per eval. Chips are of --row-dense height, --radius-chip, --text-small, with a 1px --line-group border and --ink-2 text; the chosen chip is filled with --surface-group in --ink semibold; each eval chip leads with its verdict's icon in the status colour (on a Current, with what verify would do, D-INS-8); the / stays with the Artifact's chip and the rest wrap. pills in the accent; a chip without its icon; a chip row above the Artifact's own list of evals.
D-INS-4
The Artifact's tab is sections, top to bottom, each under its header row (D-INS-9): Evals (the eval rows, each of which opens that eval's tab), Neighbours (folded until asked for; unfolded, the drawing, at least 72px, with a camera that does not move: the graph's nodes and edges, the Artifacts its evals use on the left, those whose evals use it on the right), Tools (its Human tools, each a small button with the run icon and its description, as a review's, D-REV-2: a click runs the tool at once in the branch's worktree with no claim; while one runs, every tool button of the Artifact is disabled, and one line under the tools shows the running icon in --running and Cancel, which stops the run; a failure to run takes that line in --red; a launch tool returns once its file is handed to its application), Tool log (only once a tool has run: an Artifact without runs has no Tool log section; it shows each run as a review's Tool log does, D-REV-3, the running one open with no end line yet, a launch tool's run ended by its ■ launched line, under which the application's lines keep arriving until it exits, and every run unfolds it when it was folded), Path (on a Run, read-only facts of a key/value grid in --ink-2, selectable, without a field: Target, what the PATH names as it writes it, in --mono: the path for a folder, a file, an aspect or a graph, and for a Live Artifact the address outside the worktree that its PATH names; and, for a Live Artifact, Address, the address it is observed at in --mono, added below Target; on a Current, Definition and Source in its place, D-INS-10, D-INS-11), then Debug, folded: unfolded as any section is, by a click on its row (D-INS-9), it holds every other fact of the Artifact as a key/value grid, keys right-aligned in --ink-2, values selectable and in --mono for IDs, paths and hashes, what it waits for first (the Artifacts or evals it waits on, when there are any), then the rest its Run or its scan holds of it. For a part of several wholes, on both graphs, Debug holds Drawn in, every whole whose parts name it, in graph order, also when a minimized whole hides a copy so that fewer copies are drawn; an Artifact that is a part of one whole, or of none, has no such fact. An eval row is --row-dense in four columns: the verdict's icon in the status colour, the name, the status words in the status colour, and the last column (time, tokens) pinned to the panel's right edge in --ink-2 tabular; on a Current the words say would run, would be reused or checking. accent-coloured section headings; times trailing the status words instead of aligning at the right; a section, a sentence or an empty box for a Tool log without runs; the Artifact's name or its tally repeated in the tab's body; a tool button on the graph's nodes; a shared part whose tab names only the whole of the copy that was clicked; a field or an edit control in a Run's Path.
D-INS-5
An eval's tab: for an eval with a log, the view switch as plain text under the chips, Summary then the log (Agent log, the transcript, for an Agent eval; Runtime log, the saved output, for a runtime eval), Summary chosen when the tab opens; the chosen view bold in the strong text colour, --ink at weight 700, and the other in the quieter one, --ink-2 at weight 400, both readable on the panel; no radius, no fill; an eval without a log has no switch; then the status line (the verdict's icon and word bold in the status colour; beside it in --ink-2 what running it took and spent, the time, the tokens in k or M and the model when there was one, or, for a reused result, what reuse saved, and how it ended; the line wraps rather than drop any of them), the instruction, then Result, unfolded: each field the eval's owner set in its schema, by its name as a key/value grid (keys right-aligned in --ink-2), a list's value as numbered items; then Execution options, Execution metadata and Fingerprint, each folded to its name (D-INS-9), their facts, once unfolded, in a key/value grid with keys right-aligned in --ink-2 and values in tabular figures; Debug folded last: unfolded as any section is, by a click on its row (D-INS-9), it holds every other fact of the eval as the same key/value grid, values selectable and in --mono: its request (the request ID), its source (the definition file and the eval's name), what it waits for, and the rest of its record; then its raw result whole, as --mono text at --text-small on --surface-input with a --line-input border, wrapping and scrolling in its own box. What the status line says (the duration, the exit) is not said again below it. A log replaces the body below the switch in --mono at --text-small, wrapping and scrolling in its own box. a JSON dump as the Summary; costs in the status colour; the exit code or the duration repeated in the Result or the metadata; a switch with a single view; a log chosen first.
D-INS-7
A click on the Run's headline opens the Run's Detail in the same dock (D-INS-1, D-INS-2), titled with the Run's ID and without a chip row. It holds what the header row leaves out. Outcome, unfolded: the status, its icon and word said once, the evals that failed by name, the tally (each status's icon in its colour, its count and its word), what the Run took, and its comment in full. Waits for, unfolded: what the Run waits for, then its pending evals in --ink-2. Then What, Provenance and, last, Technical, folded to their names. Facts are key/value grids (keys right-aligned in --ink-2, values in tabular figures), and every section folds and unfolds by its header row (D-INS-9). tabs on a Run's Detail; the Run's graph redrawn inside it; a sentence that says the status again (NOT SATISFIED at Run end beside RED).
D-INS-8
Over a Current the Detail keeps its anatomy and changes what the marks say: each eval chip leads its eval's name with what verify would do with that eval, one of three: would run, would be reused, or still being checked, as that plan's icon alone, in its one colour, with no words on the chip; each eval row leads with the same icon and says the same plan in words in that colour, as the Summary's plan line does (would run --running, would be reused --reused, checking --checking, the colours of the nodes' same plans). Waiting and blocked are marks of an Artifact's node, not of its evals: an eval of a waiting or blocked Artifact still says whether it would run or be reused; an eval's tab shows, in place of the verdict line, the plan (what would happen, bold in its colour) with its reason beside it in --ink-2, then the instruction, the result it would reuse as a Result section under its header row when there is one, laid out as a Run's (each owner field by name, a list as numbered items, D-INS-5) (the plan line already says why an eval would run), then Execution options, Execution metadata and Fingerprint, each a section folded to its name when its facts are present, and Debug, folded, last; when the eval has a log (the Agent log or Runtime log of the result it would reuse), the view switch of D-INS-5 stands under the chips as on a Run, Summary first; an eval without one, such as one that would run or is still being checked, has no switch; and a Current has no Human review. A node still being checked recolours its chips and rows in place as the scan learns. a Run's verdict words on a Current; a log switch that has nothing to show; a waiting or blocked mark on an eval's chip, row or Summary.
D-INS-9
The inspector reads in three levels: a section's header row, a fact's label, its value. A section's header row is --row high, opened above by --line-group: the fold icon in --ink-2 (the unfold icon while the section is unfolded), the section's name at --text-small semibold in --ink, in sentence case without letter-spacing, and nothing else: no count, value or list of what it holds stands at the row's end, folded or unfolded; what a section holds shows when it is unfolded. The whole row takes the click that folds or unfolds its section, and keyboard focus as a control (D-SYS-7); folded, a section is its row alone. A section starts unfolded when it is what its tab is opened for (an Artifact's Evals, Tools, Path and Definition, an eval's Result, a Run's Outcome and Waits for) and folded when it is read now and then (Neighbours, Source, Execution options, Execution metadata, Fingerprint, What, Provenance, Debug and a Run's Technical). Once a person folds or unfolds a section, that state is kept by the section's name, not per Artifact: every Detail opened later, of any Artifact, eval or Run, shows that section so, and so does the next launch. A section with nothing to show is not shown, header row included, and no line stands in for it: an Artifact's Evals when it has no eval, its Neighbours when its evals use no Artifact, no Artifact's evals use it and it is drawn once, its Tools when it declares no Human tool, its Tool log before its first run, an eval's Result when it has none (on a Current, no result to reuse), its Execution options, Execution metadata and Fingerprint when it has none, a Run's Waits for when it waits for nothing, and a Debug, What, Provenance or Technical without facts. A Human review's Tools, Tool log and Result, and the ordinary sections of Settings, stand under the same row without the fold icon: they do not fold. Settings' Finish by hand is the exception: a disclosure row with the fold icon that unfolds a sign-in's address and paste field (D-SET-5). 11px uppercase labels as section headings; a header row, or a line saying so, for a section with nothing to show; a summary, a count or a table of contents at a row's end; a box around a section; a fold control apart from the row.
D-INS-6
Selection and inspector agree: the node with the --outline-selected outline, every copy of a shared part included (its tab naming each whole those copies are drawn in, D-INS-4), is the Artifact the Detail is titled with, or, when that Artifact is hidden inside a minimized whole, the minimized whole that hides it while the Detail still names the Artifact; opening a neighbour or another node moves both; closing the Detail removes the outline. The Find field's choice opens the Detail and moves only the camera. a Detail left open for a node no longer highlighted.
D-INS-10
On a Current the Artifact's tab has Definition where a Run's has Path, a section of D-INS-9, its facts a key/value grid: File, the definition file's path in --mono --ink-2, selectable; Path, a --surface-input field in --mono holding the definition's path as written and nothing else, a missing target said beside it in --red; Parts, one --row-dense row per part (its name and the remove icon) and a quiet Add with the add icon, which opens a --surface-input field under Parts, focused, with a list of suggestions as the Search's (D-PANE-1): the project's Artifacts that are not yet parts of this one; the chosen suggestion is added to the file's parts as J-PARTS-EDIT says, a cycle refused with the reason under Parts. Apart from that field, a listed part's remove icon removes that part at once, with its place in this whole's box, without asking again. Under the grid, Comment, Rename and Delete as small quiet buttons: Comment opens the comment field under the Artifact's node on the graph (D-CUR-8); Rename and Delete open their dialogs (D-OVL-7). The comment itself has no section in the Detail: the Source shows it as part of the file. A Path or part is written on Enter, without a Save button; a refusal shows under its field in --red at --text-small, nothing written. An edit the project would not accept says why, the field keeping what was typed to be corrected. Reopened on the same branch after the Detail closed, the Definition shows a Path or a part left being typed as it was left: the Path field holding the typed text, or the Parts field open with it and its suggestions under it (J-DEFINITION-DRAFT). An edit whose file changed on disk since the Current read it names that file, {file} changed on disk; nothing written, without a line or column; the Current is scanned again, and the Path field and Parts then show the file as it is on disk, the edit typed not kept. After an edit that writes, the Current is scanned again and the Detail stays on the same Artifact's tab, under its new name after a Rename; a Delete is the exception: once it succeeds, its dialog and the Detail close and the node goes from the graph (J-NODE-DELETE, D-OVL-7). Delete in red or as the primary button; a Save button for the Path; the Artifact's name repeated as a field; a Definition section on a Run; a Run's facts (an address or a resolved target) turned into the Path field.
D-INS-11
Source follows Definition, folded until unfolded: the file's text in --mono at --text-small on --surface-input with a 1px --line-input border, its line numbers in --ink-3; it scrolls sideways in its own box rather than wrap, so each number stays with its line. While it holds typed text, Save, the primary button, and Revert stand in one row under it, and otherwise neither shows; Save writes the file (J-DEFINITION-EDIT), and nothing writes it on its own; Revert puts back the file's text. A refused Save says why under the text in --red at --text-small in one of two forms: an edit the project would not accept, with its line and column, that line on --selection; or a conflict, the file changed on disk since the Current read it, naming the file, with no line, column or marked line. Either way nothing is written and the typed text stays with Save and Revert; after a conflict the Current is scanned again and the text stays until Revert shows the file as it is on disk. While a Source holds typed text, an edit on the graph that would write its file is refused with the Main area's notice (D-CUR-7). a separate editor window; syntax colours taken from the status palette; Save and Revert shown or disabled while nothing was typed; a refusal that clears the typed text; a conflict said with a line and column, or an error in the text said without them.
