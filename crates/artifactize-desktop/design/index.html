The desktop app, designed as a workbench in three modes

This Artifact plans the desktop app's UI and UX: one page per screen, drawn with the same anatomy the app must have, for the journeys of rules/UX-FEATURES.md (J-…). Each page ends in rules with ids (D-…); Agent evals judge the app's UI against them. The style applies what a professional workbench does well: the screen is divided by the work, the selection drives an inspector, surfaces are few and neutral, boundaries are lines rather than gaps, and colour means something only where it sits. The brand, images and icons of any other product stay theirs; every value here is our own token.

Layout by workflow

The main window, titled Artifactize, has three modes, each a workspace of its own above one bar along the window's bottom. The bar never moves and holds three things, as the reference screens draw it: the brand at its left, which opens the command palette; the modes Chat, Verify and Review in its middle, Review with the count of Human reviews waiting for a person; and Settings at its right, whose header names the reviewer and holds the release notice. The Panes are shown and hidden from the palette. Above it, every mode reads left to right the same way: find (its Pane: Chat's sessions, Verify's search and live branches with their Runs, Review's filter and reviews), look (its main area: one header row, then a conversation, a Run's or a Current's graph under its tool strip, or a review's evaluation space) and, in Verify, inspect (the Detail, a panel docked at the graph's right edge, showing the selected Artifact or eval). Switching a mode swaps only the workspace; each keeps its place. References lead across: a Run reference in Chat opens the Run in Verify and the Run's header leads back to its session; a Human eval in the Detail opens its review in Review and the review's header leads back to the Run and its Artifact. On a branch's Current the graph and the Detail are also where its Artifacts are defined: they are moved, made parts and edited in place, and a Run's graph stays a record.

Outside the main window the app has two kinds of small windows: an Artifact Viewer, which a viewer tool opens to show one Artifact in its type's viewer (the built-in File Explorer, Markdown viewer and Artifactize Viewer, or a project's own plugin), and the pet overlay, which stays above every window while a claimed review is being worked on elsewhere, holding the same draft as Review.

Screens

  • ChatChat mode: the sessions in the Pane with their search and states, a conversation in Markdown with its tool rows, summaries, details and Run references, read where it was left, and the composer with the model selector, its menu and filter open, Stopping and a message not sent.
  • VerifyVerify mode with a Run shown: the bottom bar, the Pane, the Main area's header row with the Run's way back to Chat, the graph's tool strip and the Run graph, with its nodes, its Navigator and a large graph zoomed out.
  • CurrentA Chat session's Graph view: the branch's Current with the scan's state in the session's header row, the tool strip, the Current graph and the composer under it, where Artifacts are dragged into place and into or out of a whole and comments are written and moved.
  • DetailThe inspector, a panel docked at the graph's right edge: an Artifact's tab, an eval's Summary and, on a Current, an Artifact's Definition and Source.
  • ReviewReview mode: the filter and the reviews in the Pane, a review opened from a reference with its way back to Verify, and its evaluation space: with room, the review (instruction, Tools, Tool log, Points) at the left and the Evaluation (the verdict and its form, the actions) at the right; in the Detail, one column.
  • ViewersThe Artifact Viewer windows: a plugin's viewer with Point, the File Explorer, the Markdown viewer and the Artifactize Viewer, and the pet overlay over them.
  • SettingsThe modal over the screen: Providers, Remote and Limits.
  • OverlaysThe command palette, Help, Open by ID, the Leave and Update dialogs, the graph's find field and the New Artifact, Rename and Delete dialogs.

Tokens

Every colour, size and radius in the app comes from tokens.css, named by role. The app has one appearance, dark, whatever the system's; the swatches below render its tokens. A status has one colour wherever it shows (a list, a chip, a graph's node) and is read with a glyph or a word beside it, never alone.

Icons

The app draws its marks itself, so a status reads by its shape as well as by its colour and looks the same on every system. Each icon sits on a 12 by 12 box with a 1.5 stroke, round caps and joins; a shape is solid only where it is a solid (the half of in progress, the dots of queued and waiting, the run triangle, a star that is on). An icon takes the colour of its text: a status's icon is in that status's colour, an action's in the ink of its control. The sheet shows each at its size and enlarged; mock.css holds the drawings.

Rules of the system

These hold on every screen; each screen's page adds its own.

D-SYS-1
Every colour, radius, row height and font size of the UI is a token of tokens.css, used by its role; a component declares no colour literal. a hex value or white in a component; a token used for a role it does not name (a status colour as a border of emphasis, the accent as a status).
D-SYS-2
Surfaces step by role: the bar is darkest, the window and the graph canvas next, a panel above them, a group inside the panel one step lighter; a field or log box is one step darker than its panel; what floats is raised one step and set apart by a panel line and at most a faint shadow (--scrim, 2px offset, 8px blur). gradients; large soft shadows; a card around every item; a status colour as a panel or row background.
D-SYS-3
Boundaries are 1px lines in the strength of what they separate: --line-panel between panels (the bar's top, the Pane/Main divider, the Detail's and a dialog's edge, a review's view and its column, D-REV-1), --line-group between groups inside a panel (section tops, branch rows, the chip row's bottom), --line-input around a field, --line-node around a graph's node and whole. Large panels meet at a line, not at a gap. gaps of 16px or more between panels; boxes drawn around single items.
D-SYS-4
Density: a list row is --row (24px), a dense row --row-dense (22px), a control --control (24px) tall, a graph's tool strip --row-head (28px), and a row a Pane chooses by a click (a Chat session, a Verify branch, a review) --row-pane (36px), chosen by a click anywhere on it, across the Pane's width (J-PANE-ROWS); a panel pads its sides by --pad (12px); spacing uses the 4 / 8 / 12 / 16 scale and never exceeds 16px between groups of one panel. 32px controls; 20px panel paddings; empty space doing a line's job.
D-SYS-5
Type: body 13px, secondary 12px. A section of the inspector, of a review or of Settings stands under a header row: its name at 12px semibold in --ink, in sentence case (D-INS-9). A group label inside a list (a provider in the model menu, a palette group, a Help group, a table's head) is 11px uppercase and letter-spaced in --ink-2. Neither is ever the accent. Numbers that align or change (ages, times, counts, tokens, IDs) use tabular figures. Names may be semibold; nothing else is bold but a status word, a chosen choice and a title. accent-coloured headings; uppercase letter-spaced labels as section headings; proportional figures in an age column.
D-SYS-6
Colour carries meaning only on the element that has it: a status dot, icon or word, a node's icon and strip, an edge that leaves a RED, ERROR or blocked Artifact, a verdict button, stderr lines. A graph's own lines, a node's outline and every other edge, are neutral. Two places of a graph show a status by colour alone, as the journeys ask: a large graph zoomed out until names do not read, where each Artifact is a block filled with its status's colour and a whole is its box's outline in its colour (D-GRAPH-2), and the Navigator's blocks (D-GRAPH-5); they are exempt from the neutral outline and from the icon or word, and a click on a block of the zoomed-out graph's canvas opens its Detail, which names the status, while a click or a drag in the Navigator only moves the camera there, its zoom kept, and opens nothing (J-GRAPH-NAVIGATOR). A meaning has one colour: a status takes the same token in the Pane, a chip, a row and a graph's node, and a graph has no palette of its own (in progress is --running on a node as in a list, waiting --queued, blocked --blocked, ERROR --error). The accent marks only what is interactive or emphasised in a document (links in rendered Markdown, the outline of a drop target, D-CUR-5), a light neutral; the focus ring (D-SYS-7) a light neutral --focus; the pointing outline (D-VIEW-2) a dashed --handle line over its --handle-edge; and the one current or edited item a thin --current-mark line or mark, never a fill or text (D-SYS-13). reading every red as an error or every green as success; tinting a node's face, outline, a row or a panel by status; a second colour for a status on a graph; a star or a selection in a status colour.
D-SYS-7
States are told apart without colour: every status shows its icon or word with its colour, each status's icon a silhouette of its own, a graph's node included, except the colour blocks and whole outlines of a zoomed-out graph and of the Navigator (D-SYS-6); the selected (opened) row is the darker --selection row with its text in --ink, a row not selected names in --ink-2, a hovered row (never the selected one) --surface-group, and the row the keyboard is on the 2px --focus ring inside it; keyboard focus is a 2px --focus ring on every control; hover only highlights and opens nothing: a row or a borderless control takes --surface-group, and a button, whose face already is that surface, keeps its face while its edge steps up to --line-input (the primary button's stays --line-primary); the two resize edges, the Pane/Main divider and the Detail's left edge, take no highlight at all on hover: they show the resize cursor alone, their line unchanged; the exceptions the journeys ask for are the icon-only controls, the whole's Minimize/Expand button and the bar's Settings, whose native title names what they do, and no other control has a tooltip. a selection shown by colour alone; a focus ring removed; a popup, summary or menu on hover; a divider or a Detail edge that thickens, recolours or highlights under the pointer.
D-SYS-8
Marks are the app's own icon set (the sheet above), never characters of whatever font the system falls back to: each is drawn on a --icon box with a 1.5 stroke, round caps and joins, solid only where the shape is a solid, and takes the colour of the text around it. The same meaning has the same icon everywhere: each status its own (a node, a chip, a row, the Run's headline and tally, the find field); the run icon starts a tool, the star and the filled star mark a favorite, the fold and unfold icons fold a branch, a section and Debug, the remove icon removes, the add icon adds, minus and add minimize and expand a whole and zoom, the fit icon fits the view, the reset icon resets a layout; the Artifact mark precedes an Artifact's name. Where the journeys write a mark as a character (▶, ☆/★, ✕, + Add, a verdict's glyph), the app draws that mark's icon, read aloud as what it means. A Tool log's lines are text and keep their characters (▶ HH:MM:SS, ■ exit 0); the two rare marks without an icon (a budget's $, an absent eval's -) stay characters in --mono. An action's icon stands with its word, or carries an accessible name; a key cap shows only where D-SYS-12 allows one. two icons for one meaning; a font's dingbat where an icon exists; an icon in a colour its meaning does not have; an icon without a label or an accessible name.
D-SYS-9
Long and Korean labels keep the layout: a name wraps or is cut with an ellipsis inside its own box; nothing a panel shows widens the panel; logs and transcripts wrap within their width. a pane sized by its content; a title that pushes its actions out of the header.
D-SYS-10
The core task works with the side panels out of the way: a graph can be read and an Artifact opened with the Pane hidden from the palette, or at its minimum width, and the Detail closed; a Chat can be read and written to with its Pane hidden; a Human review can be claimed, its tools run and its result submitted inside the Detail alone, and its draft written and submitted from the pet overlay alone. a control that exists only in the Pane for a task of the main area.
D-SYS-11
A working screen shows what the task at hand needs and says it once. A fact has one home on a screen (an Artifact's name: its node and the Detail's title; a review's title: the header above it; a Run's tally, duration and comment: the Run's Detail), and other places point to it with an icon or a count rather than repeat it without need. Where the journeys give a fact two places for two tasks, both show it: the Run's headline says its verdict and what needs attention for the glance, and the Run's Detail, opened from that headline, says its outcome with the evals that failed in full. The app's own words are names, values and numbers. What goes well, runs or is the default is not put in a sentence: progress is an icon with a count, an end is a time, a success is the result itself. Five things get a sentence: what went wrong, why a control is disabled, a place that would otherwise be empty (D-MAIN-3), a decision that cannot be undone, and what a setting is when its name cannot say it (the sign-off idle grace, D-SET-8). What is read now and then starts folded to a row that is its name alone (D-INS-9), and what does not exist (no claimant, no run yet, a single group) is not said. What an author wrote (an instruction, a tool's description, a log, a result) is content and is shown whole where it is read, with two places the journeys cut it short: a Run's comment is one line in a branch's timeline (whole in the Run's Detail), and an Artifact's comment on a graph is its first three lines and then an ellipsis (whole in the field that edits it on a Current). a sentence that describes a normal state; the same fact twice in view for one task; a line that explains a control or a dialog; a legend, a hint or a count standing beside the thing it describes.
D-SYS-13
States follow one emphasis grammar, a video editor's (measured on DaVinci Resolve): a state is told by luminance, position and a small mark, never by weight and never by changing a work object's own colour. Chosen or active is brighter text (--ink, the bar's white), normal is --ink-2, unavailable is --ink-3. Each kind of selection has one mark in one place: a list's selected row is the darker --selection row with white text, hover never on it; a tab or a chosen effort is its brighter label over a 2px --current-mark line; a graph's selected node or block is outlined in --current-mark; the field being typed in has a 1px --current-mark outline, the keyboard's --focus ring staying separate; a radio's chosen option a small --current-mark dot on a fixed dark face; a toggle such as Point or the view switch is --ink-bright while on and --ink-off (the view switch) or --ink-2 (Point) while off, on the same surface, the view switch's two sides inside one 1px --line-input edge with a divider between them. Selection always wins over hover: a hovered selected row stays the darker row, a hovered selected node keeps its --current-mark outline; the keyboard's ring is drawn apart from both. A primary action has the secondary button's face and a 1px --line-primary outline. A verdict button chosen is white text with a bright outline, its meaning a small status dot of the same size on both. Information hierarchy (a title, a label in --label, a value in --ink) is apart from state; a header's view name is --ink whatever has focus, and references below it are --ink-2, told by their icon and border. bold or semibold as a state; a selected row lighter than the rest or marked by a side bar; an accent fill on a primary button; a filled tab or toggle; --current-mark as a fill or as text; a status colour on a whole verdict button.
D-SYS-12
Keyboard shortcuts are optional: every action has a visible way (a button, a menu, the palette, a click), and the screens are drawn for that way. Where the app offers keys, they are named where keys are looked up, not where the work is: in the palette, beside each command, and in Help (D-OVL-2, D-OVL-5). A key cap shows in one place of the app outside the palette and Help: as hints, on the Leave dialog's two buttons (D-OVL-1); no other control shows one, the bottom bar included. a key cap on a button of a tool strip, a panel's header, a form or any other dialog; a line that teaches a key; a key that only an inline cap names.

Before handing over a screen

  • The selected node and the open Detail name the same Artifact.
  • Every state is told apart without colour (icon, word, weight, outline).
  • Every mark is an icon of the sheet, not a font's character.
  • A status has the same colour everywhere it shows.
  • A long Korean label and large text keep the layout.
  • Keyboard focus is visible on every control.
  • The core task works with the Pane hidden and the Detail closed.
  • The bottom bar stands in the same place in every mode, and switching a mode changes only the workspace above it.
  • Every reference shows its exact target, and the target shows its way back.
  • Nothing in view is said twice, and no sentence describes a normal state.
  • Every action has a visible way; a key cap shows outside the palette and Help only on the Leave dialog's two buttons.
  • Contrast and click targets are checked in our own environment.
  • No value is copied from another product's screenshot; every value is a role token.
